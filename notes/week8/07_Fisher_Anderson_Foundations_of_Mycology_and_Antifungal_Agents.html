Handout enrichment: the 10A guide explicitly explains why these structural differences matter pharmacologically: fungi resemble human cells more than bacteria do, so the most useful selective targets are the cell wall and ergosterol-containing membrane.

Fisher mycology slide 11. The life-cycle diagram separates favorable-condition asexual reproduction (anamorph) from unfavorable-condition sexual reproduction (teleomorph).

Fisher mycology slide 15. Asexual yeast reproduction examples: blastoconidia, fission, and ballistoconidia.
Spore distinction
Asexual spores arise through mitotic reproduction. Sexual spores follow nuclear fusion and meiosis.
10A enrichment: conidia are external asexual spores, sporangiospores are produced inside a sporangium, arthrospores arise from hyphal fragmentation, and blastospores/blastoconidia arise by budding. These labels help decode the classification slides, but not every term is written out in the main deck.

Polyenes: Amphotericin B & Nystatin

Anderson antifungal slide 5. Amphotericin mechanism diagram: ergosterol binding creates a hydrophilic membrane pore with ion leakage.
Anderson antifungal slide 6. Amphotericin toxicity slide: nephrotoxicity, electrolyte wasting, anemia, infusion reactions, and hepatotoxicity are highlighted.
Amphotericin B
Bind ergosterol → membrane pores. Major exam toxicity = kidney, with K/Mg/Ca wasting highlighted by the deck.
  • Additional deck toxicities: anemia; chills/fever/headache infusion reactions; hepatotoxicity.
  • Deck spectrum: Candida, Aspergillus, Mucor, dimorphic fungi, Cryptococcus, other molds.
  • Lipid formulations are shown as improving CNS penetration; the summary handout also notes reduced nephrotoxicity.
10B enrichment: nystatin has the same polyene mechanism but is used locally/topically rather than for systemic disease. The handout also uses the classic “Ampho-terrible” mnemonic; useful, but not faculty-slide wording.

Azoles: Block 14α-Demethylase

Anderson antifungal slide 10. Ergosterol synthesis pathway: azoles inhibit the cytochrome-P450-dependent 14α-demethylase step between lanosterol and ergosterol.
Anderson antifungal slide 11. Class-wide adverse effects: hepatotoxicity, CYP450 inhibition, GI effects, and QTc prolongation, with an asterisk because isavuconazole is the exception.
Azole rule
14α-demethylase inhibition → ↓ ergosterol. Class concerns = liver + CYP interactions + GI + QTc; isavuconazole shortens QTc.
Anderson antifungal slide 13. Voriconazole: Aspergillus/Candida plus other molds; visual color changes, hallucinations/nightmares, photosensitivity, higher hepatotoxicity, strong CYP inhibition, and therapeutic drug monitoring.
Anderson antifungal slide 15. Itraconazole: dimorphic fungi/aspergillosis/onychomycosis; formulation-dependent absorption and therapeutic drug monitoring.
Anderson antifungal slide 16. Isavuconazole: Aspergillus and mucormycosis emphasis; shortens QTc; reversible LFT elevations after stopping therapy.

Other Mechanisms: Terbinafine, Echinocandins, Flucytosine, Newer Agents

Anderson antifungal slide 18. Terbinafine inhibits squalene epoxidase early in ergosterol synthesis; the deck primarily connects it to dermatologic/tinea indications.
Anderson antifungal slide 20. Echinocandin mechanism slide: inhibition of β-1,3-D-glucan synthesis in the fungal cell wall.
Echinocandins
Micafungin • caspofungin • anidulafungin • rezafungin → inhibit Fks/β-1,3-D-glucan synthase. Deck coverage is strongest for Candida; Aspergillus is marked with an asterisk; Mucor/Cryptococcus/Blastomycosis are poor/no coverage.
Anderson antifungal slide 23. Flucytosine is transported into fungal cells and converted to 5-FU, disrupting both RNA/protein synthesis and DNA synthesis.
Anderson antifungal slide 24. Flucytosine summary: Candida/Cryptococcus; bone-marrow suppression and hepatitis; oral only; used in combination.

Lower-priority / clinical-rotation material: the Anderson summary table explicitly says an asterisk marks information intended for clinical rotations. Ibrexafungerp, olorofim, fosmanogepix, and rezafungin appear in that newer-agent/informational layer. Know the mechanism map, but prioritize the classic classes and toxicities for the official objectives.
10B enrichment: flucytosine + amphotericin B for severe cryptococcal meningitis is added by the handout. The current deck itself says flucytosine is oral, used in combination, and active against Candida/Cryptococcus, without making that combination the central slide wording.
