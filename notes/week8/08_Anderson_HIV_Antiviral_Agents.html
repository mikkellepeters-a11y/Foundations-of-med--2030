Lecture Objectives

1. Explain the goals of antiretroviral therapy (ART).
2. Describe the mechanisms of action of antiretrovirals.
3. Recognize the most common adverse effects of ART.
4. Apply PrEP, PEP, treatment as prevention, rapid initiation, and Test & Treat to patient care.
How to study this lecture
Do not memorize an alphabet soup of drugs in isolation. Start with the HIV life-cycle step, attach the drug class to that step, then learn the few toxicity / interaction clues the deck actually highlights.

Slides 5-8: What ART Is Trying to Accomplish

Slide 5. The continuum-of-care slide frames HIV treatment as more than prescribing a drug: diagnosis, linkage to care, retention, and viral suppression all matter.
Slide 6. The deck organizes ART goals into virologic, immunologic, clinical, and societal/financial outcomes.
ART goals
Virologic: undetectable viral load; no replication → less mutation; U = U.
Immunologic: CD4 recovery and fewer opportunistic infections.
Clinical: prolong life expectancy.
Societal: reduce HIV transmission and costs.
Deck timing: virologic suppression generally occurs within the first 3-6 months of ART. Predictors include lower baseline viremia, potent therapy, tolerability/convenience, and adherence.
Slide 8. Starting ART is individualized around ART history, comorbid conditions, baseline viral load, drug-drug interactions, and HIV resistance.

Slide 10. The pharmacology table makes TDF vs TAF and abacavir's HLA-B*5701 hypersensitivity the major practical distinctions.
Abacavir must-not-miss
HLA-B*5701 positive → do not use abacavir. If hypersensitivity/rash occurs, the deck says never rechallenge.
Handout-only mechanism detail: NRTIs are phosphorylated by host kinases and act as faulty nucleoside/nucleotide building blocks that terminate the growing viral DNA chain because they lack the required 3'-OH. The handout also supplies additional specific pearls such as zidovudine macrocytic anemia/bone-marrow suppression.

Slides 11-12: NNRTIs - Watch the Rash and Interactions

Slide 11. The deck compares doravirine, rilpivirine, efavirenz, and nevirapine with their food, adverse-effect, CYP, and drug-interaction patterns.

Slide 12. NNRTI class warnings: skin rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, hepatitis, and a relatively low barrier to resistance.
Rilpivirine vignette: if a patient starts a strong acid-suppressing regimen and later loses virologic control, think impaired rilpivirine absorption, not automatically resistance.
Handout-only mechanism detail: NNRTIs bind an allosteric site on reverse transcriptase rather than mimicking nucleosides.

Slides 13-14: INSTIs - Modern Workhorse Class

Slide 13. INSTIs prevent insertion of HIV DNA into the host genome. The deck calls them generally the best-tolerated, most efficacious class with a high barrier to resistance.
Slide 14. INSTI pharmacology: bictegravir, dolutegravir, elvitegravir, raltegravir, and cabotegravir. Polyvalent-cation chelation is a class interaction to remember.
DrugDeck pearl
BictegravirNausea/diarrhea, headache, CPK increase; decreased tubular creatinine secretion without changing glomerular function
DolutegravirCPK/myositis, headache, hepatotoxicity
ElvitegravirTake with food; requires a booster
RaltegravirHeadache/nausea; no CYP interaction highlighted
CabotegravirLong-acting injectable; injection-site pain/headache/nausea
INSTI class
High barrier + well tolerated. Remember polyvalent cations → chelation and the deck's class effects of weight gain, insomnia, and depression.

Slide 15: Protease Inhibitors - Maturation + CYP

Slide 15. PIs block cleavage of HIV polyprotein products, preventing maturation of new virions. The deck emphasizes CYP3A4 metabolism, pharmacologic boosting, and metabolic adverse effects.
PI core
Protease inhibitor → immature/nonfunctional virions.
Ritonavir or cobicistat = pharmacologic boosters.
Class effects: hyperglycemia + GI intolerance + lipodystrophy.
Handout-only board pearls: the companion adds atazanavir → indirect hyperbilirubinemia and darunavir → sulfa-moiety caution. Those are useful board associations but are not the main content on slide 15.

Slides 16-19: Entry and Capsid Inhibitors

Slide 16. Maraviroc blocks CCR5-dependent entry. It requires a viral tropism test and only works for CCR5-tropic virus.
Maraviroc
CCR5 antagonist → requires tropism testing. If the virus uses CXCR4, maraviroc will not solve the entry problem.
Slide 17. Ibalizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds the D2 region of CD4 and prevents post-attachment conformational changes.
Slide 18. Fostemsavir is presented as a gp120 attachment inhibitor; the slide emphasizes nausea, LFT elevations, sleep disturbance, and QTc prolongation.
Slide 19. Lenacapavir is a capsid inhibitor targeting nuclear transport, viral assembly, and capsid assembly; the deck notes treatment or prevention and oral or IM administration.
Handout-only entry-agent addition: enfuvirtide binds gp41 and blocks membrane fusion; it is given subcutaneously and is associated with injection-site reactions.

Slide 21. Regimen choice is driven by adherence, comorbidities/drug interactions, pregnancy/breastfeeding considerations, and baseline viral information.

Slides 23-25: Start Early, but Know the Exceptions

Slide 23. The lecture shows the historical progression from CD4-based thresholds to starting ART as soon as possible regardless of CD4 count: rapid entry, Test & Treat, and treatment as prevention.
When to start
Now: as soon as possible regardless of CD4 count.
Keywords: rapid entry • Test & Treat • treatment as prevention.
Slide 24. The deck's rapid-start exceptions are cryptococcal meningitis and tuberculosis meningitis.
Rapid-start exceptions on this deck
Cryptococcal meningitis and tuberculosis meningitis.
Slide 25. ART initiation can trigger immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS), divided here into unmasking and paradoxical IRIS.

Unmasking IRIS

Previously unrecognized infection becomes clinically apparent as immune function returns.

Paradoxical IRIS

A known/treated infection clinically worsens after immune recovery.

Slide 27. The PrEP efficacy figure visually reinforces that adherence strongly influences prevention efficacy.
Handout-only practical PEP detail: the companion specifies a 28-day, 3-drug ART regimen started as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after a significant exposure.

Slide 28: Resistance Patterns Worth Knowing

Slide 28. The deck gives three high-yield resistance rules rather than a giant mutation catalog.

Resistance logic
Adherence matters because ongoing replication creates opportunities for mutation. Suppression is not just a viral-load goal; it is also a resistance-prevention strategy.
