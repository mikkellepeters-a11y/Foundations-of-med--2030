Slide 9. The evaluation plan starts with the infection pattern and complete examination, then asks you to identify a phenotype, order screening labs, narrow the differential, and involve immunology/genetics appropriately.

Slides 10-18: First-Line Screening - What You Should Be Able to Order

Slide 11. The faculty's first-line list: CBC with differential, immunoglobulin levels (GAME), possibly delayed-hypersensitivity skin testing, HIV-1 testing, C3/C4/CH50/AH50, and review of the newborn TREC result.

1. CBC with differential

Slide 12. CBC interpretation is not just 'normal/abnormal': calculate absolute lymphocyte and neutrophil counts and examine platelet number and size.
  • Low absolute lymphocyte count: think SCID / T-cell lymphopenia.
  • Neutropenia: consider neutrophil-number disorders.
  • Microthrombocytopenia: points toward Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome.
  • Manual smear review can reveal clues such as Chédiak-Higashi morphology.

2. Quantitative immunoglobulins - GAME

Slide 13. The deck uses the quantitative Ig pattern itself to suggest phenotype: pan-low, isolated/multiclass low, hyper-IgM, high IgE, or hypergammaglobulinemia.

3. TREC newborn screening

Slide 16. TRECs arise from DNA excision during V(D)J recombination and are counted by PCR. The screen detects congenital lymphopenia; SCID is one cause, not the only cause.
TREC trap
An abnormal TREC screen does not equal SCID. The deck lists SCID/leaky SCID, DiGeorge, ataxia-telangiectasia, other congenital syndromes, and secondary causes such as cardiac disease, GI loss, leukemia, and third spacing.

4. Complement screening

Slide 18. CH50 screens the classical pathway; AH50 screens the alternative pathway. C3 and C4 help distinguish deficiency, consumption, and possible specimen/lab issues.
Complement first-pass
CH50 = classical • AH50 = alternative • add C3/C4.
For angioedema evaluation, the deck also names C1 inhibitor quantity/function and C1q for acquired angioedema.

5. HIV-1 testing / older DHST

  • The lecture explicitly says HIV testing can be indicated even without traditional “risk factors.”
  • Delayed-hypersensitivity skin testing is a traditional in-vivo T-cell-function screen that is fading from routine use.

Slides 19-24: Second-Line Tests

Slide 19. Second-line testing is usually ordered by immunology: antibody functional studies, lymphocyte flow cytometry, mitogen/antigen stimulation, and neutrophil studies.

Antibody function

  • IgM: isohemagglutinin titers.
  • IgG: titers to childhood immunizations or common infections.
  • IgE: allergy testing.

Flow cytometry

Slide 21. Flow is not part of the basic screening evaluation. Clinically useful outputs include T-, B-, and NK-cell counts for SCID phenotyping; the deck also names CD40L detection and DHR-related neutrophil work.
Screening vs confirmation: do not jump straight to giant esoteric flow panels. The deck explicitly warns against overusing esoteric markers.

Mitogen / antigen stimulation

Lymphocytes are cultured in vitro, exposed to nonspecific mitogens or specific antigens, and assessed for proliferation.

DHR / NBT for neutrophil oxidative burst

Slide 23. DHR flow-cytometry example: normal neutrophils shift after stimulation; CGD shows essentially no oxidative-burst response. An X-linked female carrier can show two populations.
CGD test
DHR is the modern high-yield oxidative-burst test. NBT is older but still appears in books/question banks.

Slides 25-28: Third-Line / Genotyping

Slide 27. The deck emphasizes that molecular testing changes a phenotypic label into a definitive diagnosis and is essential for genetic counseling.
“CVID” faculty nuance
“CVID” is a phenotype / working preliminary diagnosis, not necessarily the final molecular diagnosis.
  • Strong suspicion: individual-gene test or targeted panel.
  • Less clear / broad phenotype: whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing may be appropriate.
  • Genetic counseling matters because disorders such as CGD and hyper-IgM have both X-linked and autosomal forms.
Companion simplification: the Word guide frames genotyping as the definitive diagnostic test for most IEIs. The current deck is more nuanced: use phenotype and targeted testing first, then individual genes/panels/exome/genome depending on suspicion and specialist input.

Slides 29-33 and 37-46: Think in Phenotypes, Not a 500-Disease List

Slide 32. The lecture's organizing framework: combined/SCID, T-cell dysfunction, hyper-IgM, antibody deficiency, complement deficiency, phagocyte deficiency, and the normal child with too many infections.
Faculty explicitly says
Don't memorize >500 IEIs. Identify the infection phenotype, connect it to a host-defense mechanism, and then test that mechanism.
Slide 33. Combined IEI phenotype: broad susceptibility across viral, bacterial, fungal, and protozoal infections - the slide's memorable phrase is the 'walking agar plate.'

Combined / SCID

All pathogen classes; low age-adjusted lymphocyte count; SCID immunophenotype; ataxia-telangiectasia can overlap.

T-cell dysfunction

Recurrent viral, fungal, protozoal infections; DiGeorge is the clinical example; WAS, CMC/APS-1 and selected TLR-pathway defects appear in the list.

Hyper-IgM

Antibody-deficiency-like infections plus some fungi; normal/high IgM with other classes near zero; CD40L deficiency is faculty high-yield.

Antibody deficiency

Mostly bacterial infections; XLA, selective IgA deficiency, “CVID,” transient hypogammaglobulinemia of infancy.

Complement deficiency

Terminal pathway → recurrent Neisseria; other patterns include bacterial disease, lupus-like immune-complex disease, PNH, angioedema.

Phagocyte deficiency

Deep boils/abscesses, pneumonia, osteomyelitis; Aspergillus, Nocardia, Serratia, Staphylococcus suggest neutrophil dysfunction.

Slide 37. T-cell-dysfunction phenotype: viral/fungal/protozoal susceptibility is the pattern. The deck separates true T-cell defects from mimic mechanisms such as UNC93B/TLR3.
Slide 38. Hyper-IgM phenotype: IgM normal/elevated while other immunoglobulin classes are very low. CD40L deficiency is highlighted as the high-yield specific cause.
Slide 39. Antibody-deficiency phenotype: predominantly bacterial disease, but the deck warns that phagocyte/complement defects can mimic it because antibody is an important opsonin.
