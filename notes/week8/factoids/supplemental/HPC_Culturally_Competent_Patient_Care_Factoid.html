Culturally Competent Patient Care - Factoids

Week 8 HPC • Working with Interpreters • current Spearman-McCarthy deck controls
Core encounter rule: use a trained interpreter, but maintain the clinician-patient relationship. Speak directly to the patient in first person.

LEP + Language Access

LEP
Primary language is not English + limited ability to speak, read, write, or understand English.
Do not assume
Conversational English ≠ safe comprehension of complex medical information.

Translation vs Interpretation

TranslationInterpretation
Written contentSpoken content
More exact; may take timeImmediate; prioritizes understanding/communication in real time

Why Professional Interpreters?

  • More accurate/timely diagnosis
  • Better access and satisfaction
  • Fewer errors and adverse events
  • More cost-effective diagnosis/treatment
  • Improved medication/treatment adherence
Legal / systems anchor: current lecture links interpreters to safety, liability, Title VI, CLAS standards, and accreditation expectations. The companion explicitly identifies Title VI as the principal federal LEP language-access anchor.

Before the Visit

ActionReason
Use trained/certified medical interpreterAccuracy + clinical safety
Allow extra timeMost communication occurs twice
Minimize distractionsProtect attention/accuracy
Brief interpreter on goals/proceduresPrepares vocabulary and flow
Document interpreter name/certificationMedical-record accountability

During the Visit

Face the patient • first person • normal voice • short/simple language • sentence-by-sentence • watch nonverbal cues.
  • Do not address introductions/questions to the interpreter.
  • Avoid jargon and idioms.
  • Do not shout.
  • Allow clarifying questions when needed.
  • Observe before interrupting a long interpreter-patient exchange; intervene if it moves outside the interpreter role.

INTERPRET + Wrap-Up

Wrap-Up

  • Teach-back: patient explains directions/recommendations back in their own words.
  • Leave time for questions and clarification.
  • Assume side comments may be understood even if patient has LEP.
  • If language assistance is declined, document that it was offered and the patient's preference.
