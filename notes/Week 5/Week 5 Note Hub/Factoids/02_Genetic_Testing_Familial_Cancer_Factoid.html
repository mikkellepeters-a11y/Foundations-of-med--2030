Genetic Testing for Familial Cancer Syndromes

Master framework: Recognize hereditary-cancer red flags, separate germline from somatic findings, and know what a pathogenic variant versus VUS permits clinically.

Hereditary red flags

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Early ageCancer substantially younger than expected.
Multiple/bilateral primariesMore than one primary tumor or bilateral disease.
Family clusteringRelated cancers across generations or multiple close relatives.

Anchor syndromes

ConceptHigh-yield fact
LynchMMR genes MLH1/MSH2/MSH6/PMS2; MSI; colorectal + endometrial anchors.
HBOCBRCA1/2 homologous-recombination repair defect; breast, ovarian, pancreatic, prostate.
FAPAPC loss → β-catenin signaling → diffuse adenomatous polyposis; osteomas/desmoids/CHRPE.

Testing logic

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Germline variantInherited; present broadly in body cells; has implications for relatives.
Somatic variantAcquired in tumor; may guide treatment but does not prove inherited risk.
PathogenicDisease-causing and clinically actionable in the appropriate context.
VUSUncertain; generally not used alone for prophylactic surgery or predictive family testing.
GeneReviewsSyndrome overview, inheritance, manifestations, management.
NCCNCurrent testing, surveillance, and risk-reduction guidance.

Exam traps

  • Tumor sequencing can suggest hereditary disease but is not equivalent to germline testing.
  • A VUS is not a positive hereditary-cancer result.
  • Autosomal-dominant tumor-suppressor syndromes can still require two cellular hits.
Last 30 seconds: Early/multiple/familial cancer → think germline • Lynch = MMR/MSI • HBOC = BRCA/HR • FAP = APC • VUS is not actionable.
