Foundations of Cancer Screening & Diagnosis

Master framework: Separate test performance from screening bias, and separate screening from tissue diagnosis and staging.

Test characteristics

ConceptHigh-yield fact
SensitivityP(test + | disease); high sensitivity → few false negatives.
SpecificityP(test − | no disease); high specificity → few false positives.
PPVP(disease | positive); increases as prevalence increases.
NPVP(no disease | negative); generally decreases as prevalence increases.

Bias

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Lead-time biasEarlier diagnosis makes survival-from-diagnosis look longer without changing time of death.
Length-time biasScreening preferentially detects slower-growing tumors with longer detectable phases.

Clinical workflow

ConceptHigh-yield fact
ScreenAsymptomatic population.
DiagnoseEstablish what the lesion is; pathology is the anchor.
IHCRefines lineage/marker expression.
NGSIdentifies molecular alterations.
Liquid biopsyCirculating tumor-derived information; not a universal replacement for tissue.
StageDetermine anatomic extent after diagnosis.

Exam traps

  • High sensitivity alone does not make a test a good population screening program.
  • Screening recommendations depend on risk, benefit, and harms—not only test accuracy.
  • Lead-time bias changes measured survival, not necessarily mortality.
Last 30 seconds: Sensitivity=few FN • Specificity=few FP • PPV ↑ with prevalence • Lead-time ≠ mortality benefit • Screen → diagnose → stage.
