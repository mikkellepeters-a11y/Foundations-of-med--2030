Palliative Care in Cancer Care

Master framework: Palliative care is needs-based serious-illness care that can coexist with disease-directed treatment; hospice is a narrower end-of-life model.

Palliative vs hospice

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Palliative careSymptom relief, quality of life, goals, communication throughout serious illness; can occur with chemotherapy/radiation/surgery.
HospiceEnd-of-life care when comfort is dominant and burdens of life-prolonging treatment outweigh likely benefit.
Referral triggersHigh symptom burden, progressive illness, functional decline, recurrent crises, difficult decisions.

Symptoms

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Somatic painLocalized/aching.
Visceral painPoorly localized; may be referred.
Neuropathic painBurning, electric, shooting.
Persistent opioid-treated painScheduled baseline therapy + short-acting breakthrough rescue; prophylactic bowel regimen.
DyspneaPositioning/fan/reassurance, treat reversible cause, titrated opioids can reduce air hunger.
DeliriumQuiet familiar setting, evaluate reversible causes consistent with goals, relieve distress.

Opioid language

ConceptHigh-yield fact
ToleranceMore drug needed for same effect.
Physical dependenceWithdrawal if abruptly stopped.
AddictionCompulsive use despite harm; distinct from expected dependence.

Exam traps

  • Palliative care does not mean anticancer treatment has stopped.
  • Opioid constipation generally does not develop meaningful tolerance—prevent it.
  • Physical dependence is not addiction.
Last 30 seconds: Palliative can coexist with treatment • Hospice = narrower EOL model • Scheduled + breakthrough opioid • Bowel regimen • opioids can relieve air hunger.
