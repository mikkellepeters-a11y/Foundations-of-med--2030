Foundations of Medicine · Week 5 Factoid Sheet

Molecular Cytogenetics

Master framework: Match chromosome-scale abnormalities to the correct tool and distinguish nondisjunction, Robertsonian translocation, and balanced versus unbalanced rearrangements.

Karyotype vs FISH

ConceptHigh-yield fact
KaryotypeGenome-wide chromosome survey; large numerical/structural changes; requires dividing cells; lower resolution.
FISHTargeted probe; faster; can detect specific microdeletions/rearrangements; only detects the probed target.

Nondisjunction

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Meiosis IHomologous chromosomes fail to separate.
Meiosis IISister chromatids fail to separate.
Trisomy 21Most often nondisjunction; Robertsonian translocation is an important alternative mechanism.

Structural abnormalities

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Robertsonian translocationFusion of long arms of acrocentric chromosomes.
Balanced rearrangementNo major net gain/loss of genetic material; carrier may be phenotypically normal.
Unbalanced rearrangementDosage gain/loss; often causes phenotype.
DiGeorge22q11.2 deletion.
Prader-Willi / Angelman15q11-q13 imprinting region.
Cri-du-chat5p deletion.

Exam traps

  • A normal targeted FISH result does not exclude abnormalities elsewhere in the genome.
  • Balanced carriers can produce unbalanced gametes/offspring.
  • Know basic notation such as 47,XY,+21.
Last 30 seconds: Karyotype = broad/large • FISH = targeted/faster • MI = homologs • MII = sisters • Robertsonian = acrocentric long-arm fusion.
