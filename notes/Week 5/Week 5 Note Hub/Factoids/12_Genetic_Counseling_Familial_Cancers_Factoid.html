Genetic Counseling for Familial Cancers

Master framework: Genetic counseling is a process: pedigree + risk assessment + informed discussion + testing + interpretation + family implications.

Who to refer

ConceptHigh-yield fact
CluesEarly age, rare tumor, multiple/bilateral primaries, strong family clustering, suggestive tumor testing, known familial variant, high-risk ancestry.

Testing strategy

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Affected relative firstUsually the most informative person to test.
Pathogenic germline variantAllows targeted cascade testing in relatives.
VUSNot a positive result; do not use alone for prophylactic surgery or predictive testing of relatives.
Cascade testingTargeted testing for the known familial pathogenic variant.

Pretest & resources

ConceptHigh-yield fact
Pretest discussionGoals, anxiety, privacy, family impact, insurance/discrimination concerns, incomplete pedigree.
NCCNTesting/management algorithms.
GeneReviewsSyndrome overview and inheritance.
ClinVarPublic archive of variant interpretations.

Exam traps

  • Ordering a panel is not the same as genetic counseling.
  • Testing an unaffected relative first can be less informative when an affected relative is available.
  • A negative result is interpreted in the context of whether a familial pathogenic variant is known.
Last 30 seconds: Counsel first • Test affected relative first • Pathogenic → cascade testing • VUS ≠ positive • NCCN algorithms • GeneReviews syndrome • ClinVar variants.
