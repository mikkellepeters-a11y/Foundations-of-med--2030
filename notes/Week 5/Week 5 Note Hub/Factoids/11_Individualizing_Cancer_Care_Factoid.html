Foundations of Medicine · Week 5 Factoid Sheet

Individualizing Care of the Cancer Patient

Master framework: Modern oncology combines histology, molecular profiling, predictive biomarkers, pharmacogenomics, and clonal evolution.

Biomarkers

ConceptHigh-yield fact
PredictivePredicts response to a specific therapy.
PrognosticPredicts outcome independent of a specific therapy.
Oncotype DXTumor gene-expression assay for recurrence/chemo-benefit in selected HR+/HER2− early breast cancer; not hereditary BRCA testing.

Pharmacogenomics

ConceptHigh-yield fact
DPYD/DPD deficiencySevere fluoropyrimidine toxicity risk.
UGT1A1 reduced activityHigher irinotecan/SN-38 toxicity.
G6PD deficiencyOxidant hemolysis risk; rasburicase is a classic concern.

Actionable biology

ConceptHigh-yield fact
BCR-ABLImatinib-class tyrosine-kinase targeting.
HER2Trastuzumab-class targeting.
Hormone receptorCreates endocrine-therapy vulnerability; tamoxifen antagonizes estrogen in breast.
Checkpoint inhibitorsCan cause autoimmune-like immune-related adverse events.
CAR-TCytokine-release syndrome + neurotoxicity are classic toxicities.
Clonal selectionTreatment removes sensitive clones and favors resistant subclones.

Exam traps

  • Somatic targetability and inherited risk are separate questions.
  • Oncotype DX ≠ BRCA germline testing.
  • A biomarker may be diagnostic, prognostic, predictive—or more than one—but define the role asked.
Last 30 seconds: Predictive=response • Prognostic=outcome • DPYD→5-FU toxicity • UGT1A1→irinotecan • G6PD→rasburicase • drivers guide targeted therapy.
