Basics of Innate Immunity II

William K. Dolen, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 3 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

This lecture takes the innate immune system from a list of components to a set of testable mechanisms. You are expected to understand how danger is recognized, how inflammatory signaling is transmitted, how neutrophils physically reach and kill pathogens, and how a specific cytokine loop activates macrophages against intracellular organisms.

  • TLRs: compartment + ligand + signaling pathway + clinical consequences of failed signaling.
  • Inflammasome: NLRP3 -> caspase-1 -> IL-1beta/IL-18 + pyroptosis; overactivation -> autoinflammatory disease.
  • Other PRRs: know that STING, RLRs, NOD/NLRs, and C-type lectin receptors exist and what broad danger they detect.
  • Neutrophils: rolling/adhesion/diapedesis/chemotaxis -> phagocytosis -> respiratory burst -> microbial killing.
  • Neutrophil disorders: Chédiak-Higashi vs CGD should be distinguishable from a vignette.
  • IL-12/IFN-gamma axis: macrophage -> IL-12 -> T/NK -> IFN-gamma -> activated macrophage; defects -> MSMD.
Need to memorize - master list
  1. Surface vs endosomal TLRs: surface TLRs mainly bacterial; endosomal TLR3/7/8/9 mainly nucleic-acid sensing.
  2. TLR4 = LPS. TLR3 = dsRNA. TLR7/8 = ssRNA. TLR9 = unmethylated CpG DNA.
  3. TLR signaling: TLR -> MyD88 -> IRAK4 -> TRAF6/IKK -> I-kappa-B phosphorylation/degradation -> NF-kappa-B -> cytokines + adhesion molecules.
  4. MyD88/IRAK4 defects: recurrent pyogenic bacterial infections, especially S. pneumoniae; viral immunity can be relatively preserved.
  5. NLRP3 inflammasome: cryopyrin/NLRP3 -> procaspase-1 -> caspase-1 -> pro-IL-1beta/IL-18 -> active cytokines; can trigger pyroptosis.
  6. Autoinflammatory = innate. Autoimmune = adaptive.
  7. FCAS1: AD NLRP3 gain-of-function, cold-triggered fever/rash/arthralgia; IL-1 blockade works.
  8. Other PRRs: STING -> cytosolic DNA -> type I IFN; RLR -> cytosolic viral RNA -> type I IFN; C-type lectin -> carbohydrates/fungi; NOD/NLR -> intracellular microbial/danger sensing.
  9. Extravasation: selectin/Sialyl-Lewis-x slowing/rolling -> integrin LFA-1/ICAM-1 firm adhesion -> diapedesis -> chemotaxis.
  10. Respiratory burst: NADPH oxidase O2 -> superoxide -> SOD -> H2O2 -> MPO + Cl- -> HOCl bleach.
  11. CGD: NADPH oxidase defect; recurrent catalase-positive infections; DHR abnormal; X-linked form most common.
  12. Chédiak-Higashi: LYST/lysosomal trafficking defect, giant granules, partial albinism/silvery hair, recurrent pyogenic infections, neuropathy; impaired phagolysosome function and abnormal neutrophil trafficking.
  13. IL-12/IFN-gamma: macrophage IL-12 -> T/NK -> IFN-gamma -> macrophage activation through IFN-gamma receptor/JAK-STAT.
  14. MSMD: defects in IL-12/IFN-gamma signaling -> disseminated weakly virulent mycobacteria/BCG + Salmonella.

LO 3.1 - The Toll-Like Receptor System

🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Dolen calls the TLR signaling cascade “very high yield” because of its clinical relevance. This lecture is not just asking “which TLR binds what?” It wants you to connect recognition -> signaling -> transcription -> cytokines -> disease when the pathway is broken.

Toll-like receptor (TLR): a germline-encoded pattern-recognition receptor that detects conserved microbial molecular patterns and triggers intracellular inflammatory or antiviral signaling.

BOARD PRECISION

Dolen notes that most TLRs use the MyD88 pathway and that the IL-1 receptor also uses this signaling logic. This is why MyD88/IRAK4 defects create a characteristic bacterial susceptibility rather than eliminating every form of innate recognition.

PHARMACOLOGY FACTOID

The slide labels a TLR7/8 agonist as “Inquimod.” The standard drug name is imiquimod. The conceptual point is that pharmacology can intentionally stimulate TLR signaling for antiviral/antitumor effects.

LO 3.2 - NLRP3, the Inflammasome, and Autoinflammatory Disease

Inflammasome: a cytosolic multiprotein signaling complex that activates inflammatory caspases, especially caspase-1, in response to intracellular danger.
NLRP3 / cryopyrin: the NOD-like receptor emphasized by Dolen because it can assemble an inflammasome and is mutated in classic autoinflammatory syndromes.
Cropped from lecture page 23. Focus on assembly and enzymatic activation: NLRP3/cryopyrin recruits procaspase-1, which becomes caspase-1 and processes inflammatory cytokine precursors.

Pyroptosis: inflammatory programmed cell death associated with inflammasome/caspase activity and release of inflammatory cellular contents.
🩺 SEVERE INFLAMMATION CONNECTION

Dolen connects intense NLRP3 activation with release of inflammatory cytokines and notes that activated inflammasomes can contribute to cytokine storm and septic shock. He also uses severe COVID-19 as an example of dysregulated NLRP3-associated inflammation.

CAPS and familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome

Cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes (CAPS): autoinflammatory disorders caused by excessive NLRP3/cryopyrin signaling and IL-1 activity.
Dolen examplesKey point
Familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome (FCAS1)Cold-triggered episodes; NLRP3 gain-of-function.

Need to memorize - FCAS1

Cold exposure -> fever + non-urticarial rash + arthralgias • autosomal dominant • NLRP3 gain-of-function • responds to IL-1 blockade.

🩺 LOGICAL THERAPY

If excess IL-1 is driving the syndrome, block IL-1. Dolen lists canakinumab (anti-IL-1beta monoclonal antibody), rilonacept (IL-1 inhibitor/trap), and anakinra (recombinant IL-1 receptor antagonist).

PROFESSOR / LOCAL FUN BIT

Dolen notes that FCAS is described as rare in the literature, then adds: “but not in South Georgia.” He describes an MCG family in which a grandmother's detailed family history helped lead to discovery of the gene responsible for the family's mysterious disorder.

RECOGNIZE, DON'T MEMORIZE EVERY DETAIL

Dolen also lists familial Mediterranean fever, TRAPS, Behçet disease, CANDLE, mevalonate kinase deficiency/hyper-IgD syndrome, and Still disease. The lecture explicitly says more detail may come later. For this lecture, know the innate/autoinflammatory concept and CAPS/NLRP3 mechanism best.

Need to memorize - broad PRR map

RLR = cytosolic viral RNA -> type I IFN.
C-type lectin = microbial carbohydrates / fungi.

BOARD ENRICHMENT

A clean way to organize PRRs is by where the suspicious material is located: cell-surface TLRs survey extracellular microbial structures; endosomal TLRs inspect nucleic acids from internalized material; RLRs and STING-associated sensing monitor the cytosol; NLRs monitor intracellular bacterial/damage signals.

LO 3.4 - Neutrophils: From Bloodstream to Microbial Killing

🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Dolen says the neutrophil disorders in this lecture are uncommon but not esoteric and notes that patients with them have presented at MCG. The entire sequence - circulate -> extravasate -> phagocytose -> kill - is testable.

Neutrophil: a polymorphonuclear leukocyte (PMN; “poly” or “seg”) produced in bone marrow that circulates in blood and is rapidly recruited to sites of inflammation to phagocytose and kill pathogens.

Oxidase starts the burst; MPO makes the bleach. If you can remember where the pathway starts and where bleach appears, CGD vs MPO deficiency becomes much easier.

Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD)

CGD: inherited deficiency of a component of the NADPH oxidase complex, causing defective respiratory-burst generation of reactive oxygen species.
  • Dolen: boils, abscesses, pneumonia, osteomyelitis; delayed umbilical cord separation can occur but is not specific.
  • X-linked recessive is most common; Dolen identifies p91-phox as the X-linked component.
  • Autosomal-recessive defects occur in other NADPH oxidase subunits.
  • DHR assay = current test. Nitroblue tetrazolium = obsolete/older test.
Need to memorize - CGD pathogens from the companion handout

Staphylococcus aureus • Aspergillus • Serratia • Nocardia • Burkholderia.
The unifying board concept is catalase-positive organisms.

WHY CATALASE-POSITIVE ORGANISMS MATTER

The companion handout explains the mechanism: a CGD neutrophil cannot make its own H2O2 normally. Catalase-positive microbes also destroy the H2O2 they produce, depriving the neutrophil of a potential backup source for downstream oxidant killing.

Need to memorize - DHR

Normal neutrophil -> oxidative burst -> DHR fluorescence increases.
CGD -> poor ROS production -> reduced/absent DHR fluorescence.

Chédiak-Higashi syndrome

Chédiak-Higashi syndrome: inherited lysosomal-trafficking disorder caused by abnormal LYST function, producing giant cytoplasmic granules and defective leukocyte vesicle trafficking.
Lecture emphasisCompanion-handout addition
Large cytoplasmic granules interfere with neutrophil diapedesis and other functions; silvery blond hair/blue eyes; distinctive neutrophils. Impaired phagosome-lysosome fusion; recurrent pyogenic infections; partial oculocutaneous albinism; peripheral neuropathy; giant granules on smear.
Need to memorize - Chédiak-Higashi

LYST trafficking defect -> giant granules + impaired phagolysosome function/abnormal trafficking -> recurrent pyogenic infections + partial albinism + neuropathy.

⚠️ DO NOT MIX THE DEFECTS

CGD = killing chemistry fails. Chédiak-Higashi = intracellular trafficking/fusion machinery fails. Both produce recurrent infection, but the mechanism and physical clues are different.

🩺 VIGNETTE LOGIC

A child becomes very ill after BCG vaccination or develops disseminated environmental mycobacterial disease, yet does not otherwise look like classic SCID. Think about the IL-12 / IFN-gamma macrophage-activation axis.

MEMORY HOOK

“Mac eats bug -> calls 12 -> T/NK shout gamma -> Mac gets stronger.” The pathway is a positive feedback loop for killing intracellular organisms.

Common Exam Traps

TrapCorrect logic
“Every TLR uses the same ligand and location.”No. Attach each high-yield TLR to compartment + PAMP.
“TLR4 is just a receptor; signaling details do not matter.”Dolen specifically calls the MyD88/IRAK4/NF-kappa-B cascade very high yield.
“Autoinflammatory = autoimmune.”No. Innate inflammasome disease vs adaptive self-reactivity.
“Pyroptosis is just another word for apoptosis.”No. Pyroptosis is highly inflammatory; apoptosis is comparatively quiet.
“NLRP3 is the only PRR worth knowing.”No. It is the major NLR focus, but recognize STING, RLR, C-type lectin, and NOD pathways.
“Integrins roll; selectins adhere.”Board-standard sequence is the reverse: selectins roll, integrins firmly adhere.
“CGD neutrophils cannot phagocytose.”They can ingest microbes; the core problem is defective ROS-dependent killing.
“Chédiak-Higashi = NADPH oxidase defect.”No. Chédiak-Higashi = lysosomal trafficking/LYST.
“DHR is the test for Chédiak-Higashi.”No. DHR evaluates oxidative burst and is used for CGD.
“IL-12 activates macrophages directly.”The key loop is IL-12 -> T/NK -> IFN-gamma -> macrophage activation.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Innate immunity recognizes specific classes of danger through compartmentalized PRRs. TLRs detect extracellular or endosomal PAMPs and often signal through MyD88, IRAK4, IKK, and NF-kappa-B to turn on inflammatory genes; defects in that pathway produce distinctive pyogenic bacterial susceptibility. Intracellular NLRP3 can assemble an inflammasome that activates caspase-1, IL-1beta/IL-18, and pyroptosis; excessive activation causes autoinflammatory disease. Neutrophils then convert inflammatory signals into action by rolling, adhering, crossing endothelium, following chemotactic gradients, phagocytosing microbes, and using the NADPH oxidase respiratory burst for killing. CGD disrupts oxidant killing, whereas Chédiak-Higashi disrupts lysosomal trafficking. Finally, intracellular pathogens such as mycobacteria depend on the macrophage IL-12 -> T/NK IFN-gamma -> macrophage activation loop; defects create Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease.
