T Cell Receptors and the MHC

William K. Dolen, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 8 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

T cells do not inspect intact antigen the way B cells do. They inspect a processed peptide displayed by an MHC molecule. Almost everything in this lecture follows from deciding two things: where the antigen came from and which T-cell lineage should respond.

  • TCR recognition: the receptor recognizes the combined peptide-MHC surface, not peptide or MHC alone.
  • MHC I: endogenous/intracellular peptides -> CD8 cytotoxic T cells.
  • MHC II: exogenous/extracellular peptides -> CD4 helper T cells.
  • HLA genetics: chromosome 6, extremely polymorphic, inherited as haplotypes, codominantly expressed.
  • Antigen processing: proteasome/TAP/ER for class I; phagolysosome/invariant chain/CLIP/HLA-DM for class II.
  • Clinical application: HLA typing, abacavir HLA-B*5701, bare lymphocyte syndromes, and transplant rejection.
Need to memorize - master list
  1. Professional APCs: dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells.
  2. TCR recognizes peptide + MHC as one unit.
  3. CD4 -> MHC II. CD8 -> MHC I. Rule of 8: 4x2=8, 8x1=8.
  4. MHC/HLA locus = chromosome 6.
  5. Class I HLA: A, B, C. Class II HLA: DP, DQ, DR.
  6. MHC I structure: one alpha chain + beta2-microglobulin.
  7. MHC II structure: alpha chain + beta chain.
  8. Class I expression: essentially all nucleated cells. Class II: professional APCs.
  9. Class I presents endogenous antigen to CD8.
  10. Class II presents exogenous antigen to CD4.
  11. MHC I processing: ubiquitin/proteasome -> TAP -> ER -> MHC I -> surface.
  12. MHC II processing: endocytosis/phagocytosis -> phagolysosome; invariant chain -> CLIP; HLA-DM swaps CLIP for peptide -> surface.
  13. TAP deficiency / BLS I: poor MHC I peptide loading -> poor CD8 responses.
  14. BLS II: defective MHC II expression/transcription -> impaired CD4 responses.
  15. Abacavir: screen for HLA-B*5701.
  16. Hyperacute rejection: minutes, preformed antibody, thrombosis.
  17. Acute rejection: days-weeks-months, T cells and/or antibody, potentially treatable.
  18. Chronic rejection: months-years, graft arteriosclerosis + fibrosis, generally irreversible.
  19. GVHD: donor T cells attack recipient; classic rash + diarrhea + liver injury.

LO 7.1 - How T Cells “See” Antigen

Antigen-presenting cell (APC): a cell that processes protein antigen into peptides and displays those peptides bound to MHC molecules for T-cell inspection.
Need to memorize - professional APCs

Dendritic cell • macrophage • B cell.

Professional APCUseful way to think about it
Dendritic cellCompanion handout: the most potent activator of naive T cells.
MacrophagePhagocytoses microbes and presents antigen to T cells while participating in tissue inflammation.
B cellUses its BCR to internalize specific antigen, then presents peptide to helper T cells.

B cells and T cells recognize antigen differently

B-cell receptorT-cell receptor
Can bind an epitope on a relatively intact antigen.Cannot meaningfully “see” intact protein antigen.
Antigen recognition does not require an MHC display platform.Needs a processed peptide presented by MHC.
Can drive receptor-mediated endocytosis of bound antigen.Inspects peptide-MHC complexes on another cell's surface.
MHC restriction: the principle that a TCR recognizes antigen only in the context of the appropriate self-MHC molecule. Dolen phrases this as recognizing the unity of peptide plus MHC, not one or the other alone.
🔥 PROFESSOR HIGH-YIELD CONCEPT

MHC-peptide binding itself is relatively degenerate: one MHC molecule can bind many different peptides. The TCR, however, is specific for a particular peptide-MHC combination.

CD4 and CD8 stabilize the right MHC interaction

Cropped from lecture p. 20. CD8 stabilizes interaction with MHC I; CD4 stabilizes interaction with MHC II. The TCR simultaneously recognizes the displayed peptide-MHC surface.
Need to memorize - Rule of 8

CD4 x MHC II = 8.
CD8 x MHC I = 8.

COMPANION-HANDOUT ENRICHMENT - “SIGNAL 1”

The handout calls the TCR + peptide-MHC + co-receptor interaction the first signal of T-cell activation. Board preview: activation of a naive T cell also requires appropriate costimulation; signal 1 alone is not the entire activation story.

LO 7.2 - MHC Class I vs Class II

Major histocompatibility complex (MHC): a highly polymorphic gene region on chromosome 6 encoding antigen-presenting proteins. In humans, these molecules are called human leukocyte antigens (HLA).
Need to memorize - HLA locus

Chromosome 6 • highly polymorphic • inherited • codominantly expressed.

⚠️ “ONLY APCs” IS A USEFUL STARTING RULE, NOT EVERY POSSIBLE BIOLOGIC EXCEPTION

The handout uses the high-yield rule that MHC II is on professional APCs. That is exactly how to answer introductory comparison questions. In later immunology, remember that inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-gamma can induce MHC II expression on some additional cell types.

Haplotype inheritance

HLA haplotype: the linked set of HLA alleles inherited together on one chromosome 6.
  • You inherit one HLA haplotype from each parent.
  • HLA genes are close together, so they tend to travel together as a block.
  • Expression is codominant: maternal and paternal alleles can both be expressed.
  • A serologic family name such as HLA-B27 can contain many molecularly distinct alleles.
Need to memorize - haplotype logic

One HLA block from mom + one from dad, both expressed.

LOWER PRIORITY

Dolen discusses detailed modern HLA allele nomenclature such as HLA-B*02:101:01:02N and the difference between serologic-family names and molecular typing. Know what the notation represents; do not spend your main study time decoding every field unless specifically assigned.

Dolen's clinical uses

  • Paternity testing.
  • Predicting adverse drug reactions.
  • Disease associations.
  • Forensic analysis, although modern DNA approaches are more commonly used.

⭐ WHY INVARIANT CHAIN MATTERS

The companion handout explains the logic: newly synthesized MHC II sits in the ER, where many endogenous peptides are present. Invariant chain temporarily blocks the groove so MHC II does not accidentally load the wrong pathway's peptides.

Bare lymphocyte syndrome II

Bare lymphocyte syndrome type II: inherited defects in transcriptional regulation of MHC II expression, producing defective class II antigen presentation.
Need to memorize - BLS II

Autosomal recessive • deficient MHC II expression -> impaired CD4 T-cell activation.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT

Classic BLS II defects involve transcriptional regulators such as CIITA/RFX-family factors. Conceptually, this differs from TAP deficiency: BLS I = can't load class I peptide properly; BLS II = can't express class II properly.

LO 7.5 - Transplantation and Rejection

Why HLA and ABO matter

  • Dolen emphasizes matching HLA-A/B/C; matching DP/DQ/DR also helps.
  • ABO antigens occur on vascular endothelium, not just RBCs.
  • Recipients can have natural IgM isohemagglutinins against incompatible ABO antigens.
  • Even excellent HLA/ABO matching cannot eliminate every minor histocompatibility antigen difference.
Need to memorize - pretransplant logic

ABO compatibility + HLA matching + antibody crossmatch reduce rejection risk.

Hyperacute vs acute vs chronic

AcuteDays, weeks, or monthsT cells and/or newly formed antibodyCellular: lymphocytic infiltration; humoral: vascular injuryOften responsive to immunosuppression
ChronicMonths-yearsSlow immune-mediated vascular injury/remodelingGraft arteriosclerosis + interstitial fibrosisGenerally irreversible
Need to memorize - rejection timing

Hyper = here NOW. Preformed antibody, thrombosis.
Acute = activated immune response. T cells +/- antibody.
Chronic = chronic narrowing/scarring. Arteriosclerosis + fibrosis.

⚠️ HYPERACUTE IS NOT “FAST T-CELL REJECTION”

If the organ fails essentially immediately after reperfusion, think antibody already existed before transplant. The immune system did not have time to generate a new T-cell response.

Graft-versus-host disease (GVHD)

GVHD: donor immune cells - especially donor T cells - recognize the recipient as foreign and attack host tissues.
Need to memorize - GVHD

Graft attacks host. Companion handout classic triad: maculopapular rash + diarrhea + liver injury/jaundice.

⚠️ REJECTION vs GVHD

Solid-organ rejection: recipient immune system attacks graft.
GVHD: immunocompetent donor T cells attack recipient, classically after hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - DIRECT ALLORECOGNITION

In acute cellular rejection, recipient T cells can react strongly to donor MHC molecules displayed by donor APCs and graft cells. This unusually strong alloresponse helps explain why HLA mismatch is so immunogenic.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

T cells recognize processed peptide presented by MHC, not intact antigen. MHC I (HLA-A/B/C) is expressed on essentially all nucleated cells, carries endogenous peptides processed by the proteasome and TAP into the ER, and presents to CD8 T cells. MHC II (HLA-DP/DQ/DR) is centered on professional APCs, presents phagocytosed/endocytosed exogenous peptides to CD4 T cells, and uses invariant chain, CLIP, and HLA-DM to control peptide loading. HLA genes are highly polymorphic chromosome-6 genes inherited as codominant haplotypes, which makes them useful in disease/drug associations but central to transplant compatibility. Hyperacute rejection is preformed-antibody thrombosis, acute rejection is T-cell and/or antibody mediated, chronic rejection produces graft arteriosclerosis/fibrosis, and GVHD is donor T cells attacking the recipient.
