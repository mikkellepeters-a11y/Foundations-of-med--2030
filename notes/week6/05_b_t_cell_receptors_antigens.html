B Cell and T Cell Receptors, and their Antigens

William K. Dolen, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 5 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

This is a structure-to-function lecture. You are learning what BCRs/antibodies and TCRs physically look like, how receptor diversity is generated, how B cells later change antibody class without changing antigen specificity, and how receptors actually recognize antigens.

  • Antibody anatomy: heavy/light chains, variable/constant regions, Fab/Fc, classes/subclasses, CDRs.
  • Classic digestion: papain vs pepsin - old terminology that still shows up on exams.
  • Monoclonal antibodies: hybridoma concept + lecture-specific mouse/chimeric/humanized/human suffixes.
  • TCR: structurally Fab-like, usually alpha-beta, always membrane bound, recognizes processed peptide on MHC.
  • V(D)J: VJ vs VDJ logic, RAG1/2, junctional diversity, TRECs, SCID/Omenn, DNA-repair consequences.
  • Class switching: change heavy-chain constant region/effect, keep the same antigen-binding VDJ; know Dolen's exact heavy-chain sequence.
  • Antigens: antigen vs immunogen, epitope, hapten, linear vs conformational epitopes, affinity, cross-reactivity/molecular mimicry.
Need to memorize - master list
  1. 5 isotypes: IgG, IgA, IgM, IgE, IgD. IgG subclasses 1-4; IgA subclasses 1-2.
  2. Antibody = 2 identical heavy + 2 identical light chains joined by disulfide bonds.
  3. Heavy-chain constant region determines antibody class/subclass.
  4. Light chains: kappa or lambda, never both in one antibody; Dolen gives human kappa:lambda about 2:1.
  5. Fab binds antigen; Fc mediates downstream effector functions.
  6. Each heavy-chain V region has 3 CDRs + each light-chain V region has 3 CDRs.
  7. Papain -> 2 Fab + 1 intact Fc. Pepsin -> 1 F(ab')2 + degraded Fc fragments.
  8. mAb suffixes taught in this lecture: -omab mouse, -ximab chimeric, -zumab humanized, -umab human.
  9. TCR: usually alpha-beta; one V + one C region per chain; monovalent; never secreted; recognizes processed peptide + MHC.
  10. VJ: B light chain + TCR alpha. VDJ: B heavy chain + TCR beta.
  11. RAG1/RAG2 direct receptor recombination; P/N nucleotide addition increases junctional diversity.
  12. Low TREC = abnormal T-cell development; SCID is a major cause.
  13. RAG loss -> T- B- NK+ SCID; partial RAG/Artemis -> Omenn syndrome.
  14. Class switching changes constant region/isotype, NOT antigen specificity.
  15. Dolen's heavy-chain gene order: M - D - G3 - G1 - A1 - G2 - G4 - E - A2.
  16. Companion-handout class-switch machinery: CD40-CD40L + cytokines + AID; CD40L defect -> Hyper-IgM syndrome.
  17. BCR recognizes intact/native epitopes; TCR recognizes processed peptide presented on MHC.
  18. Affinity = strength of one epitope-binding-site interaction.
  19. Molecular mimicry/cross-reactivity: classic lecture example = rheumatic fever after S. pyogenes.

LO 5.1 - B-Cell Receptor / Antibody Structure

B-cell receptor (BCR): membrane-bound immunoglobulin expressed by a B cell. A secreted molecule with the same immunoglobulin architecture is called an antibody.
Immunoglobulin isotype/class: antibody category determined by the heavy-chain constant region: IgG, IgA, IgM, IgE, or IgD.
Subclass: subdivision within an isotype. Dolen lists IgG1-4 and IgA1-2 in humans.
Cropped from lecture page 4. Use this to orient the Y-shaped molecule: two heavy chains form the inner arms/stem, two light chains sit on the outer arms, Fab contains the antigen-binding ends, and Fc is the constant stem region.

The 4-chain architecture

ComponentStructureWhat it contributes
Heavy chains2 identical chainsPart of antigen-binding site + entire Fc region; heavy-chain constant region determines class/subclass.
Light chains2 identical chainsPart of each antigen-binding site; either kappa or lambda.
Disulfide bondsCovalent stabilizing bondsHold heavy-heavy and heavy-light components together.
Need to memorize - antibody skeleton

2 heavy + 2 light • disulfide bonds • N-termini bind antigen • C-terminal heavy-chain region determines class/effect.

Variable vs constant regions

RegionLocationMain job
Variable regionN-terminal portion of heavy and light chainsCreates antigen specificity. Heavy variable = VH; light variable = VL.
Constant regionMore C-terminal portionDefines chain class/subclass and supports effector function.
Fab: “fragment, antigen-binding” region - the antibody arms containing the antigen-binding site.
Fc: constant stem region that engages complement/Fc receptors and determines important effector properties after antigen binding.

The handout emphasizes the simple split: Fab decides what the antibody binds; Fc helps decide what happens next. Fc-dependent functions include complement activation, opsonization, and engagement of Fc receptors on immune cells.

CDRs - where specificity gets extremely specific

Complementarity-determining region (CDR): a hypervariable loop at the tip of an immunoglobulin/TCR variable domain that directly contributes to antigen recognition.
  • Dolen treats hypervariable region and CDR as synonymous terms for this course.
  • Each heavy-chain variable domain contains 3 CDRs.
  • Each light-chain variable domain contains 3 CDRs.
  • Therefore, one antibody antigen-binding site is shaped by six CDR loops total - three heavy + three light.
Need to memorize - CDRs

3 on VH + 3 on VL = 6 CDR loops shaping one antigen-binding site.

LO 5.4 - T-Cell Receptor Structure

T-cell receptor (TCR): membrane antigen receptor on a T lymphocyte. It resembles a single antibody Fab-like unit but has important recognition and signaling differences.
FeatureBCR / antibodyTCR
Common chain pairHeavy + lightAlpha + beta; gamma-delta subset also exists
Binding sites2 per antibody molecule (bivalent)1 (monovalent)
Secreted?Yes - secreted immunoglobulin = antibodyNo - always membrane bound
What it recognizesIntact/native antigen, including proteins, carbohydrates and other structuresProcessed peptide fragment presented on MHC
Variable-region CDRsPresentPresent
Need to memorize - TCR

Alpha is analogous to BCR light chain; beta is analogous to BCR heavy chain.
TCR is Fab-like, monovalent, never secreted, and requires peptide-MHC.

⚠️ BCR vs TCR EXAM TRAP

A BCR can bind the native three-dimensional surface of an intact antigen. A conventional TCR cannot simply bind a free intact protein; it recognizes a processed peptide in the context of MHC.

WHY “SLOPPY” JOINING IS BOTH BRILLIANT AND DANGEROUS

The lecture emphasizes that imprecise DNA joining increases diversity through junctional P/N nucleotides, but the same DNA-cutting machinery can produce dangerous chromosomal errors. That is why developmental checkpoints and DNA-repair systems matter.

BRECs and TRECs

Receptor excision circle: loop of unused DNA excised during receptor rearrangement. Dolen calls the B-cell versions BRECs and T-cell versions TRECs.
Need to memorize - TREC newborn screening

Low TREC count = impaired T-cell development. Dolen says TREC counting is part of newborn screening in all 50 states and labels the concept very high yield. SCID is a serious cause of low TRECs.

Partial receptor-recombination defect + rash/erythroderma + eosinophilia + high/preserved IgE + restricted T cells = Omenn.

Class Switching - Keep Specificity, Change the Tool

Class-switch recombination: activated B-cell DNA rearrangement that places the already-formed VDJ antigen-binding region next to a different heavy-chain constant-region gene, changing antibody isotype without changing antigen specificity.
Cropped from lecture page 39. VDJ is preserved while intervening heavy-chain constant-region DNA is looped out. Upstream deleted options are gone; downstream constant-region genes can remain available for future switching.

Before switching

  • After VDJ assembly, Dolen describes transcripts including mu and delta, allowing mature naive B cells to express IgM and IgD.
  • In a primary immune response, Dolen states that the first circulating class made by plasma cells is IgM.

What switching changes - and what it does NOT change

Stays the sameChanges
VDJ variable region / antigen specificityHeavy-chain constant region / antibody isotype and effector function
COMPANION-HANDOUT CLASS-SWITCH MACHINERY

The handout adds the board-level mechanism: CD40 on B cell + CD40L on activated helper T cell + cytokines + AID. AID creates breaks in switch regions; cytokines help direct which isotype is selected. CD40L deficiency -> Hyper-IgM syndrome.

Need to memorize - Hyper-IgM

High IgM + very low IgG/IgA/IgE + failed class switching -> think CD40L defect.

Dolen's TESTABLE heavy-chain gene order

Need to memorize EXACTLY

M - D - G3 - G1 - A1 - G2 - G4 - E - A2

Dolen's mnemonic: “My Dear Great Grandma And Great Grandpa Eat Apples.”

🔥 WHY ORDER MATTERS

When a B cell switches downstream, the DNA between VDJ and the new constant region is looped out and gone forever. Genes distal to the new position may remain available for another switch, but genes deleted upstream are no longer options.

⚠️ SOURCE SIMPLIFICATION

The handout summarizes heavy-chain classes as mu, delta, gamma, epsilon, alpha. Dolen's lecture specifically expands this to the subclass-level genomic order above and says the sequence is testable. Use the detailed Dolen sequence for course recall.

LO 5.6 - Antigens, Epitopes, and CDR Binding

SOURCE DEFINITION DIFFERENCE

Dolen's slide 44 calls antigens molecules that can induce an adaptive immune response. The companion handout separates two terms more precisely: antigen = can be bound by an immune receptor; immunogen = can actually elicit an immune response. This guide keeps that distinction because it explains haptens cleanly.

Antigen: in the companion-handout usage, a substance that can be specifically bound by a BCR/antibody or recognized by a TCR after processing/presentation.
Immunogen: an antigen capable of eliciting an immune response.
Epitope / antigenic determinant: the small specific part of an antigen contacted by a receptor's CDRs.
Hapten: small molecule that can be antigenic but is not immunogenic alone; attachment to a carrier protein can make the combination immunogenic. The handout gives penicillin as the classic drug-allergy example.

Antigens can carry one or many epitopes

  • Univalent: one epitope recognized.
  • Polyvalent: multiple distinct epitopes.
  • A large antigen can contain some B-cell epitopes and different T-cell epitopes.

Linear vs conformational epitopes

Epitope typeMeaningRecognition consequence
LinearResidues adjacent in primary sequenceCan remain recognizable even when a protein is unfolded; processed T-cell peptides are linear sequences.
ConformationalResidues distant in primary sequence but brought together by 3D foldingClassic intact-protein BCR/antibody recognition; may disappear if structure is denatured.

BCR vs TCR “view of the world”

BCR / antibodyTCR
Can recognize epitopes on intact native protein or carbohydrate antigens; Dolen says B cells can then internalize the whole antigen by receptor-mediated endocytosis.Relies on other cells to process proteins and recognizes short amino-acid sequences carried by MHC.
Need to memorize - receptor recognition

B sees the intact surface. T sees the processed peptide + MHC.

What physically holds antigen and antibody together?

Affinity: Dolen defines affinity as the net sum of attractive and repulsive forces in the interaction between one antigen epitope and one antibody binding site.
  • Binding is noncovalent.
  • Dolen emphasizes electrostatic interactions/salt bridges and hydrogen bonds as most important.
  • Van der Waals and hydrophobic forces also contribute.
  • The interface is not literally a rigid “lock and key”; three-dimensional conformation and an interacting atomic cloud determine whether binding is favorable enough.
⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - affinity vs avidity

Affinity is the strength of one binding site interacting with one epitope. Avidity is the overall combined binding strength when multiple binding sites engage multiple epitopes. The source explicitly defines affinity; avidity is added here as a commonly tested distinction.

Common Exam Traps

TrapCorrect logic
“Fab determines antibody class.”No. Heavy-chain constant/Fc region determines class/subclass.
“Kappa and lambda are antibody classes.”No. They are light-chain types.
“Papain gives F(ab')2.”No. Papain gives 2 separate Fab + intact Fc; pepsin preserves F(ab')2.
“TCR can be secreted like antibody.”No. Conventional TCR stays membrane bound.
“BCR and TCR both bind free intact antigen.”No. TCR requires processed peptide on MHC.
“All receptor chains use VDJ.”No. Light/alpha = VJ; heavy/beta = VDJ.
“Class switching changes what antigen the antibody recognizes.”No. VDJ specificity stays the same; constant region/effect changes.
“RAG deficiency should remove NK cells too.”No. NK cells do not require V(D)J-generated antigen receptors.
“A low TREC proves only one disease.”No. It indicates impaired T-cell development; SCID is one important cause.
“Antigen and immunogen always mean the exact same thing.”The handout distinguishes binding (antigen) from ability to elicit a response (immunogen).
“Lock-and-key is literal.”Dolen explicitly says binding is a conformational/noncovalent interaction, not a rigid literal lock-and-key fit.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Antibodies/BCRs are four-chain molecules with two identical heavy and two identical light chains. Their variable-region CDRs bind antigen, while the heavy-chain constant region determines class and downstream effector function. Papain separates two Fab arms from Fc; pepsin leaves a linked F(ab')2. Monoclonal antibodies can be made through hybridoma technology and Dolen teaches legacy suffixes that indicate mouse versus human content. TCRs are Fab-like alpha-beta heterodimers that remain membrane bound and recognize processed peptide-MHC. Initial receptor diversity comes from RAG-mediated V(D)J recombination: light/TCR-alpha use VJ, heavy/TCR-beta use VDJ, with junctional diversity adding more variation. Recombination leaves TRECs/BRECs and can fail in SCID/Omenn or contribute to translocations such as Burkitt lymphoma. Activated B cells can later class switch by moving the same VDJ next to a different heavy-chain constant region, changing isotype without changing specificity. Finally, CDRs bind epitopes through noncovalent forces; cross-reactivity with similar self epitopes can produce molecular mimicry such as rheumatic fever.
