Inflammation and Repair

Hasan Samra, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 11 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

Inflammation is a coordinated response that delivers plasma proteins and leukocytes to injured tissue, removes the cause and dead cells, and then transitions into resolution, regeneration, or scar formation. The highest-yield questions come from following that sequence and knowing the molecules at each step.

  • Acute inflammation: rapid vascular change + neutrophil recruitment.
  • Extravasation: margination -> rolling -> firm adhesion -> diapedesis -> chemotaxis.
  • Macrophage pivot: continued acute inflammation, chronic inflammation, or resolution/healing.
  • Chronic inflammation: macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells + simultaneous injury and repair.
  • Systemic inflammation: fever, acute-phase proteins, leukocytosis.
  • Repair: regeneration when architecture/cell capacity permit; fibrosis when they do not.
Need to memorize - master list
  1. 5 cardinal signs: rubor, calor, tumor, dolor, functio laesa.
  2. Acute = minutes-hours, lasts days, neutrophils. Chronic = weeks-years, macrophages/lymphocytes/plasma cells.
  3. Vascular sequence: vasodilation -> increased permeability/protein-rich edema -> stasis -> leukocyte margination.
  4. Extravasation: margination -> rolling -> adhesion -> diapedesis -> chemotaxis.
  5. Rolling = selectins: E-selectin induced by TNF/IL-1; P-selectin stored in Weibel-Palade bodies and mobilized by histamine; leukocyte sialyl-Lewis X.
  6. Firm adhesion = integrins: leukocyte CD11/CD18 -> endothelial ICAM-1.
  7. Diapedesis = PECAM-1/CD31.
  8. LAD-1: CD18 defect -> impaired adhesion, neutrophilia, recurrent bacterial infection, little/no pus; board add-on: delayed cord separation.
  9. LAD-2: defective sialyl-Lewis X/fucosylation -> impaired rolling.
  10. Chemotaxis: bacterial products, IL-8/CXCL8, LTB4, C5a.
  11. Neutrophils first; macrophages peak ~2-3 days.
  12. Granuloma: epithelioid macrophages +/- giant cells; necrosis favors infection such as TB/fungi.
  13. Acute-phase response: IL-6/IL-1/TNF -> liver; fibrinogen raises ESR; hepcidin causes iron sequestration.
  14. Bacterial = neutrophilia + left shift; viral = lymphocytosis; allergy/parasites = eosinophilia.
  15. Regenerative capacity: labile, stable, permanent.
  16. Healing: 24-48 h inflammatory -> days 3-7 proliferative/granulation -> week 1 to months remodeling.
  17. Proliferative phase: macrophages, fibroblasts, type III collagen, angiogenesis/VEGF, myofibroblasts, epithelial proliferation.
  18. Remodeling: type III -> type I collagen; tensile strength rises but does not return to 100%.
  19. Keloid extends beyond original wound. Board comparison: hypertrophic scar stays within boundaries.
  20. Contracture: exaggerated wound contraction, classically after severe burns.

1. General Inflammation - Purpose and Cardinal Signs

Inflammation: a protective response involving blood vessels, leukocytes, and soluble mediators that is triggered by injury, infection, necrosis, foreign material, or immune reactions.

Three purposes

  1. Respond to the cause of cell injury.
  2. Remove necrotic/dead cells.
  3. Initiate tissue repair.
⚠️ WHEN INFLAMMATION BECOMES HARMFUL

Excessive inflammation can cause septic shock; prolonged inflammation occurs with persistent infection such as TB; inappropriate inflammation causes immune-mediated disease.

Five cardinal signs

Need to memorize

Rubor, calor, tumor, dolor, functio laesa = red, hot, swollen, painful, impaired.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - PAIN

Bradykinin and PGE2 are classic inflammatory pain mediators. PGE2 also contributes to fever and sensitizes nociceptors.

2. Acute Inflammation - Vessels First

Acute inflammation: rapid, transient response developing within minutes to hours and lasting hours to a few days, characterized by neutrophils.

Samra's triggers include bacterial infection, tissue necrosis from ischemia/hypoxia/trauma/toxins, foreign bodies, and immune reactions.

Vasodilation

Samra highlights nitric oxide as a mediator of arteriolar smooth-muscle relaxation. This produces rubor + calor.

Increased vascular permeability

Histamine promotes endothelial-cell retraction and gap formation, allowing protein-rich fluid to leave the circulation. This produces edema and swelling.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - EXUDATE vs TRANSUDATE

Exudate: inflammatory, protein-rich/cell-rich fluid due to increased vascular permeability. Transudate: low-protein fluid caused mainly by hydrostatic or oncotic imbalance without major inflammatory endothelial injury.

Integrins stop the cell: CD11/CD18 -> ICAM-1.

Diapedesis = PECAM-1

PECAM-1/CD31 is located at endothelial junctions. Neutrophils squeeze between endothelial cells and cross a basement membrane containing type IV collagen.

Ingest and destroy

Samra lists lysosomal proteases/elastase, defensins, cathelicidins, lysozyme, reactive oxygen species, and reactive nitrogen species.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - OPSONINS

The major opsonins to know are IgG Fc and C3b. They bind phagocyte Fc/complement receptors and make engulfment more efficient.

⚠️ COLLATERAL DAMAGE

Neutrophil/macrophage enzymes and oxidants do not distinguish perfectly between pathogen and host when released extracellularly, so inflammation itself can damage normal tissue and endothelium.

4. Neutrophils First, Macrophages Later

Cropped from lecture p. 22. Edema comes early, neutrophils dominate early, and macrophages rise later with a peak around days 2-3.
Need to memorize - timing

Neutrophils first. Macrophages later, peak ~2-3 days.

Macrophages decide the next chapter

  1. Continued acute inflammation: IL-8 recruits more neutrophils; abscess can form.
  2. Chronic inflammation: antigen presentation to CD4 T cells.
  3. Resolution/healing: IL-10 and TGF-beta help terminate inflammation and promote repair.
Abscess: localized collection of purulent inflammatory and necrotic tissue (pus), commonly caused by deep seeding of pyogenic bacteria.
Need to memorize - abscess

Central necrotic neutrophils/tissue + surrounding viable neutrophils.

5. Chronic Inflammation

Chronic inflammation: prolonged inflammatory response lasting weeks to years, dominated by mononuclear cells and characterized by ongoing tissue destruction together with repair.
Cropped from lecture p. 32. Acute inflammation is neutrophil-rich; chronic inflammation contains lymphocytes, plasma cells, and macrophages.
FeatureAcuteChronic
TimingMinutes-hours; daysWeeks-months-years
Dominant cellsNeutrophilsMacrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells
Immune emphasisInnateAdaptive + macrophage interaction

Causes Samra emphasizes

  • Persistent infection or unresolved acute infection.
  • Hypersensitivity/autoimmune disease: contact dermatitis, IBD, rheumatoid arthritis.
  • Prolonged toxic exposure: cigarette smoke, silica.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - M1 vs M2

M1 macrophages are inflammatory/microbicidal; M2 macrophages support repair and fibrosis. IFN-gamma/microbial signals favor M1-type activity, while IL-4/IL-13 favor M2-type repair programs.

Granulomatous inflammation

Granuloma: organized collection of activated macrophages that become epithelioid histiocytes, often with multinucleated giant cells and surrounding lymphocytes.
Cropped from lecture p. 33. Samra's TB example shows central necrosis, epithelioid histiocytes, and giant cells.

Samra's causes: TB and fungi (Histoplasma), foreign bodies, sarcoidosis, and Crohn disease.

Need to memorize - granuloma

Necrotizing/caseating granuloma -> infection first (TB/fungi). Noncaseating granuloma -> classic sarcoidosis association.

⚠️ NOT EVERY GRANULOMA IS TB

Granuloma is a reaction pattern, not a diagnosis. Infectious, immune-mediated, and foreign-body causes all exist.

6. Systemic Effects of Inflammation

Samra's three systemic effects

Fever • acute-phase proteins • leukocytosis.

Fever

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - FEVER PATHWAY

IL-1 and TNF drive prostaglandin production, especially PGE2, in hypothalamic thermoregulatory pathways, raising the temperature set point. This is why COX inhibition is antipyretic.

7. Tissue Healing - Regeneration vs Repair

Regeneration: replacement of damaged tissue by native tissue.
Repair: replacement of damaged tissue by fibrous scar.
Cropped from lecture p. 42. Mild superficial injury can regenerate; severe/deep injury that destroys tissue framework tends to scar.

Regenerative capacity

ClassMeaningExamples Samra gives
LabileContinuously cycling/stem-cell-supportedBowel mucosa, bone marrow, skin
StableUsually quiescent but can re-enter cycleLiver
PermanentMinimal regenerative potentialMyocardium, neurons
Need to memorize - labile/stable/permanent

Labile lives in the cycle • stable can restart • permanent generally scars.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - ECM MATTERS

Regeneration requires not only viable cells but also a sufficiently preserved extracellular-matrix scaffold. Destruction of the stromal framework pushes healing toward scar even in tissues with regenerative potential.

8. Wound Healing Timeline

24-48 hours - inflammatory phase

  • Rapid coagulation -> thrombus/clot.
  • Surface dehydration -> scab.
  • Neutrophils arrive within ~24 h and clear debris.

Days 3-7 - proliferative phase

Cropped from lecture p. 44. Granulation tissue fills the wound with new vessels, fibroblasts, macrophages, loose matrix, and early collagen.
Need to memorize - proliferative phase

Macrophages • granulation tissue • fibroblasts • type III collagen • angiogenesis/VEGF • myofibroblast contraction • epithelial proliferation.

Granulation tissue: newly formed vascular connective tissue composed of proliferating capillaries, fibroblasts, loose extracellular matrix, and inflammatory cells. It is not the same thing as a granuloma.
⚠️ GRANULATION TISSUE ≠ GRANULOMA

Granulation tissue = wound repair. Granuloma = chronic macrophage inflammatory structure. Similar words, totally different pathology.

1 week to 6 months+ - remodeling phase

Cropped from lecture p. 46. Remodeling replaces early type III collagen with stronger type I collagen and progressively increases tensile strength.
Need to memorize - collagen switch

Early = type III collagen. Remodeling = type I collagen replaces III.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - TENSILE STRENGTH

Wound strength rises over months as collagen is remodeled and cross-linked, but a mature scar generally reaches only about 70-80% of original tensile strength, not 100%.

Primary vs secondary intention

Factors that impair repair

Samra explicitly lists infection, diabetes or poor perfusion, nutritional status, mechanical factors, and type/extent/location of injury.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - VITAMIN C / ZINC / STEROIDS

Vitamin C is required for hydroxylation of proline/lysine in collagen; deficiency impairs wound healing. Zinc deficiency can also impair repair. Chronic glucocorticoids reduce inflammation and collagen synthesis and can delay healing.

9. Abnormalities in Tissue Repair

Wound dehiscence

Dehiscence: rupture/separation of a wound, often promoted by increased mechanical stress or pressure such as vomiting/coughing.

Ulceration

Ulcer: local surface defect produced by sloughing of inflamed necrotic tissue.

Samra highlights lower-extremity ulcers from poor arterial perfusion and neuropathic pressure ulcers in patients with loss of sensation, including diabetic neuropathy.

Keloid

Keloid: excessive collagen scar that extends beyond the boundaries of the original wound.
⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - KELOID vs HYPERTROPHIC SCAR

Keloid crosses the original wound border and tends to recur. Hypertrophic scar remains within the original border and may regress over time.

Contracture

Cropped from lecture p. 51. Exaggerated wound contraction after a burn can deform surrounding tissue and restrict joint motion.
Contracture: exaggerated wound contraction that produces deformity and impaired movement, especially after severe burns.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Inflammation starts with vascular changes that increase blood flow and permeability, then recruits leukocytes. Acute inflammation is neutrophil-dominant; neutrophils marginate, roll through selectins, adhere through CD11/CD18 integrins, cross by PECAM-1, and follow IL-8/LTB4/C5a/bacterial gradients. Macrophages arrive later and determine whether inflammation persists, becomes chronic, or resolves into healing. Chronic inflammation is dominated by macrophages, lymphocytes, and plasma cells and may form granulomas. Systemically, inflammatory cytokines drive fever, acute-phase proteins, and leukocytosis. Tissue then regenerates if viable cells and architecture remain, or repairs by fibrosis. Wound healing moves from inflammation to granulation tissue/type III collagen to remodeling/type I collagen; abnormal healing produces dehiscence, ulcers, keloids, or contractures.
