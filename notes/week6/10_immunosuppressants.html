Immunosuppressants

Yunchao Su, MD, PhD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 10 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

Immunosuppressants are easiest to learn by asking where in the immune response each drug interrupts the pathway. Su organizes the lecture into four broad classes, but the exam-relevant skill is matching each drug to its mechanism, major use, and signature toxicity.

  • Glucocorticoids: broad transcriptional suppression of inflammation and lymphocyte activity.
  • Calcineurin inhibitors: block calcineurin/NFAT -> decrease IL-2 transcription -> suppress T-cell activation.
  • Antiproliferative / antimetabolic drugs: interrupt mTOR, purine, folate, or DNA pathways required for lymphocyte expansion.
  • Polyclonal / monoclonal biologics: target lymphocytes, cytokines, or surface receptors much more selectively.

The unifying tradeoff is simple: less rejection/autoimmunity, but more infection, malignancy, and drug-specific organ toxicity.

Need to memorize - master list
  1. Four classes: glucocorticoids; calcineurin inhibitors; antiproliferative/antimetabolic agents; polyclonal/monoclonal antibodies.
  2. Prednisone/prednisolone: intracellular glucocorticoid receptor -> altered transcription; ↓ inflammatory mediators and lymphocyte proliferation.
  3. Cyclosporine: binds cyclophilin -> inhibits calcineurin -> ↓ NFAT activation -> ↓ IL-2.
  4. Tacrolimus: binds FKBP -> inhibits calcineurin -> ↓ NFAT activation -> ↓ IL-2.
  5. Calcineurin inhibitor toxicity: nephrotoxicity, hypertension, neurotoxicity; CYP3A interactions. Board split: cyclosporine = gingival hyperplasia/hirsutism; tacrolimus = more diabetes/neurotoxicity.
  6. Sirolimus/everolimus: bind FKBP but inhibit mTOR, not calcineurin -> block response to IL-2 and T-cell proliferation.
  7. Mycophenolate: inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase -> ↓ de novo guanine synthesis -> lymphocyte antiproliferation.
  8. Azathioprine: prodrug -> 6-MP/6-thioguanine nucleotides -> inhibits purine/DNA synthesis; TPMT matters; allopurinol interaction is board-relevant.
  9. Methotrexate: folate antagonist / DHFR inhibitor -> ↓ tetrahydrofolate-dependent nucleotide synthesis.
  10. Cyclophosphamide: alkylating prodrug -> DNA crosslinking; board toxicity = hemorrhagic cystitis from acrolein, prevented with MESNA.
  11. Antithymocyte globulin (ATG): polyclonal antibodies against multiple T-cell antigens -> T-cell depletion/blockade.
  12. Muromonab-CD3: anti-CD3 T-cell depletion; cytokine release syndrome + anti-mouse antibodies; historically important.
  13. Etanercept: soluble TNF-receptor fusion protein.
  14. Infliximab/adalimumab/golimumab/certolizumab: TNF-alpha blockers.
  15. Anti-TNF danger: serious infection, especially TB reactivation; screen for latent TB.
  16. General immunosuppression danger: opportunistic infection and malignancy risk.

The Big Picture - Where Immunosuppressants Interrupt Immunity

Cropped from lecture p. 3. Su frames immunosuppression along a sequence: antigen recognition -> lymphocyte proliferation -> differentiation/synthesis -> cell-cell interaction -> attack/tissue injury. Different drug classes interrupt different stages.
Need to memorize - why we immunosuppress

Two giant indications: prevent/treat transplant rejection and control autoimmune/inflammatory disease.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - THE PRICE OF IMMUNOSUPPRESSION

Any sufficiently potent immunosuppressive regimen raises the risk of serious/opportunistic infection. Chronic immune suppression also raises risk for certain malignancies, particularly lymphoproliferative disease and skin cancer. Always separate this class-wide effect from the signature toxicity of the individual drug.

Class 1 - Glucocorticoids

Glucocorticoid: steroid hormone/drug that diffuses through the cell membrane, binds an intracellular glucocorticoid receptor, and changes gene transcription to produce broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Mechanism

  • Su emphasizes interference with the cell cycle of activated lymphoid cells.
  • Decreases production of inflammatory mediators, including prostaglandins/leukotrienes and inflammatory cytokine programs.
  • Because the receptor is intracellular, glucocorticoids act primarily through gene regulation, not a surface receptor second messenger.
Cropped from lecture p. 7. The steroid-receptor complex changes nuclear transcription. Su contrasts transactivation-associated metabolic/endocrine effects with transrepression of pro-inflammatory transcription.
⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - NF-kB / AP-1

A useful board-level mechanism is suppression of pro-inflammatory transcription factors such as NF-kB and AP-1, contributing to reduced cytokine synthesis. This helps explain why steroids broadly suppress many inflammatory pathways at once.

Clinical uses

  • Solid-organ and hematopoietic-stem-cell transplant immunosuppression.
  • Treatment of organ rejection and graft-versus-host disease.
  • Rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, systemic lupus erythematosus, ITP, dermatomyositis, psoriasis, asthma, ophthalmic inflammation, and multiple-sclerosis exacerbations.
  • Premedication to blunt undesired immune reactions to other therapies.

Cushingoid + hyperglycemia + hypertension + osteoporosis/osteonecrosis + myopathy + cataracts/glaucoma + infection + adrenal suppression.

Class 2 - Calcineurin Inhibitors

Calcineurin: Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent phosphatase that dephosphorylates NFAT, allowing NFAT to enter the nucleus and promote IL-2 and other T-cell cytokine transcription.
Need to memorize - same endpoint, different binding protein

Cyclosporine + cyclophilin -> calcineurin inhibited.
Tacrolimus + FKBP -> calcineurin inhibited.
Both -> ↓ NFAT activation -> ↓ IL-2 -> ↓ T-cell activation/proliferation.

Cropped from lecture p. 13. Cyclosporine and tacrolimus use different immunophilins but converge on calcineurin inhibition, preventing NFAT-driven IL-2 transcription.

Uses

  • Prevention/treatment of organ-transplant rejection.
  • GVHD prevention/treatment in hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation.
  • Selected autoimmune disorders.

Major toxicities

SharedCyclosporine pearlsTacrolimus pearls
Nephrotoxicity, hypertension, neurotoxicity, hyperkalemia/metabolic abnormalities, infectionGingival hyperplasia + hirsutism are classic board associationsHyperglycemia/new-onset diabetes + tremor/neurotoxicity are especially emphasized on boards
⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - CYP3A

Both drugs are heavily affected by CYP3A interactions. Azoles, macrolides, some calcium-channel blockers, grapefruit, and other CYP3A inhibitors can raise levels and toxicity; strong inducers can reduce levels and risk rejection.

Need to memorize - calcineurin inhibitor toxicity

Kidney + pressure + nerves. Then remember: cyclosporine grows gums/hair; tacrolimus grows glucose.

Class 3 - Antiproliferative and Antimetabolic Agents

Overview

These drugs suppress activated lymphocytes by blocking pathways required for cell-cycle progression, nucleotide synthesis, or DNA integrity. Su groups sirolimus, mycophenolate, azathioprine, methotrexate, and cyclophosphamide together.

🔥 CLASS-WIDE PROFESSOR TOXICITY

Su emphasizes bone-marrow suppression and GI toxicity as recurring adverse effects across antiproliferative/antimetabolic drugs.

Sirolimus (rapamycin) / Everolimus

mTOR: intracellular kinase that promotes cell growth and cell-cycle progression in response to growth signals such as IL-2.
Cropped from lecture p. 18. Sirolimus binds FKBP but, unlike tacrolimus, the drug-FKBP complex inhibits mTOR, blocking lymphocyte cell-cycle progression.
Need to memorize - tacrolimus vs sirolimus

Both bind FKBP.
Tacrolimus -> calcineurin.
Sirolimus -> mTOR.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - SIROLIMUS TOXICITY

High-yield toxicities include hyperlipidemia, myelosuppression, impaired wound healing, and interstitial pneumonitis. A classic distinction is that sirolimus is not primarily nephrotoxic like calcineurin inhibitors, although combinations can worsen renal toxicity.

Mycophenolate mofetil

Cropped from lecture p. 19. Mycophenolate inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase, reducing de novo guanine nucleotide synthesis and limiting lymphocyte proliferation.
Need to memorize - mycophenolate

IMPDH inhibitor -> ↓ guanine synthesis -> ↓ B/T-cell proliferation.
Toxicity: GI upset + leukopenia/bone-marrow suppression; board pearl: teratogenic.

Azathioprine

Azathioprine: purine antimetabolite prodrug converted through 6-mercaptopurine pathways into thioguanine nucleotides that impair DNA synthesis and lymphocyte proliferation.
Need to memorize - azathioprine

Azathioprine -> 6-MP -> 6-thioguanine nucleotides -> impaired purine/DNA synthesis.
Major toxicities: myelosuppression + hepatotoxicity/GI effects.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - TPMT / NUDT15 / ALLOPURINOL

Low TPMT or NUDT15 activity increases thiopurine toxicity and myelosuppression. Because 6-MP is metabolized by xanthine oxidase, allopurinol/febuxostat can increase active thiopurine exposure unless the dose is reduced.

Methotrexate

Methotrexate: folate antagonist that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, reducing tetrahydrofolate needed for thymidylate and purine synthesis.
Need to memorize - methotrexate

DHFR inhibitor -> ↓ THF -> ↓ DNA synthesis.
Major board toxicities: myelosuppression, mucositis/GI toxicity, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, teratogenicity.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - LEUCOVORIN

Leucovorin (folinic acid) can rescue normal cells from high-dose methotrexate toxicity because it bypasses DHFR blockade.

Cyclophosphamide

Cyclophosphamide: alkylating prodrug activated by hepatic CYP enzymes; its metabolites crosslink DNA and kill rapidly proliferating immune cells.
Cropped from lecture p. 22. Hepatic activation generates the therapeutic alkylating metabolite and the toxic byproduct acrolein.
Need to memorize - cyclophosphamide

Alkylator -> DNA crosslinks.
Board toxicity: hemorrhagic cystitis from acrolein -> prevent with MESNA + hydration.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - OTHER CYCLOPHOSPHAMIDE TOXICITIES

Also know myelosuppression, infertility/gonadal toxicity, and increased bladder-cancer risk with cumulative exposure.

Class 4 - Polyclonal and Monoclonal Antibodies / Biologics

Antithymocyte globulin (ATG) / antilymphocyte globulin

Cropped from lecture p. 25. ATG is polyclonal and recognizes multiple T-cell surface antigens, producing lymphocyte depletion and functional blockade.
  • Derived from immunized horse or rabbit preparations.
  • Targets multiple T-cell antigens rather than one single receptor.
  • Used for induction immunosuppression and acute transplant rejection.
  • Side effects in Su's lecture: fever/chills, hypotension, antibodies against the animal globulin, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
Need to memorize - ATG

Polyclonal anti-T-cell antibodies -> rapid T-cell depletion. Think infusion reaction + cytopenias + serum-sickness/anti-animal-antibody potential.

Muromonab-CD3 (OKT3)

Need to memorize - OKT3

Murine anti-CD3 monoclonal antibody -> rapid T-cell inactivation/depletion.
Classic toxicities: cytokine release syndrome + HAMA (human anti-mouse antibody) response.

CURRENT-PRACTICE CONTEXT

Muromonab-CD3 is mainly a historical pharmacology/exam drug rather than a routine modern transplant agent. For this course, learn its target and classic cytokine-release/HAMA toxicities because Su explicitly teaches it.

TNF blockers

Cropped from lecture p. 30. Etanercept is a soluble TNF-receptor fusion protein that binds TNF before TNF reaches cell-surface receptors.

Anti-TNF therapy can also worsen demyelinating disease and advanced heart failure. These are common board-style contraindication clues.

What to Memorize vs Understand

Tier 1 - memorize cold

Four classes; cyclosporine vs tacrolimus vs sirolimus; mycophenolate/azathioprine/MTX/cyclophosphamide mechanisms; signature toxicities; ATG, OKT3, anti-TNF.

Tier 2 - understand deeply

Where each class interrupts the immune-response cascade and why suppressing lymphocyte proliferation increases infection/malignancy risk.

Tier 3 - board enrichment

Cyclophilin vs FKBP, CYP3A interactions, TPMT/NUDT15, allopurinol-thiopurine interaction, leucovorin vs MESNA, TB/HBV screening for TNF blockers.

Tier 4 - recognition level

Less-developed antibody names on Su's list such as basiliximab, rituximab, alemtuzumab, belimumab, and alefacept unless specifically emphasized in class.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Immunosuppressants suppress rejection and autoimmunity by interrupting immune-cell activation, cytokine transcription, lymphocyte proliferation, or specific cell/cytokine targets. Glucocorticoids broadly alter inflammatory gene transcription. Cyclosporine-cyclophilin and tacrolimus-FKBP inhibit calcineurin/NFAT and IL-2. Sirolimus-FKBP instead inhibits mTOR. Mycophenolate blocks IMPDH/guanine synthesis, azathioprine enters the 6-MP purine pathway, methotrexate blocks DHFR, and cyclophosphamide alkylates DNA. ATG depletes T cells polyclonally; OKT3 targets CD3; TNF blockers neutralize TNF signaling. For exam questions, mechanism alone is not enough: pair every drug with its signature toxicity.
