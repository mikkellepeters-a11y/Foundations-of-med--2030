Inflammation and Repair

Acute/chronic inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, systemic effects, regeneration, and fibrosis

Pathology 11High-density reviewSource-derived

If you remember nothing else

  • Inflammation delivers plasma proteins and leukocytes to injured tissue, removes the cause/dead cells, and transitions toward resolution or repair.
  • Acute inflammation is dominated by rapid vascular change and neutrophil recruitment.
  • Chronic inflammation combines macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells, ongoing injury, and repair.
  • Repair proceeds by regeneration when architecture and cell capacity permit, or fibrosis when they do not.

Extravasation sequence

  • Margination -> rolling -> firm adhesion -> diapedesis -> chemotaxis.
  • Treat the sequence as a pathway: each step has a distinct function and set of molecules.

Macrophage pivot

  • Macrophages help determine whether inflammation continues, becomes chronic, or resolves into healing.
  • The same inflammatory response can therefore connect tissue injury with later repair.

Systemic inflammation

  • Fever.
  • Acute-phase protein response.
  • Leukocytosis.
  • Recognize these as systemic consequences of inflammatory signaling rather than separate diseases.
