Foundation of Diagnostic Imaging

X-ray, CT, ultrasound, MRI, nuclear medicine, orientation, contrast, and modality selection

SALHigh-density reviewSource-derived

Modality framework

  • Compare radiography, CT, ultrasound, MRI, and nuclear medicine by acquisition method, strengths, limitations, radiation, risk, cost, and indications.
  • CT provides rapid cross-sectional anatomy; MRI emphasizes soft-tissue/CNS contrast when time and safety permit.
  • Ultrasound is portable, real-time, nonionizing, and useful for fluid, vessels, pregnancy, and bedside imaging.
  • Nuclear medicine detects signal from an administered tracer and emphasizes function/physiology.

Language to know

  • Radiography: lucency and opacity.
  • CT: density / attenuation.
  • MRI: signal intensity.
  • Use modality-appropriate language before interpreting pathology.

Orientation checklist

  • Identify the modality first.
  • Identify the plane or projection.
  • Confirm patient right and left.
  • Only then interpret the abnormality.

Contrast + selection

  • Iodine/barium are paired with x-ray/CT contexts; gadolinium with MRI; radiotracers with nuclear medicine.
  • Contrast is a patient-specific risk-benefit decision.
  • Choose the modality based on the clinical question rather than simply selecting the most detailed test.
