Immunosuppressants

Glucocorticoids, calcineurin inhibitors, antiproliferative drugs, biologics, and signature toxicities

Pharmacology 10High-density reviewSource-derived

If you remember nothing else

  • Learn each immunosuppressant by asking where it interrupts the immune response.
  • The central tradeoff is reduced rejection/autoimmunity at the cost of infection, malignancy risk, and drug-specific toxicity.
  • Mechanism + major clinical use + signature adverse effect is the lecture's exam-relevant triad.

Major classes

  • Glucocorticoids: broad transcriptional suppression of inflammation and lymphocyte activity.
  • Calcineurin inhibitors: block calcineurin/NFAT signaling -> decreased IL-2 transcription -> reduced T-cell activation.
  • Antiproliferative/antimetabolic drugs: interrupt mTOR, purine, folate, or DNA pathways needed for lymphocyte expansion.
  • Polyclonal/monoclonal biologics: target lymphocytes, cytokines, or surface receptors more selectively.

Calcineurin anchor

  • Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin.
  • Tacrolimus binds FKBP.
  • Both converge on calcineurin inhibition and reduced IL-2 transcription.

Exam frame

  • First identify the immune pathway being targeted.
  • Then distinguish drugs in the same broad class by their binding partner or signature toxicity.
  • Do not memorize the drugs as one flat list; group them by the interrupted signaling/proliferation step.
