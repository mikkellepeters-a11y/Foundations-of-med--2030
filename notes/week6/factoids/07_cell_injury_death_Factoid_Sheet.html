Cell Injury and Death

Reversible/irreversible injury, necrosis patterns, apoptosis, ROS, and reperfusion injury

Pathology 07High-density reviewSource-derived

If you remember nothing else

  • Cell injury progresses from reversible dysfunction to irreversible mitochondrial and membrane failure when stress is severe or prolonged.
  • Major mechanisms include ATP depletion, pump failure, calcium accumulation, reactive oxygen species, membrane injury, and organelle damage.
  • Reversible injury includes cellular/organelle swelling, ribosome detachment, hydropic change, and fatty change.
  • Irreversible injury features severe mitochondrial dysfunction, membrane damage, lysosomal leakage, and cell death.

Nuclear sequence

  • Pyknosis -> karyorrhexis -> karyolysis.
  • These nuclear changes are high-yield morphologic evidence of irreversible cell death.

Five necrosis patterns

  • Coagulative.
  • Liquefactive.
  • Caseous.
  • Fat.
  • Fibrinoid.

Board-oriented frame from the guide

  • Know the point-of-no-return concept separating reversible from irreversible injury.
  • Differentiate necrosis from apoptosis.
  • Reactive oxygen species and reperfusion injury are specifically highlighted as enrichment concepts.
  • Recognize classic vignette associations for each necrosis pattern.
