Antibody Function

Neutralization, opsonization, ADCC, complement, mast cells, Fc receptors, FcRn, and IgA transport

Core 06High-density reviewSource-derived

Five-function framework

  • Neutralization: antibody blocks a microbe or toxin from reaching its target.
  • Opsonization: antibody coats a target so phagocytes can capture it through Fc receptors.
  • ADCC: IgG-coated targets can recruit NK-cell cytotoxicity.
  • Complement activation: antibody recognition can drive opsonization, inflammation, immune-complex handling, and lysis.
  • IgE-mediated mast-cell sensitization allows rapid mediator release after later antigen exposure.

Transport facts

  • FcRn is emphasized for IgG transport/recycling and placental transfer.
  • Dimeric IgA is transported across epithelium to protect mucosal surfaces.
  • Antibody function depends heavily on the Fc region and isotype after antigen has been bound.

Exam frame

  • If the question asks what happens after binding, think Fc/isotype function rather than variable-region specificity.
  • Different Fc receptors recruit different effector cells and pathways.
  • Match the isotype to the location and job being tested.

Rapid distinctions

  • BCR/antibody binding is the recognition step.
  • Neutralization blocks interaction; opsonization improves phagocytosis; ADCC recruits cytotoxic effector cells.
  • Complement links adaptive recognition to innate effector mechanisms.
