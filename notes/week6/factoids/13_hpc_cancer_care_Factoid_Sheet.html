Holistic Patient Care in Cancer

Cancer-related distress, screening, interdisciplinary care, communication, and referral logic

HPCHigh-density reviewSource-derived

If you remember nothing else

  • A cancer diagnosis can affect physical function, cognition, identity, relationships, finances, future plans, emotional health, and sense of meaning.
  • Distress changes over time and can peak at predictable transition points in the cancer journey.
  • The companion frames distress screening as a routine '6th vital sign.'
  • Care should preserve patient autonomy while connecting the patient with the right level of support.

Whole-patient domains

  • Physical.
  • Emotional.
  • Social and relational.
  • Practical/financial.
  • Behavioral and spiritual concerns.

Match intervention to need

  • Psychoeducation and supportive counseling for appropriate lower-intensity needs.
  • CBT and structured mental-health interventions when indicated.
  • Specialty psycho-oncology/psychiatry for higher-acuity concerns.
  • Interdisciplinary practical support for nonpsychiatric barriers affecting cancer care.

Communication frame

  • Use Elicit-Provide-Elicit rather than reflexively imposing a referral.
  • Normalize discussion of distress.
  • Screen, clarify what matters to the patient, offer information/options, then check the patient's response and goals.
