Basics of Innate Immunity I

William K. Dolen, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 2 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

Lecture 1 gave you the map. This lecture starts filling in the innate side of the map: who the first responders are, how they detect danger, which cytokines coordinate the response, and how complement converts recognition into opsonization, inflammation, and direct killing.

  • Cells: know the major innate cells and what each one actually does.
  • PRRs: know the receptor families and the kind/location of danger they detect.
  • Cytokines: this is real memorization territory - especially IL-1, TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-12, CXCL8/IL-8, IL-10, and interferons.
  • Complement: understand the cascade as a network that converges on C3 and then produces three board-level outcomes: opsonization, inflammation/chemotaxis, and lysis.
  • Regulation matters: complement is powerful enough to damage self, so diseases such as PNH and hereditary angioedema are fundamentally diseases of failed regulation.
Need to memorize - master list
  1. 3 innate mechanisms: inflammation, cytotoxicity, phagocytosis.
  2. Major innate cells + one-line job: neutrophil, monocyte/macrophage, dendritic cell, NK cell/ILC, mast cell, basophil, eosinophil.
  3. NK pathway: type I IFNs + IL-12 activate; NK produces IFN-gamma/TNF-alpha; kills through perforin/granzymes or Fas-FasL.
  4. M1 vs M2 macrophage: M1 pro-inflammatory/pathogen defense; M2 anti-inflammatory/repair.
  5. PRR families: TLR, NLR, RLR, STING pathway, C-type lectin, scavenger; complement receptors are also important innate receptors.
  6. TLR4 = LPS. Endosomal TLRs 3/7/8/9 detect nucleic-acid danger in the companion handout.
  7. Endogenous pyrogens: IL-1, TNF-alpha, IL-6.
  8. IL-12 -> NK activation -> IFN-gamma.
  9. CXCL8/IL-8 -> neutrophil chemotaxis.
  10. IL-10 = major anti-inflammatory brake.
  11. Type I IFNs: IFN-alpha/beta = antiviral. Type II: IFN-gamma = macrophage activation.
  12. Complement functions: lysis, opsonization, binding, chemotaxis, activation, scavenging.
  13. Complement anchors: C3b = opsonization; C3a/C5a = inflammation; C5b-C9 = MAC/lysis.
  14. 3 activation pathways: alternative, lectin, classical -> converge on C3.
  15. Terminal complement deficiency -> recurrent Neisseria.
  16. PIGA/GPI -> CD55 + CD59 protection. Somatic PIGA loss -> PNH -> complement-mediated RBC lysis + thrombosis.
  17. C1 inhibitor deficiency -> hereditary angioedema -> bradykinin-mediated painless/nonpruritic swelling.

SUPPLEMENTAL HANDOUT / BOARD ANCHOR

The handout makes the "missing-self" clue explicit: many virus-infected or tumor cells have reduced MHC class I, removing an inhibitory signal to NK cells. That is why "low MHC I" is a classic NK clue.

⚠️ NK vs CD8 T CELL

Both can kill infected/tumor cells. NK = innate, no rearranged antigen-specific receptor, can respond to missing MHC I. CD8 = adaptive, antigen-specific TCR recognition of peptide on MHC I.

Macrophage: a large phagocytic tissue cell derived from the monocyte lineage that kills microbes, clears debris, produces cytokines, participates in repair, and can present antigen.

LO 2.2 - Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs)

🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Dolen says PRRs are "basically how the innate immune system works" and notes that this may go beyond a typical undergraduate immunology course. This is a concept to understand, not merely a receptor list to recite.

Cropped from lecture page 10. The useful idea is location: some danger is detected at the cell surface, some in endosomes, and some in the cytosol. Different PRR families occupy different compartments because pathogens expose different molecular patterns in different places.

  • TLR4 -> LPS from gram-negative bacteria.
  • Endosomal TLR3/7/8/9 -> nucleic-acid danger.
  • TLR9 -> unmethylated CpG DNA is specifically stated in the handout answer explanations.
⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - beyond the explicit source detail

TLR3 is classically associated with dsRNA; TLR7/8 with ssRNA; TLR9 with unmethylated CpG DNA. RIG-like receptors sense cytosolic viral RNA. NLRP3 can assemble an inflammasome that promotes IL-1-beta/IL-18 activation and pyroptosis. STING is best thought of as a signaling adaptor in cytosolic DNA sensing (classically downstream of cGAS) that promotes type I interferon responses.

⚠️ RECEPTOR LOCATION MATTERS

An extracellular bacterial wall component points you toward surface PRRs. Viral nucleic acid inside an endosome/cytosol points you toward endosomal or cytosolic sensors. Do not memorize receptor names without attaching them to a compartment and type of danger.

LO 2.3 - Cytokines: The Language of Innate Immunity

Cytokine: a messenger molecule produced by a cell that binds a specific receptor on or in a target cell and changes that cell's behavior. Cytokines operate in both innate and adaptive immunity.

Dolen says these are the molecules that "get the job done", will recur again and again, and are therapeutically targetable. This is one of the sections where memorization now will pay off repeatedly.

🩺 SARS-CoV-2 LECTURE CORRELATION

Dolen uses viral RNA as a PAMP that should engage TLRs, RLRs, NLRP3 and other sensors. The innate antiviral response then involves type I interferons, TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6, IL-18, and ILCs/NK cells.

Pyroptosis vs apoptosis

Apoptosis: programmed, relatively "quiet" cell death that tends to limit inflammatory spillover.
Pyroptosis: inflammatory programmed cell death associated with strong inflammatory signaling and release of intracellular contents.
PROFESSOR'S GREEK LESSON

Dolen breaks down apoptosis from Greek roots and then contrasts pyro- ("fire"/fever) with apo-. His practical takeaway: a cytopathic virus can promote inflammatory pyroptosis, whereas successful immune killing can often remove infected cells through quieter apoptosis.

LO 2.4 - The Complement System

Complement: a cascade of more than 30 soluble plasma proteins, largely made in the liver. It is officially categorized with innate immunity but interacts extensively with adaptive immunity and leukocytes.
🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Dolen explicitly says complement is both clinically important and a source of exam questions. This is the section to know cold.

C3 convertase: an enzyme complex generated by complement activation that cleaves C3 into C3a and C3b. Dolen calls C3 a pivotal component of the cascade.
SUPPLEMENTAL HANDOUT SHORTCUT

The handout summarizes the central board logic as: Classical = IgM/IgG-antigen complexes; Alternative = pathogen surfaces; MBL = mannose. It also explicitly calls C3 convertase the most important convergence step.

C3b: opsonization and leukocyte binding

Opsonization: coating a target so phagocytes recognize and ingest it more efficiently.
  • C3b can deposit directly on a pathogen surface.
  • Macrophage CR1 recognizes C3b.
  • Macrophage CR3 and CR4 recognize iC3b, a C3b metabolite.
  • The lecture also notes IgM and IgG can bind invading cells, connecting adaptive recognition with innate clearance.
Need to memorize - C3

C3 -> C3a + C3b. C3b is the major opsonization anchor. The companion handout ties C3 deficiency to poor clearance of encapsulated bacteria.

🩺 SUPPLEMENTAL VIGNETTE - C3 DEFICIENCY

Recurrent infections with encapsulated bacteria plus impaired "tagging" for phagocytosis should make you think C3/C3b opsonization failure.

MAC: terminal complement = hole punching

Membrane attack complex (MAC): terminal complement complex formed from C5b, C6, C7, C8, and C9 that forms membrane pores and causes lysis.
Need to memorize - MAC

C5b-C9 = membrane attack complex.
Terminal complement deficiency = recurrent Neisseria gonorrhoeae / N. meningitidis.

⚠️ COMPLEMENT DEFICIENCY PATTERN

C3 problem -> broad pyogenic/encapsulated infection susceptibility because opsonization is central.
C5-C9 problem -> Neisseria susceptibility because terminal membrane lysis is especially important.

🩺 TREATMENT CONNECTION

Dolen highlights eculizumab, a monoclonal antibody that binds C5 and prevents cleavage to C5a/C5b, thereby inhibiting the terminal pathway and improving anemia/thrombosis.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT

Because C5 blockade functionally suppresses terminal complement, patients receiving terminal-complement inhibitors have increased susceptibility to meningococcal infection. Also, clinical flow cytometry in PNH commonly demonstrates loss of GPI-linked proteins such as CD55/CD59.

⚠️ HAE IS NOT HISTAMINE-DRIVEN ALLERGY

If swelling is nonpruritic, recurrent, familial, and unresponsive to antihistamines, think bradykinin-mediated angioedema rather than mast-cell histamine. That distinction is exactly what the handout's vignette is testing.

Acquired angioedema

Dolen distinguishes acquired disease from hereditary disease. Causes include autoantibody to C1INH or excessive C1 activation in autoimmune disease/cancer that consumes C1INH faster than it can be replaced. A low C1q suggests acquired angioedema.

Need to memorize - hereditary vs acquired clue

Low C1q -> think acquired angioedema.

Common Exam Traps

TrapCorrect logic
"All lymphocytes are adaptive."No. NK cells are lymphocyte-like innate lymphoid cells without rearranged antigen receptors.
"NK killing means antigen-specific MHC I recognition."No. That is CD8 logic. NK cells can respond when inhibitory MHC I signals are reduced.
"All cytokines are pro-inflammatory."No. IL-10 is a major anti-inflammatory suppressor; TGF-beta is also mentioned as anti-inflammatory/repair associated.
"IL-8 is mainly a fever cytokine."No. CXCL8/IL-8 is a chemokine for neutrophil recruitment.
"Classical complement is the only route to C3."No. Classical, lectin, and alternative pathways all converge on central C3 cleavage.
"C3 deficiency and C8 deficiency cause the same infections."No. C3 affects central opsonization broadly; terminal-component defects especially predispose to Neisseria.
"PNH is an inherited germline PIGA disease."No. Dolen emphasizes somatic PIGA variants; germline variants are thought lethal.
"HAE is treated like histamine allergy because it causes swelling."No. HAE is bradykinin-driven and classically nonpruritic; antihistamines may not help.
"C1INH only regulates complement."No. Dolen emphasizes roles in complement, contact activation, and fibrinolysis.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Innate immunity uses specialized cells, germline-encoded danger sensors, cytokine networks, and complement to respond immediately to infection or injury. Neutrophils phagocytose, macrophages fight or repair, dendritic cells connect to adaptive immunity, and NK cells kill infected or malignant cells through cytokine activation and cytotoxic mechanisms. PRRs detect danger in extracellular, endosomal, and cytosolic compartments. IL-1/TNF-alpha/IL-6 drive fever and inflammation; IL-12 activates NK cells; CXCL8 recruits neutrophils; IL-10 suppresses inflammation; type I interferons create antiviral responses. Complement converges on C3, generating opsonization and inflammation, then terminal C5b-C9 can lyse targets. Because complement can injure self, regulation by proteins such as CD55/CD59 and C1 inhibitor is essential - and failures produce high-yield diseases such as PNH and hereditary angioedema.
