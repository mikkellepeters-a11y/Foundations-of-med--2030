B Cell and T Cell Activation

William K. Dolen, MD • Week 6: Foundations of Immunology • Lecture 9 • Enriched Study Guide

What this lecture is really trying to teach

This lecture finally makes the earlier receptor/cytokine material do something. The major theme is how adaptive immune cells meet in lymph nodes, receive the correct activation signals, differentiate into specialized effector cells, and generate memory without activating indiscriminately.

  • B-cell activation: BCR captures antigen -> B cell presents peptide on MHC II -> helper T-cell recognition + CD40L/CD40 + cytokines -> plasma cells, germinal centers, class switching, affinity maturation, memory.
  • T-independent B cells: strong repetitive antigen can activate B cells without T help, but the response is mostly IgM and poor memory/class switching.
  • Conjugate vaccines: attach a protein to a polysaccharide so a polysaccharide-specific B cell can recruit T-cell help.
  • CD8 activation: peptide-MHC I is not enough; B7-CD28 costimulation prevents inappropriate activation.
  • Helper subsets: Th1, Th2, Th17, Treg have characteristic cytokines and jobs.
  • ILCs: innate cells reproduce Th-like cytokine patterns without antigen-specific receptors.
  • Cytokine signaling: receptor assembly -> JAK -> STAT -> nucleus; pathway defects and drugs are clinically testable.

🔥 IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

The B-cell epitope and the T-cell epitope do not have to be the same molecular structure. The BCR binds the native surface epitope, then the B cell processes the entire antigen and presents a peptide from it to the helper T cell.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT - AID/Tfh

The companion handout frames the germinal center around T follicular helper (Tfh) cells. Standard board linkage: AID is required for both somatic hypermutation and class-switch recombination. This is enrichment beyond Dolen's slide wording, but it is a useful mechanistic anchor.

Memory B cells and the secondary response

Dolen emphasizes that memory B cells have high-affinity, class-switched receptors and respond rapidly on re-exposure by proliferating and differentiating into plasma cells. This is the mechanistic basis for the stronger/faster secondary antibody response.

Clinical correlation - Hyper-IgM syndrome

⭐ COMPANION-HANDOUT HIGH YIELD

The handout adds the classic X-linked mechanism: CD40L deficiency on T cells prevents effective T-cell help for B-cell class switching. Expect high or normal IgM with low IgG, IgA, and IgE and recurrent infections.

LO 8.2 - T-Independent Activation and Conjugate Vaccines

T-independent antigen: an antigen capable of activating B cells without helper-T-cell participation, often because a highly repetitive/polyvalent structure strongly cross-links BCRs and can engage innate receptors such as TLRs.
Need to memorize - T-independent response

Fast • mostly IgM • little/no class switching • little/no affinity maturation • poor memory.

🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Dolen explicitly connects the lack of class switching to the absence of CD40L-CD40 signaling.

Why polysaccharides are a problem

Many bacterial capsules are polysaccharides. A BCR can recognize and internalize a polysaccharide, but after digestion there is no peptide from that polysaccharide to load onto MHC II for helper-T-cell recognition. The response therefore tends to remain T-independent.

How a conjugate vaccine “tricks” the system

Cropped from lecture p. 14. The B cell recognizes the bacterial polysaccharide, internalizes the polysaccharide-protein conjugate, and presents carrier-protein peptides to helper T cells. T-cell help upgrades the anti-polysaccharide response.

LO 8.3 - Cytotoxic T-Lymphocyte Activation and Function

Cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL): activated CD8 T cell that recognizes specific peptide-MHC I complexes and induces apoptosis in infected or abnormal target cells.

Two-signal activation

Cropped from lecture p. 17. Signal 1 is peptide-MHC I/TCR with CD8 stabilization. Signal 2 is B7 on the APC binding CD28 on the T cell.
Need to memorize - naive T-cell activation

Signal 1 = TCR + peptide-MHC.
Signal 2 = B7 (CD80/86) on APC + CD28 on T cell.
Signal 1 without signal 2 -> anergy.

COMPANION-HANDOUT “THREE-SIGNAL” MODEL

The handout adds a useful third concept for helper-T-cell differentiation: signal 3 = cytokines from the APC/environment, which push the activated T cell toward a specific lineage such as Th1.

Anergy: a state of functional nonresponsiveness after antigen recognition occurs without adequate costimulation.

CTL effector mechanisms

  • Perforin + granzyme B: induces apoptosis of the target cell.
  • Companion-handout addition: Fas-FasL can also trigger apoptotic death.
  • IFN-gamma release: inhibits viral replication, promotes antigen processing/MHC I expression, and activates macrophages to clear dying cells.
  • CTLs can detach and move on to another target: Dolen calls them trained “serial killers.”
PROFESSOR MEMORY HOOK

CD8 CTLs are antigen-specific “serial killers.” They do not explode themselves with each kill; they trigger target apoptosis and continue searching.

Calcineurin - the signaling node Dolen says is high yield

Cropped from lecture p. 19. The fine-detail pathway is labeled advanced, but the Ca2+/calmodulin -> calcineurin -> NFAT branch is clinically important because calcineurin inhibitors suppress T-cell activation.
Need to memorize - calcineurin

Calcineurin activates NFAT-dependent transcription in T cells. Dolen specifically names tacrolimus as a widely used calcineurin inhibitor.

⭐ BOARD ENRICHMENT

Standard board pairing: tacrolimus binds FKBP and cyclosporine binds cyclophilin; both ultimately inhibit calcineurin and reduce IL-2 transcription/T-cell activation.

Clinical correlation - Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome

Need to memorize - Wiskott-Aldrich

X-linked • WAS cytoskeletal/actin defect • microthrombocytopenia/small platelets • eczema • recurrent infections.

🔥 “HIGH YIELD - DO NOT MISS”

Dolen explicitly labels Wiskott-Aldrich a high-yield, do-not-miss diagnosis and emphasizes that microthrombocytopenia is characteristic of WAS-spectrum disease.

Th1

Dolen emphasizes APC IL-12 -> Th1 -> IFN-gamma, with activation of macrophages and cytotoxic T cells. This is the intracellular-pathogen/cell-mediated arm.

Th2

Th2 cells center on IL-4 and produce IL-4/IL-5/IL-13. Think B-cell help/IgE, eosinophils, helminths, and allergy.

Th17

Th17 cells produce IL-17 and IL-22 and cooperate with neutrophils/innate immunity, especially against fungi and extracellular bacteria. Dolen also links this pathway to multiple autoimmune diseases.

Treg

Treg cells restrain CD4/CD8 activity and preserve self-tolerance. Dolen specifically notes activation by TGF-beta; the companion handout adds the classic FoxP3 / IL-10 / IL-2 framework.

⭐ COMPANION-HANDOUT BOARD ADD-ON - IPEX

FoxP3 deficiency -> IPEX: Immune dysregulation, Polyendocrinopathy, Enteropathy, X-linked. Classic infant pattern: severe diarrhea, endocrine autoimmunity (often type 1 diabetes), eczema.

Cytokine environment and class switching

PROFESSOR-SIMPLIFIED ASSOCIATION

Dolen teaches the useful introductory rule: Th1-type cytokine environment favors IgG, whereas Th2-type cytokine environment favors IgE. Real human subclass switching is more detailed, but this is the lecture-level association to know.

LO 8.5 - Innate Lymphoid Cells Mirror Th Cytokine Programs

Innate lymphoid cell (ILC): an innate immune cell that lacks rearranged antigen-specific receptors but can rapidly produce cytokine programs analogous to helper-T-cell subsets.

Common gamma-chain deficiency / X-linked SCID

Need to memorize - X-linked SCID

Common gamma-chain mutation -> IL-2/4/7/9/15 receptor signaling fails -> T- B+ NK- -> X-linked recessive SCID.

WHY T- B+ NK-?

The companion handout spells out the useful mechanism: IL-7 signaling is critical for T-cell development and IL-15 for NK-cell development. B-cell numbers can remain present, but function is impaired because effective T-cell help is missing.

PROFESSOR HISTORICAL BIT

Dolen ties common-gamma-chain SCID to David Vetter (“the bubble boy”) and notes the case's role in the early history of medical-ethics committees.

Companion-Handout Board Add-Ons Not Central in the Current Slide Deck

⭐ CHECKPOINT INHIBITORS

The handout adds CTLA-4 and PD-1 as inhibitory T-cell checkpoints. Tumors can exploit these “brakes”; antibodies such as anti-CTLA-4 or anti-PD-1 release inhibition but can cause autoimmune-like adverse effects such as colitis/hepatitis. This is valuable board material, but it is not developed in the current Dolen slide deck.

⭐ LEPROSY AS A Th1 vs Th2 DEMONSTRATION

The handout uses tuberculoid leprosy as a strong Th1/IFN-gamma/macrophage response with low bacterial burden and lepromatous leprosy as Th2-skewed with poor macrophage control and high bacterial burden. Treat this as companion-only enrichment.

30-second end-of-lecture summary

Adaptive activation is a choreography of location plus signaling. B and T cells circulate through lymph nodes, where antigen-specific B cells internalize native antigen and present processed peptide on MHC II to helper T cells. CD40L-CD40 plus cytokines then drive proliferation, germinal-center class switching/affinity maturation, plasma cells, and memory. Repetitive polysaccharides can bypass T help but mainly generate IgM; conjugate vaccines restore T-cell help by attaching a protein carrier. Naive T cells require peptide-MHC plus B7-CD28 costimulation or they become anergic. CD8 CTLs kill via apoptosis, while CD4 cells differentiate into Th1, Th2, Th17, and Treg programs that are mirrored by ILC1/2/3 cytokine patterns. Cytokines signal through surface receptors and JAK-STAT pathways; defects such as STAT3 hyper-IgE syndrome and common-gamma-chain SCID make this signaling clinically testable.
