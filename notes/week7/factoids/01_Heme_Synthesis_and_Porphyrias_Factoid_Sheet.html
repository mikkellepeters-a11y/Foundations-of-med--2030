Regulation - ALAS1 vs ALAS2

ALAS1 (hepatic)

  • Heme negative feedback
  • Fasting/glucagon -> PGC-1alpha -> ↑ ALAS1
  • CYP450-inducing drugs/hormones -> ↑ ALAS1
  • Insulin/carbohydrate suppress fasting drive

ALAS2 (erythroid)

  • EPO -> JAK/STAT5 -> ↑ transcription
  • Iron/IRE controls translation
  • Low iron -> IRP blocks ALAS2 translation
  • Heme production coordinated with globin
LO 4 mechanism: Glucose reduces fasting-driven hepatic ALAS1. Hemin restores heme feedback inhibition of ALAS1. Both reduce ALA/PBG production upstream of an AIP block.

Vignette triggers

Severe abdominal pain + confusion/psych + neuropathy after fasting or phenobarbital
-> AIP. Think ↑ urine PBG; treat by suppressing ALAS1.
Adult with fragile blistering dorsal hands + scarring/hypertrichosis
-> PCT. Think UROD + urine uroporphyrin + HCV/iron/alcohol associations.
Child burns/swells within minutes of sun, no blisters
-> EPP. Think ferrochelatase + RBC protoporphyrin + hepatobiliary risk.
Abdominal/neuro symptoms + anemia after lead exposure
-> ALAD + ferrochelatase inhibition; ↑ ALA and Zn-protoporphyrin.

Professor-specific details worth knowing

  • PCT sunscreen nuance: porphyrins absorb visible light, so ordinary UV-only sunscreen may be inadequate; faculty handout favors opaque physical blockers/protective clothing.
  • EPP urine trap: protoporphyrin IX is water-insoluble -> bile/feces, not primarily urine.
  • EPP liver: gallstones/cholestasis; rust-brown hepatic deposits; Maltese-cross birefringence in severe hepatic disease.
  • Lead vs EPP: both involve ferrochelatase, but zinc protoporphyrin is less phototoxic, so lead does not mimic classic EPP photosensitivity.
  • Porphyrinogen vs porphyrin: porphyrinogens are colorless; oxidation generates colored porphyrins.

Board enrichment / common add-ons

Lead: basophilic stippling, ring sideroblasts, wrist/foot drop may appear in board-style stems. ALAS: requires B6, linking B6 deficiency/isoniazid to impaired heme synthesis and sideroblastic anemia.
