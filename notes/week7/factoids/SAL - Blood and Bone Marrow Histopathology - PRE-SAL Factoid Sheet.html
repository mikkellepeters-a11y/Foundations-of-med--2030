Platelets

Tiny anucleate purple fragments from megakaryocytes. Alpha granules = vWF, fibrinogen, PDGF. Dense granules = ADP, Ca2+, serotonin. Primary-hemostasis cross-link: vWF-GpIb -> activation -> GpIIb/IIIa-fibrinogen aggregation.

Maturation sequences

Erythroid
Proerythroblast -> basophilic -> polychromatophilic -> orthochromatic -> retic -> RBC.
Trend: smaller; blue -> pink; nucleus condenses/extrudes.
Neutrophil
Myeloblast -> promyelocyte -> myelocyte -> metamyelocyte -> band -> segmented.
Trend: granules appear; nucleus indents then segments.

Practical traps

  • Reticulocyte ≠ nucleated RBC: retic is anucleate with residual RNA/polychromasia.
  • Band vs monocyte: band = neutrophilic U-shaped nucleus; monocyte = larger folded/kidney nucleus + gray-blue cytoplasm.
  • T/B/NK cannot be separated reliably by routine small-lymphocyte morphology.
  • Blasts or nucleated RBCs in peripheral blood are not normal mature-blood findings and should make you stop and reassess.
Immediate marrow giveaway: giant megakaryocyte + many nucleated precursor stages.
