Nucleotide Metabolism

Puttur D. Prasad • Tuesday Lecture 2 • Week 5: Foundations of Cancer Biology

🔥 Professor-emphasis map

The lecture uses bold, blue/purple/red text, “important concept,” “take home message,” and explicit Step-exam comments. Those points are deliberately elevated below.

  • Know nucleobase vs nucleoside vs nucleotide. The professor states this is crucial for understanding the pathway.
  • PRPP is shared by purine and pyrimidine synthesis and stimulates nucleotide synthesis; increased PRPP drives increased synthesis.
  • ~70-90% of human nucleotides come from salvage in the lecture; salvage therefore strongly controls PRPP availability.
  • Glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase is the major regulatory enzyme of de novo purine synthesis: inhibited by IMP/GMP/AMP and stimulated by PRPP.
  • Purines degrade to URIC ACID, not urea. This distinction is explicitly emphasized.
  • Gout: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent monosodium urate crystals; acute treatment begins with anti-inflammatory therapy, not urate-lowering therapy.
  • Tumor lysis syndrome: ↑ uric acid, ↑ K+, ↑ phosphate, ↓ Ca2+.
  • Lesch-Nyhan: HGPRT deficiency → failed salvage, ↑ PRPP, ↑ de novo purine synthesis, severe hyperuricemia + neurologic/self-injurious behavior.
  • ADA deficiency: ↑ dATP inhibits ribonucleotide reductase → deficient/imbalanced dNTP pools → SCID.
  • Orotic aciduria has two major contexts: UMP synthase deficiency vs urea-cycle dysfunction; the professor explicitly calls this a major take-home distinction.
  • Ribonucleotide reductase: uses NDP substrates, is inhibited by dATP, and is inhibited pharmacologically by hydroxyurea.
  • Drug mechanisms: 6-MP → thio-IMP; 5-FU → 5-FdUMP → thymidylate synthase inhibition; methotrexate → DHFR inhibition.

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the significance of de novo and salvage pathways in purine nucleotide synthesis; describe purine synthesis, regulation, degradation, and the clinical manifestations of HGPRT and ADA deficiencies.
  2. Describe pyrimidine nucleotide synthesis and explain the connection between pyrimidine synthesis and urea-cycle disorders in the etiology of orotic aciduria.
  3. Describe the ribonucleotide reductase reaction and synthesis of thymidine nucleotides.
  4. Apply nucleotide-metabolism principles to the mechanisms of action of 6-mercaptopurine, 5-fluorouracil, and methotrexate.

Foundations

Nucleobase vs nucleoside vs nucleotide

CategoryWhat it containsExamples from lecture
NucleobaseNitrogenous base onlyGuanine, adenine, hypoxanthine, uracil, cytosine, thymine
NucleosideBase + sugarGuanosine, adenosine, inosine, uridine, cytidine, thymidine
NucleotideBase + sugar + phosphate(s)GMP, AMP, IMP, UMP, CMP, TMP
🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

The lecture explicitly says being able to differentiate these three categories is crucial.

PRPP: the shared activated ribose donor

Ribose-5-phosphate + ATP → PRPP + AMP
  • Enzyme: PRPP synthetase.
  • PRPP is needed for both purine and pyrimidine nucleotide synthesis.
  • PRPP synthetase is feedback inhibited mainly by purine/pyrimidine nucleotides, especially ADP and GDP in the lecture.
  • ↑ intracellular phosphate and ↑ glucose-6-phosphate can increase PRPP synthesis.
  • PRPP allosterically stimulates nucleotide synthesis, so ↑ PRPP → ↑ nucleotide production.
Lecture p. 4 - PRPP is the common precursor and a major feed-forward signal for nucleotide synthesis.
⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - PRPP overproduction

Increased PRPP can promote purine overproduction and hyperuricemia. This is important in HGPRT deficiency and can also occur with PRPP synthetase overactivity.

LO1 - Purine Nucleotide Synthesis, Salvage, and Degradation

Core contrast: de novo synthesis builds purines from simple precursors; salvage recycles preformed purine bases. In the lecture, salvage accounts for most nucleotide production and suppresses unnecessary de novo synthesis by consuming PRPP.
Lecture p. 5 - integrated map of de novo synthesis, salvage, and degradation to uric acid.

De novo purine synthesis

  • Occurs mainly in the cytosol; liver has the highest capacity.
  • The purine ring is built directly on ribose of PRPP.
  • The pathway produces IMP first, which is then used to generate AMP and GMP.
  • De novo purine synthesis requires folate coenzymes; N10-formyl-THF contributes carbon atoms to the purine ring.

Rate-limiting / major regulatory step

PRPP + glutamine → 5-phosphoribosylamine
via glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase
  • Feedback inhibited by IMP, GMP, and AMP.
  • Feed-forward activated by PRPP.
  • Folate deficiency therefore impairs nucleotide production and contributes to megaloblastic anemia.
⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Purine branch balancing

IMP is the branch-point precursor for AMP and GMP. A classic board concept is reciprocal energy use: AMP synthesis uses GTP, whereas GMP synthesis uses ATP, helping balance purine pools.

Purine salvage

EnzymeReactionImportance
HGPRTHypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP
Guanine + PRPP → GMP		Major clinically important purine-salvage enzyme.
APRTAdenine + PRPP → AMPSalvages adenine.

The lecture emphasizes that HGPRT has a high affinity for PRPP. When hypoxanthine/guanine are available, salvage consumes PRPP and suppresses de novo synthesis. If salvage fails, PRPP accumulates and drives de novo synthesis.

🔥 PROFESSOR EMPHASIS

Defect in salvage → ↑ PRPP → ↑ de novo purine synthesis → more purines available for degradation → even more uric acid.

Purine Degradation, Gout, and Tumor Lysis Syndrome

🔥 VERY IMPORTANT

The end product of human purine degradation is URIC ACID - NOT UREA.

Purine degradation

  • Hypoxanthine → xanthine → uric acid via xanthine oxidase/dehydrogenase.
  • Guanine is converted to xanthine and then to uric acid.
  • Uric acid is excreted through the kidney, but much filtered urate is reabsorbed.
  • Lactate and some organic acids/diuretics can compete with urate handling and contribute to hyperuricemia.
Lecture p. 7 - the degradation pathway connects excess purines to uric acid and gout.

Gout

Excess urate can precipitate as needle-shaped monosodium urate crystals that are negatively birefringent under polarized light.

⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Acute vs chronic gout treatment

Acute gout: treat inflammation (commonly NSAIDs, colchicine, or glucocorticoids depending on the patient).
Chronic urate lowering / prevention: xanthine oxidase inhibitors such as allopurinol or febuxostat can reduce new uric acid production. Do not confuse acute anti-inflammatory treatment with long-term urate lowering.

Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS)

Rapid destruction of highly proliferative, chemosensitive tumors releases nucleic acids and intracellular electrolytes. The purine load is degraded to uric acid.

FindingDirectionWhy

Lecture p. 9 - the professor emphasizes ↑ PO4, ↑ K, ↑ uric acid, and ↓ calcium.
⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - TLS prevention/treatment

Allopurinol prevents formation of new uric acid by inhibiting xanthine oxidase. Rasburicase directly converts existing uric acid to a more soluble metabolite and is especially useful for high-risk or established severe TLS. Aggressive IV hydration is central.

Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome - HGPRT Deficiency

Near-complete HGPRT deficiency causes failure of hypoxanthine/guanine salvage.

Lecture p. 12 - HGPRT deficiency simultaneously increases unsalvaged purines and raises PRPP-driven de novo synthesis.

Why uric acid rises

  1. Hypoxanthine and guanine cannot be salvaged → they are degraded to uric acid.
  2. PRPP is not consumed by salvage → PRPP rises.
  3. Low IMP/GMP/AMP removes feedback inhibition of de novo synthesis.
  4. High PRPP stimulates glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase → more purines are made → more uric acid is produced.

Clinical pattern

  • X-linked recessive.
  • Hyperuricemia, gout, nephrolithiasis, and renal injury.
  • Severe neurologic/behavioral abnormalities.
  • Classic self-injurious behavior, especially biting lips/fingers.
⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Treatment limitation

Allopurinol lowers uric acid and helps gout/nephrolithiasis risk, but it does not correct the neurologic or behavioral manifestations of Lesch-Nyhan syndrome.

ADA Deficiency and SCID

Lecture p. 14 - ADA/PNP defects are shown within purine degradation; ADA deficiency causes an autosomal-recessive form of SCID.

Mechanism

  • ADA normally participates in purine degradation, including deamination of deoxyadenosine.
  • ADA deficiency → accumulation of adenosine/deoxyadenosine metabolites → ↑ dATP.
  • dATP strongly inhibits ribonucleotide reductase.
  • Deoxyribonucleotide production becomes deficient/imbalanced → impaired DNA synthesis, especially harmful to lymphocytes.
⚠️ COMMONLY CONFUSED

ADA-deficient SCID = autosomal recessive. The lecture separately emphasizes that the most common genetic cause of SCID overall is an X-linked IL-2 receptor common gamma-chain defect.

LO2 - De Novo Pyrimidine Synthesis and Orotic Aciduria

Key contrast with purines: the pyrimidine ring is built first, then attached to PRPP. Purines are built directly on PRPP.

Pathway architecture

  • CPS II begins pyrimidine synthesis in the cytosol using glutamine + bicarbonate.
  • The first three activities form the CAD complex: CPS II, aspartate transcarbamoylase, dihydroorotase.
  • Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase is mitochondrial.
  • Orotate is attached to PRPP and converted to OMP.
  • UMP synthase contains orotate phosphoribosyltransferase + OMP decarboxylase activities.
  • UMP ultimately supplies pyrimidines used to make cytidine and thymidine nucleotides.

Hereditary orotic aciduria - UMP synthase deficiency

  • Orotic acid accumulates because UMP cannot be formed efficiently.
  • Megaloblastic anemia and growth impairment occur from pyrimidine deficiency.
  • No hyperammonemia from the pyrimidine defect itself.
  • Treat with uridine, which bypasses the block and restores pyrimidine nucleotide pools.

Orotic aciduria from urea-cycle disorders

If the urea cycle is blocked downstream of carbamoyl phosphate formation, excess mitochondrial carbamoyl phosphate can spill into cytosolic pyrimidine synthesis and increase orotic acid.

Lecture p. 18 - side-by-side distinction: UMP synthase defect → megaloblastic anemia; urea-cycle dysfunction → hyperammonemia + decreased BUN.
🔥 IMPORTANT TAKE-HOME MESSAGE

Orotic aciduria is not synonymous with a urea-cycle disorder.
- UMP synthase deficiency: orotic aciduria + megaloblastic anemia, no hyperammonemia.
- Urea-cycle block with carbamoyl-phosphate spillover: orotic aciduria + hyperammonemia + low BUN.

⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - OTC deficiency

The classic urea-cycle disorder with high orotic acid is ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency (X-linked). In contrast, CPS I deficiency causes hyperammonemia with low/normal orotic acid because carbamoyl phosphate is not produced in excess.

LO3 - Ribonucleotide Reductase and Thymidine Nucleotide Synthesis

Ribonucleotide reductase

NDP → dNDP via ribonucleotide reductase
  • Physiologic substrates are nucleoside diphosphates: ADP, GDP, CDP, UDP.
  • The enzyme supplies deoxyribonucleotides required for DNA synthesis.
  • ATP activates its activity site; dATP inhibits it.
  • A separate specificity site helps balance the relative dNTP pool; the professor explicitly says not to memorize individual specificity pairings.
Lecture p. 19 - professor summary: NDP substrates, hydroxyurea inhibition, and dATP-mediated inhibition in ADA deficiency.
⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Hydroxyurea

In cancer therapy, hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and lowers deoxyribonucleotide supply. In sickle cell disease its major therapeutic benefit is different: it increases HbF.

dTMP synthesis

dUMP + N5,N10-methylene-THF → dTMP
via thymidylate synthase

Thymidylate synthase uses methylene-THF as the one-carbon donor/reducing cofactor, converting it to DHF. THF must then be regenerated by dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR).

⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Folate cycle connection

This creates the classic pharmacologic chain:
Methotrexate inhibits DHFR → ↓ THF derivatives → ↓ dTMP and ↓ purine synthesis.
5-FU inhibits thymidylate synthase → ↓ dTMP directly.

⚠️ COMMONLY CONFUSED - 6-MP + allopurinol

Allopurinol inhibits xanthine oxidase, so it reduces 6-MP degradation. Therefore the 6-MP/azathioprine dose generally must be reduced when combined with strong xanthine oxidase inhibition to avoid severe toxicity.

⭐ STEP 1 HIGH YIELD - Leucovorin connections

Leucovorin (folinic acid) can rescue normal cells from high-dose methotrexate because it bypasses DHFR. In contrast, leucovorin can enhance 5-FU activity by stabilizing the inhibitory complex between 5-FdUMP and thymidylate synthase.

Why rapidly dividing normal tissues are injured

These drugs preferentially injure cells with high nucleotide demand, so toxicities commonly involve bone marrow, GI/oral mucosa, and hair follicles in addition to malignant cells.
