Overview: Official Week 7 Learning Objectives

LO 1. Define lymphoma and compare with leukemia.
LO 2. Discuss the methods and ancillary studies used in diagnosis of lymphoid neoplasms, including IHC, flow cytometry, and FISH.
LO 3. Discuss the important clinicopathologic and genetic features of acute lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma, including B-ALL/LBL and T-ALL/LBL.
LO 4. Discuss the major subtypes of Hodgkin lymphoma, including clinical presentation, morphology, immunophenotype, treatment, and clinical course: classic HL and NLPHL.
LO 5. Discuss the important clinicopathologic and genetic features of common NHL subtypes: CLL/SLL, hairy cell leukemia, follicular lymphoma, MALT lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, DLBCL, Burkitt lymphoma, and mycosis fungoides/Sézary syndrome.
LO 6. Discuss plasma-cell neoplasms, including MGUS, smoldering myeloma, plasma-cell myeloma, and primary amyloidosis (AL).

The lecture's master strategy
Do not memorize lymphomas as a random alphabet soup. For every neoplasm, ask:
1) What cell/stage is malignant? 2) Where does it present? 3) What does it look like? 4) What markers does it express? 5) What genetic lesion drives it? 6) Is it indolent or aggressive?

Key conceptual pointThe terms can describe presentation of the same biologic entity. CLL/SLL and ALL/LBL are the classic examples.
Need to memorize
CLL and SLL = same malignant B cell, different dominant compartment.

Lecture slide 7 - full slide retained. The deck first separates immature precursor B/T neoplasms from mature lymphoid disease, then mature disease into Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin branches.
Why developmental stage makes the rest easier

Markers and behavior reflect what the normal counterpart should be doing. TdT/CD34 point toward immaturity. Mature B-cell neoplasms carry B-cell markers and may retain stage-specific programs such as germinal-center CD10 expression or mantle-zone cyclin D1 biology.

Why this section is here

The official Week 7 objectives explicitly require IHC, flow cytometry, and FISH. The current deck demonstrates many immunostains and cytogenetic lesions but does not give a single diagnostic-toolbox slide. The uploaded companion supplies the framework below.

ToolWhat it answersBest use
MorphologyWhat do the cells and tissue architecture look like?Blast vs mature cell; follicular vs diffuse growth; RS cells; starry sky; plasma-cell morphology.
IHCWhere are proteins expressed in intact tissue?Preserves architecture; e.g., CD20, CD3, CD15/CD30, cyclin D1, BCL2.
Flow cytometryWhat markers are co-expressed on individual suspended cells?Rapid immunophenotyping and clonality; especially blood, marrow, and fresh tissue.
FISH / cytogeneticsWhat rearrangement or copy-number lesion is present?t(14;18), t(11;14), MYC rearrangement, BCR-ABL1, etc.
Clinical contextWho has the disease and where is it?Age, site, tempo, B symptoms, marrow/blood involvement, extranodal pattern.
Clonality idea
A reactive B-cell population is polyclonal; a neoplastic population is often light-chain restricted (predominantly kappa or lambda), supporting derivation from one clone.
Exam trap

No single marker diagnoses most lymphomas. CD5, for example, is normally a T-cell marker but is aberrantly expressed in both CLL/SLL and mantle-cell lymphoma. You need a pattern, not one stain.

Slides 9-16Acute lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphomaLO 3

Slides 9-10: same precursor disease, different dominant presentation

  • ALL/LBL arises from immature B or T lymphoid precursors.
  • It is predominantly a disease of childhood; Savage gives a peak around 3-7 years.
  • B-ALL/LBL is more common and more often presents with marrow/blood disease.
  • T-ALL/LBL more often presents as a tissue mass, especially a mediastinal/thymic mass in adolescent males.

Slides 11-12: marrow failure + blast phenotype

  • Marrow replacement -> anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia/infections, bone pain.
  • B-lymphoblasts: small-medium blasts with scant cytoplasm and no Auer rods.
  • Slide phenotype: CD19+, CD20 +/- , CD10+, TdT+, CD34+.
Lecture slide 13 - full slide retained. Use the full-slide smear to recognize a blast-rich picture: large nuclei, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, scant basophilic cytoplasm, and immature chromatin.
Lecture slide 14 - full slide retained. The marrow is replaced by lymphoblasts, explaining cytopenias and bone pain.
Need to memorize - immaturity
TdT + CD34 = precursor/blast program.
B-ALL: CD19/CD10. T-ALL: T-lineage markers such as CD3/CD7 plus TdT.

Slides 15-16: prognosis and B vs T

  • Favorable in the deck: age 2-10, lower leukocyte count, t(12;21), rapid initial response.
  • Unfavorable in the deck: Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22), meningeal/testicular involvement.
  • T-ALL/LBL: adolescent male, mediastinal mass, CD2/CD3/CD7/TdT.
Board enrichment - sanctuary sites

CNS and testes are classic sanctuary sites in ALL, which is why CNS-directed prophylaxis is built into treatment strategies. This comes from the uploaded companion rather than the current 70-slide deck.

Slides 17-29Hodgkin lymphoma: Reed-Sternberg biology, phenotype, spread, stagingLO 4

Slides 19-21: what makes Hodgkin lymphoma clinically distinctive

  • Primarily lymph-node based.
  • Usually presents above the diaphragm.
  • Classically spreads in a predictable contiguous fashion through nodal groups.
  • B symptoms: fever, night sweats/chills, weight loss; pruritus may occur.
  • Diagnosis requires tissue biopsy.

Slides 22-24: the malignant cell is rare but memorable

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma is a clonal B-cell neoplasm in which the malignant Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg cells are surrounded by a large reactive inflammatory background.

Lecture slide 23 - full slide retained. Classic RS morphology: very large cell with two nuclear lobes/nuclei and prominent eosinophilic nucleoli with perinuclear clearing - the classic 'owl-eye' concept.
Classic Hodgkin phenotype
CD15+ • CD30+ • CD45- • usually CD20- / weak B-cell program.
Lecture slide 25 - full slide retained. Nodular sclerosis shows broad collagen bands dividing the node and characteristic lacunar variants of RS cells; it commonly presents with a mediastinal mass.
Lecture slide 28 - full slide retained. The immunostain panel visually reinforces CD15/CD30 positivity and weak/absent conventional leukocyte/B-cell-marker expression.
Hodgkin vs NHL spread

Hodgkin: more orderly contiguous nodal spread. NHL: often multicentric/disseminated at diagnosis with extranodal disease more common.

Lecture slide 29 - full slide retained. Stage I through IV is based on distribution; the slide also labels A vs B symptoms, E for extranodal extension, and X for bulky disease.
Official LO gap-fill - NLPHL

The deck explicitly says nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma will not be discussed in detail, but the official LO requires it. Recognition-level companion facts:

  • Characteristic LP / “popcorn” cells.
  • CD20+ and CD45+, unlike classic HL.
  • CD15- and CD30-, essentially the opposite of classic RS immunophenotype.
  • Typically more indolent and can have late recurrences.

Slides 30-55Mature B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomasLO 5

Slides 31-34: behavior matters as much as name

  • NHLs are a heterogeneous group of mature B, T, or NK-cell neoplasms.
  • They are often multicentric and disseminated at diagnosis and can arise extranodally.
  • Indolent diseases may be widespread but slow; aggressive lymphomas grow fast but can be more treatment-responsive/curable.
  • The deck's B-cell split: lower-grade CLL/SLL, follicular, MALT; mantle-cell “in a class by itself”; high-grade DLBCL and Burkitt.

CLL / SLL

Slides 35-38

  • Same underlying B-cell neoplasm; CLL = blood/marrow dominant, SLL = node/tissue dominant.
  • Median age around 70; often indolent.
  • Can cause lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, B symptoms, autoimmune hemolytic anemia/ITP, and hypogammaglobulinemia.
  • Immunophenotype: CD19+, dim CD20+, aberrant CD5+, CD23+.
Lecture slide 38 - full slide retained. The slide combines the classic peripheral-blood pattern of small mature lymphocytes and a smudge cell with marrow involvement.
CLL vs mantle cell
Both can be CD5+ B-cell neoplasms.
CLL/SLL = CD23+. Mantle = CD23- + cyclin D1+.
Companion enrichment - Richter transformation

A previously indolent CLL/SLL patient who suddenly develops a rapidly enlarging node/mass, rising LDH, or new systemic symptoms should raise concern for transformation to an aggressive lymphoma, classically DLBCL.

Hairy Cell Leukemia - official-LO gap-fill

Not developed in the current 70-slide deck

The official LO lists HCL, so the uploaded companion supplies the recognition set:

  • Mature B cells with fine hair-like cytoplasmic projections.
  • Massive splenomegaly + pancytopenia + dry tap from marrow fibrosis.
  • Markers: CD19/CD20, CD11c, CD25, CD103, Annexin A1; classic TRAP+.
  • BRAF V600E is a signature driver.
  • Classic treatment association: cladribine.

Follicular Lymphoma

Slides 39-43

  • Indolent germinal-center B-cell lymphoma with painless generalized lymphadenopathy.
  • Can transform to DLBCL.
  • Node shows uniform malignant follicles lacking normal germinal-center polarity, mantle zones, and tingible-body macrophages.
  • CD19/CD20/CD10+, with t(14;18) IgH-BCL2 -> BCL2 overexpression -> impaired apoptosis.
Lecture slide 41 - full slide retained. Reactive follicles vary in size, preserve mantle zones/polarity, and contain tingible-body macrophages because normal germinal-center B cells undergo apoptosis.
Lecture slide 42 - full slide retained. Malignant follicles are more monotonous and lack mantle zones, polarity, and tingible-body macrophages - a direct visual contrast with the preceding reactive node.
Follicular lymphoma
t(14;18) -> BCL2 -> blocks apoptosis.
Indolent, waxing/waning, can transform to DLBCL.

MALT / Marginal Zone Lymphoma

Slide 44

  • Indolent B-cell lymphoma associated with chronic antigenic/inflammatory stimulation.
  • Examples in the deck: H. pylori stomach, Sjögren salivary gland, Hashimoto thyroiditis.
  • Localized gastric MALT may regress after H. pylori eradication.

Mantle Cell Lymphoma

Slides 45-48

  • Older males; nodes, marrow, GI tract/Waldeyer ring can be involved.
  • More aggressive than the classic low-grade small-cell lymphomas.
  • CD19/CD20+ with CD5+, but unlike CLL it is CD23-.
  • t(11;14) IgH-CCND1 -> cyclin D1 overexpression -> cell-cycle drive.
Lecture slide 48 - full slide retained. The slide shows mantle-zone growth, small irregular/cleaved cells, GI involvement, and nuclear cyclin D1 staining.
Translocation trio

Follicular = 14;18 = BCL2.
Mantle = 11;14 = cyclin D1.
Burkitt = 8;14 = MYC.

Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma

Slides 49-51

  • Most common lymphoma in the US in the deck.
  • Usually older adults; can arise de novo or by transformation from an indolent lymphoma.
  • Rapidly enlarging symptomatic nodal or extranodal mass.
  • Aggressive without treatment, but durable remissions are possible.
Lecture slide 51 - full slide retained. The defining visual idea is size: malignant large cells are more than twice the size of a normal small lymphocyte.
Current treatment enrichment - DLBCL

R-CHOP remains the classic board regimen association. Current U.S. therapy has evolved: FDA also approved polatuzumab vedotin + R-CHP for selected previously untreated DLBCL/high-grade B-cell lymphoma patients. Keep this as current clinical enrichment rather than replacing the lecture's foundational behavior/morphology focus.

Burkitt Lymphoma

Slides 52-54

  • Extremely aggressive / STAT diagnosis; high tumor growth fraction.
  • Endemic African form often involves jaw/maxilla; sporadic North American form often abdominal; immunodeficiency-associated form occurs with HIV.
  • CD19/CD20/CD22/CD10 and surface Ig.
  • t(8;14) MYC-IgH.
  • “Starry sky” = pale tingible-body macrophages among rapidly proliferating/dying tumor cells.
Lecture slide 54 - full slide retained. The full slide shows the starry-sky pattern, cytoplasmic vacuoles, and tingible-body macrophages that reflect Burkitt's extraordinarily high proliferation and turnover.
Burkitt cold set
MYC • t(8;14) • CD10+ mature B cell • starry sky • very high Ki-67 • jaw or abdominal mass depending epidemiologic form.

Slide 55: rituximab

Rituximab is an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody and was a major therapeutic shift for mature B-cell malignancies. Mechanistically, antibody binding enables immune-mediated killing of CD20+ B cells.

Slides 56-60Mature T-cell lymphoma: mycosis fungoides / Sézary syndromeLO 5

Slides 57-60

  • Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, usually adults/elderly.
  • Can mimic eczema, psoriasis, or dermatitis for years.
  • Patches -> plaques -> tumors; classically non-sun-exposed skin.
  • Histology: epidermotropism + Pautrier microabscesses.
  • Neoplastic cells have cerebriform nuclei; Sézary syndrome is the leukemic/erythrodermic form.
  • Slide phenotype: CD3+, CD4+, loss of CD7.
Lecture slide 60 - full slide retained. The slide shows epidermotropism/Pautrier microabscess and a cerebriform Sézary nucleus - the two visual findings to remember.
Need to memorize
Mycosis fungoides = skin patches/plaques + epidermotropic CD4 T cells + Pautrier microabscesses.
Sézary = erythroderma + circulating cerebriform malignant T cells.
Slides 62-69Plasma-cell neoplasms: MGUS -> smoldering -> myeloma + AL amyloidLO 6

Slides 62-64: think spectrum, not one disease

  • Clonal plasma cells produce a monoclonal immunoglobulin/paraprotein.
  • Serum protein electrophoresis may show an M-spike.
  • Suppression of normal immunoglobulin diversity contributes to infection risk.
  • The deck places MGUS -> smoldering myeloma -> symptomatic myeloma on a progression spectrum.
  • Deck teaching point: roughly 1% of MGUS cases per year progress; smoldering myeloma has greater progression risk.

Slides 65-66: why myeloma causes systemic disease

  • Marrow replacement -> anemia and other cytopenias.
  • Osteoclast-driving marrow biology -> lytic lesions, bone pain, fractures, hypercalcemia.
  • Monoclonal light chains/proteins -> renal injury and can form AL amyloid.
  • Reduced normal Ig -> infections.
CRAB
Calcium high • Renal injury • Anemia • Bone lesions.
Lecture slide 67 - full slide retained. High circulating protein reduces normal RBC repulsion, producing the classic stacked-coin rouleaux pattern.
Lecture slide 68 - full slide retained. Malignant plasma cells retain eccentric nuclei and abundant cytoplasm; the slide emphasizes CD138 and clonal cytoplasmic immunoglobulin, often with aberrant CD56.
Official LO gap-fill - MGUS, smoldering myeloma, AL amyloid
  • MGUS: monoclonal protein with low clonal burden and no myeloma-defining organ damage; companion uses <10% marrow plasma cells as the classic board threshold.
  • Smoldering myeloma: higher clonal burden than MGUS but no myeloma-defining event.
  • AL amyloidosis: misfolded monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains deposit in tissues; classic board clues include nephrotic proteinuria, restrictive cardiomyopathy, macroglossia, periorbital purpura; Congo red shows apple-green birefringence.
Current diagnostic enrichment - SLiM-CRAB

Modern IMWG criteria allow active myeloma to be diagnosed before classic CRAB damage if certain high-risk myeloma-defining biomarkers are present. The SLiM additions are: ≥60% clonal marrow plasma cells, involved:uninvolved serum free-light-chain ratio ≥100 (with involved FLC ≥100 mg/L), or ≥2 MRI focal lesions. This is current external enrichment beyond the lecture's CRAB-focused slides.

Common exam traps
  • CD5+ B cell does not uniquely mean CLL: CLL is CD23+, mantle is CD23- / cyclin D1+.
  • TdT = immaturity, not T-cell lineage specifically.
  • Starry sky reflects very high turnover and is classically Burkitt, but morphology must be integrated with phenotype/genetics.
  • RS-like cells are not enough by themselves: classic Hodgkin diagnosis is architectural + morphologic + immunophenotypic.
  • An M-spike alone does not equal multiple myeloma.
