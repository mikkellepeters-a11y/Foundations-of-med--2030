Official Lab Objectives

1. Recognize normal peripheral-blood RBC histology and identify anisocytosis, microcytosis, spherocytosis, sickled cells, and reticulocytosis.
2. Recognize normal histology and functions of lymphocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, and monocytes.
3. Recognize normal peripheral-blood platelets.
4. Recognize normal bone marrow and differentiate bone marrow from peripheral blood.
What this review deck actually emphasizes
Identify the 5 major WBCs -> identify platelets -> recognize a normal peripheral-blood field -> recognize marrow as denser/darker with precursors -> spot megakaryocytes.

Slides 2-6: The WBC Lineup

Released SAL review slide 2. The slide labels the neutrophil as the most abundant granulocyte, associates it with phagocytosis, and emphasizes its lobulated nucleus.
Slide words to know: most abundant granulocyte • phagocytosis • lobulated nucleus.
Released SAL review slide 3. The slide emphasizes a spherical nucleus, very little cytoplasm, agranular morphology, and the fact that T and B cells cannot be distinguished without a special stain.
Lymphocyte practical clue
Round/spherical dark nucleus + very little cytoplasm. Do not call it T vs B from routine morphology.
Released SAL review slide 4. The slide calls out a large horseshoe-shaped nucleus and notes that monocytes differentiate into phagocytic cells after entering connective tissue. It labels them agranular.
Monocyte practical clue
Large cell + horseshoe/folded nucleus.
Released SAL review slide 5. The review slide emphasizes bright eosinophilic cytoplasmic granules and links eosinophils to allergic reactions, parasitic infections, and phagocytosis.
Eosinophil practical clue
Bright pink-red/eosinophilic granules.
Released SAL review slide 6. The slide emphasizes large basophilic cytoplasmic granules and identifies the basophil as the least numerous granulocyte.
Basophil practical clue
Coarse dark basophilic granules dominate the cell.

Slide 7: Platelets

Released SAL review slide 7. The black arrows identify tiny platelet fragments in the peripheral smear. The review slide states that platelets are fragments of megakaryocytes and are important in blood clotting and wound healing.
Released-slide platelet set
Small fragmented elements in peripheral blood -> fragments of marrow megakaryocytes -> clotting and wound healing.

Slide 8: Peripheral Blood

Released SAL review slide 8. This whole-field image is the key normal peripheral-blood reference. The slide labels the three visible categories: erythrocytes, leukocytes, and platelets.
Peripheral-blood visual gestalt
Mostly erythrocytes, with scattered leukocytes and tiny platelets. The field is relatively open compared with marrow.

Slides 9-10: Bone Marrow & Megakaryocytes

Released SAL review slide 9. The released review slide explicitly contrasts marrow with peripheral blood: marrow is denser and darker and contains precursor cells such as megakaryocytes.
Deck's marrow-vs-blood distinction
Bone marrow = denser + darker + precursor cells.
Peripheral blood = mature erythrocytes, leukocytes, platelets in a more open field.
Released SAL review slide 10. Yellow arrows mark the large megakaryocytes. The review slide calls them platelet precursors and describes them as large, multinucleated cells found in bone marrow.
Keep the SAL wording: the released slide describes megakaryocytes as large, multinucleated cells and identifies them as platelet precursors. Other Week 7 material may use more detailed nuclear terminology, but this note preserves the review-slide wording here.

Practical Recognition Review

Last 60 seconds
Neutrophil: lobulated nucleus. Lymphocyte: spherical nucleus, scant cytoplasm. Monocyte: large horseshoe nucleus. Eosinophil: bright eosinophilic granules. Basophil: large basophilic granules.
Platelet: tiny megakaryocyte fragment.
Peripheral blood: mostly RBCs + scattered WBCs + tiny platelets.
Marrow: denser/darker + precursors; megakaryocyte is the giant platelet precursor.
