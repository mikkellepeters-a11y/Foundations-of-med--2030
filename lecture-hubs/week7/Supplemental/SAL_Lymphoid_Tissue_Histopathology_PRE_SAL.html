Lymph Node

Cross-reference from Week 7 Hematopoiesis & Lymphoid System, not an SAL slide. Cross-reference from Lecture 3. Learn the outside-in map now; replace/add the actual SAL specimen later.
Outside -> in
Capsule -> subcapsular sinus -> cortex (B-cell follicles) -> paracortex (T cells + HEVs) -> medulla (cords + sinuses) -> hilum.
ZoneNormal histologyKey function
CortexB-cell follicles. Primary follicles are dark/uniform; secondary follicles have pale germinal centers.B-cell activation and germinal-center reactions.
ParacortexT-cell-rich zone between follicles and medulla; contains high endothelial venules.Naive lymphocytes enter from blood through HEVs; T-cell/APC interactions.
Medullary cordsCellular cords rich in plasma cells, lymphocytes, macrophages.Antibody-producing plasma-cell compartment.
Medullary sinusesPaler channels containing macrophages/reticular cells.Filter lymph on its way to the hilum.
Primary vs secondary follicle

Primary follicle: small/dark resting B cells, no pale center. Secondary follicle: pale germinal center + darker mantle zone, meaning active antigen-driven B-cell proliferation.

Tonsil & Gut-Associated Lymphoid Tissue

Cross-reference from Week 7 Hematopoiesis & Lymphoid System, not an SAL slide. Cross-reference from Lecture 3: nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium + deep crypts + underlying lymphoid follicles.
StructureRecognition clue
Palatine tonsilNonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium, deep branching crypts, many lymphoid nodules, incomplete deep capsule.
Pharyngeal tonsil / adenoidRespiratory epithelium; folds rather than the deep squamous crypts of palatine tonsil.
Peyer's patchIleum; aggregated lymphoid follicles in mucosa/submucosa, dome/M-cell region, no true capsule.
AppendixNarrow irregular lumen, colonic crypts, no villi, circumferential lymphoid follicles.
Cross-reference from Week 7 Hematopoiesis & Lymphoid System, not an SAL slide. Cross-reference from Lecture 3. Aggregated ileal follicles and overlying antigen-sampling epithelium are the visual anchor.
Mucosal rule
Lymphoid tissue sits directly under epithelium and is generally unencapsulated; antigen can cross the epithelium directly.
SAL IMAGE SLOT: palatine vs pharyngeal tonsil • Peyer's patch • appendix

Spleen

Cross-reference from Week 7 Hematopoiesis & Lymphoid System, not an SAL slide. Cross-reference from Lecture 3. The splenic signature is red pulp + white pulp centered around arterioles, not cortex/paracortex/medulla.
RegionRecognition clue
White pulp - PALST-cell sheath around a central arteriole.
White-pulp follicleB-cell nodule adjacent to PALS; may have a germinal center when active.
Marginal zoneInterface between white and red pulp; macrophages + memory B cells.
Red pulpRBC-rich cords of Billroth and venous sinusoids; mechanical blood filtration.
Lymph node vs spleen

Lymph node: capsule + cortex/paracortex/medulla + subcapsular sinus. Spleen: red/white pulp + central arteriole, no cortex/medulla and no afferent lymphatics.

SAL IMAGE SLOT: spleen low power • PALS/central arteriole • red pulp/sinus

Thymus

Cross-reference from Week 7 Hematopoiesis & Lymphoid System, not an SAL slide. Cross-reference from Lecture 3: lobules, dark cortex, pale medulla, and Hassall corpuscles.
Thymus cold set
Lobulated organ + dark cortex + pale medulla + Hassall corpuscles = thymus.
No follicles. No germinal centers. No afferent lymphatics.
RegionWhat you see / function
CortexVery dark because packed with immature thymocytes; positive selection occurs here.
MedullaPaler, fewer lymphocytes, more epithelium; negative selection and Hassall corpuscles.
Hassall corpuscleConcentric eosinophilic epithelial whorl in medulla only.

Reactive Lymphadenopathy - Provisional Pre-SAL Framework

Important

The official objective says to recognize pathological lymph-node changes caused by lymphadenopathy, but there is no dedicated summary or released SAL image set yet. The patterns below are the standard benign/reactive framework to study provisionally. We will revise this section to match exactly what your SAL instructor shows.

Reactive patternWhat expandsHistology clueTypical association
Follicular hyperplasiaB-cell follicles/germinal centersNumerous variable-size secondary follicles; germinal centers with polarity and tingible-body macrophages; architecture generally preserved.Antigen-driven humoral responses, autoimmune/infectious stimulation.
Paracortical hyperplasiaT-cell zonesExpanded paracortex, immunoblasts may be prominent, follicles can be compressed.Viral infections, drug/immune stimulation.
Sinus histiocytosisSubcapsular/medullary sinusesDilated sinuses filled with macrophages/histiocytes.Nodes draining inflammation or tumors.
Suppurative lymphadenitisNeutrophilic inflammatory fociNeutrophils, necrosis, microabscess/abscess formation.Pyogenic bacterial infection.
Granulomatous lymphadenitisMacrophage aggregates/granulomasEpithelioid histiocytes +/- giant cells; may be necrotizing depending cause.Mycobacteria, fungi, sarcoid-pattern disease, cat-scratch variants, etc.
Reactive vs lymphoma: first-pass architecture rule
Reactive: usually polymorphic cells, preserved overall architecture, follicles vary in size/shape, germinal centers can show polarity and tingible-body macrophages.
Neoplastic concern: monotony, architectural effacement, repetitive uniform follicles/sheets, loss of normal compartment relationships, clonal phenotype.
Ozzy checkpoint - click him once you can explain “reactive preserves a pattern; lymphoma tends to replace the pattern.”
Do not overcall a big germinal center as lymphoma

Reactive germinal centers can be very large and mitotically active. Look for the whole pattern: preserved mantle zones, variation from follicle to follicle, mixed cell populations, tingible-body macrophages, and preserved nodal compartments.

Fast Organ-ID Algorithm

QuestionAnswer
Deep squamous crypts?Palatine tonsil.
Ileal wall with aggregated follicles?Peyer's patch.
Central arteriole inside lymphoid tissue + red pulp?Spleen.
Lobules + dark cortex/pale medulla + Hassall corpuscle?Thymus.
Capsule + subcapsular sinus + cortical follicles + paracortex + medulla?Lymph node.
Then ask the pathology question
Is normal architecture preserved? Which compartment is expanded? Are the cells polymorphic/reactive or monotonous/effacing?
