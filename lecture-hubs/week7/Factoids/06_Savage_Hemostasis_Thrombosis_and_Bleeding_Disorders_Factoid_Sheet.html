Primary = platelet plug / mucosal. Secondary = fibrin / deep delayed bleeding.

Primary hemostasis

  • Adhesion: vWF-GpIb
  • Activation: ADP-P2Y12, TXA2, granules
  • Aggregation: GpIIb/IIIa-fibrinogen
  • vWF carries factor VIII

Platelet disorders

  • Glanzmann: GpIIb/IIIa; ristocetin normal
  • Bernard-Soulier: GpIb; giant platelets; ristocetin abnormal
  • ITP: isolated low platelets, high MPV, normal PT/aPTT
  • vWD: adhesion defect +/- low VIII

Natural anticoagulants

  • Antithrombin: IIa/Xa especially; heparin accelerates it
  • Protein C + S: inactivate Va/VIIIa
  • C/S are vitamin K-dependent

Inherited thrombophilia

  • FVL: factor V resists protein C
  • Prothrombin G20210A: too much prothrombin
  • C/S deficiency: failure to turn off V/VIII
  • Don't test C/S during acute clot/warfarin if avoidable

HIT

Heparin + PF4 IgG -> platelet activation -> thrombosis + falling platelets. Low platelets, but the disease is a clotting emergency.
  • 4 Ts: Thrombocytopenia, Timing, Thrombosis, oTher causes.
  • High suspicion: stop all heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant.
  • PF4 ELISA = sensitive screen; SRA = functional confirmation.
  • Don't rush into warfarin during acute HIT.

If you see X -> think Y

  • Mucosal + normal platelets + abnormal ristocetin -> vWD
  • Giant platelets + no ristocetin response -> Bernard-Soulier
  • Normal ristocetin + aggregation failure elsewhere -> Glanzmann
  • Hemarthrosis + isolated high aPTT correcting on mix -> Hemophilia A/B
  • Schistocytes + low Plt + normal coag -> TTP/HUS
  • Schistocytes + low Plt + high PT/aPTT -> DIC
  • Thrombosis + heparin day 5-10 + >50% Plt fall -> HIT
  • Warfarin skin necrosis -> think early protein C depletion
