Oncogenic Microbes and Their Role in Cancer

Slides 3-7: How Can a Virus Cause Cancer?

Core language
Proto-oncogenes are the gas pedal. Tumor suppressors are the brakes. Viral oncogenesis can increase growth signaling, activate oncogenes, integrate into host DNA, or interfere with tumor-suppressor pathways.
Slide 5. The lecture's cell-cycle framing: proto-oncogenes promote division; tumor suppressors restrain it. p53 and RB are the two named tumor suppressors.
Cell-cycle controlLecture concept
Proto-oncogene / oncogeneGrowth-promoting signal; viral insertion or stimulation can drive excessive proliferation.
p53Allows DNA repair or directs severely damaged cells toward apoptosis.
RBRestrains cell-cycle progression; loss removes a major brake on division.
Slide 6. Major oncogenic DNA viruses named in the deck: EBV, HPV, HHV-8/KSHV, and HBV.
Slide 7. Current-deck RNA-virus list includes retroviral and hepatitis-associated examples. Use the deck's wording for lecture-specific testing; the handout adds a more conventional board-style HTLV-1 framing.
Current-deck vs handout mismatch: slide 7 lists HTLV-1: lymphoblastic leukemia, HTLV-2: T-cell lymphoma, HTLV-3: B-cell lymphoma. The MCG 2029 handout instead emphasizes the board-style association HTLV-1 → adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma via Tax. This companion does not silently merge those statements: the deck wording is faculty content; the Tax/ATLL connection is labeled handout enrichment.
Handout-only board framing: DNA tumor viruses commonly remove tumor-suppressor “brakes,” while retroviruses may activate host growth pathways through proviral insertion or viral regulatory proteins. The handout specifically reviews RB holding E2F at the G1/S checkpoint and calls p53 the “guardian of the genome.”

Slides 8-18: Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)

EBV backbone
Enveloped dsDNA herpesvirus → saliva → oropharynx → B-cell infection → latency in B lymphocytes.
Slide 9. EBV pathogenesis in the current deck: oropharyngeal infection, viremia, B-cell infection, CD8 T-cell response producing atypical/Downey cells, and increased risk of lymphoproliferative disease when T-cell immunity is impaired.
Slide 10. EBV infectious-mononucleosis pattern: adolescent/young adult, fever, fatigue, sore throat, exudative pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, atypical lymphocytosis, elevated liver enzymes, and heterophile antibodies.
Slide 11. Peripheral blood smear comparison between a normal lymphocyte and atypical/reactive lymphocytes in infectious mononucleosis.
Slide 13. EBV oncogenic proteins on the faculty slide: LMP-1, EBNA-2, and viral IL-10.
EBV factorCurrent-deck function
LMP-1Acts like a constitutively active CD40 receptor; stimulates B-cell growth; activates BCL2 and prevents apoptosis.
EBNA-2Transactivates host genes such as cyclin D and promotes B-cell immortalization.
vIL-10Reduces macrophage activation of T cells and supports EBV-dependent B-cell transformation.

Burkitt lymphoma

Slide 14. Faculty nuance: endemic Burkitt is strongly EBV-associated, but the slide explicitly says EBV is not directly oncogenic here. EBV acts as a polyclonal B-cell mitogen that helps set the stage for MYC translocation and additional mutations.
Slide 15. The lecture's high-yield Burkitt figure centers on the MYC/Ig translocation, classically chromosomes 8 and 14, followed by increased MYC expression and neoplastic B-cell outgrowth.
Do not flatten the EBV story
Slide 13: EBV has transforming latent proteins.
Slide 14: in Burkitt lymphoma specifically, the deck emphasizes that EBV is not directly oncogenic; it creates a proliferative setting in which MYC t(8;14) and other mutations produce cancer.
Handout-only enrichment: EBV binds CD21 on B cells; endemic Burkitt often presents as an African child with a jaw mass and “starry-sky” histology. The handout also reviews Reed-Sternberg morphology and posterior cervical lymphadenopathy in EBV mono. Those are useful board links, but the current oncogenic-virus deck does not teach each of them as dedicated slides.
Slide 17. The deck visually links EBV latent proteins to Hodgkin Reed-Sternberg cells and survival signaling.

Slides 19-26: Human Papillomavirus (HPV)

Slide 20. The key mechanistic slide: HPV infects squamous epithelium; benign warts maintain viral DNA in an episomal form, whereas oncogenic transformation is associated with viral integration and expression of E6/E7.
HPV cancer mechanism
E6 disables p53. E7 disables RB. Removing both brakes allows continued proliferation, survival of damaged cells, and genomic instability.
Slide 21. Disease spectrum: HPV 1-4 skin/plantar warts; low-risk 6/11 anogenital warts; high-risk 16/18/31/33 cancer associations.
Slide 22. Faculty visual distinction: condylomata acuminata from HPV 6/11 should not be confused with condylomata lata.
Slide 23. E7 binds RB, releases E2F, and turns on transcription that promotes mitosis.
Ozzy checkpoint - click him once “E6 = p53 / E7 = RB” is automatic and you can explain why each one removes a different cell-cycle safeguard.
Slide 24. The faculty E6/E7 summary broadens the mechanism: E6 increases telomerase and inhibits p53; E7 suppresses p21/RB-related control and increases proliferative signaling.
Slide 25. Histopathology: koilocytes are squamous epithelial cells with perinuclear halos/cytoplasmic vacuolization.

Slides 27-32: HHV-8 / KSHV

Slide 27. HHV-8 is an enveloped dsDNA herpesvirus, found in all forms of Kaposi sarcoma. The deck places latent virus in endothelial spindle cells and names sexual and organ-transplant transmission.
HHV-8 concept
HHV-8 acts like a “molecular pirate”: it carries viral versions of growth/survival regulators that suppress apoptosis, interfere with tumor suppressors, and promote endothelial-cell proliferation.
Slide 28. Faculty oncogenesis list: viral BCL2, LANA-1, vFLIP, viral IL-6, and additional cytokine/chemokine signaling drive survival and proliferation.
Slide 29. HHV-8 proteins interact with RB and p53, and viral interferon-regulatory signaling can keep c-MYC activity from being suppressed.
HHV-8 product / pathwayCurrent-deck function
Viral BCL2Prevents apoptosis
LANA-1 / cyclin-related activityInterferes with RB-linked cell-cycle control
vFLIPFLICE-inhibitory anti-apoptotic activity
Viral IL-6 / IL-6 / IL-8 / chemokinesGrowth signaling for B lymphocytes and endothelial spindle cells
Viral IFN regulatory factorPrevents interferon-mediated repression of c-MYC
Slide 31. Kaposi histopathology: violaceous raised plaques with proliferating endothelial spindle cells, slit-like vascular spaces/channels containing RBCs, and mitotic figures.
Kaposi pathology
Violaceous lesion + spindle endothelial proliferation + slit-like vascular spaces with extravasated RBCs = Kaposi sarcoma.
Handout-only enrichment: the MCG 2029 notes add primary effusion lymphoma and describe Kaposi as an endothelial malignancy that may involve skin, GI tract, and lung. The current oncogenic deck is centered on Kaposi sarcoma itself.

Slides 33-34: HBV and Retroviral Oncogenesis

Slide 33. HBV/HCC model in the current deck: repeated cycles of cell injury and repair drive fibrosis and eventual HCC; HBV genome integration may activate oncogenes, destabilize chromosomes, or disrupt cell-cycle regulators.
Hepatitis-virus cancer logic
Chronic injury → repeated regeneration → more opportunities for oncogenic mutation. The deck additionally gives HBV-specific integration mechanisms.
Slide 34. Retroviral oncogenesis slide: proviral insertion can alter adjacent proto-oncogenes; the slide also describes deletion/transduction mechanisms and transformation after integration.
Handout-only board enrichment: the Drive handout emphasizes HTLV-1 → adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma via Tax and frames both HBV and HCV hepatocarcinogenesis primarily through chronic inflammation/regeneration. These are kept separate from slide 7's current faculty list.
