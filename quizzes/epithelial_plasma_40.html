Part II: Answer Key and Explanations

Epithelial Structure and Classification

1. D — Diffusion from capillaries in the underlying connective tissueIntermediate

Reasoning: Epithelia are avascular, so oxygen and nutrients diffuse from vessels in the connective tissue beneath them.

Key distractor distinction: Direct perfusion is impossible because normal epithelium contains no blood vessels.

2. C — Apical surfaceIn-house

Reasoning: The apical surface faces the lumen or external environment.

Key distractor distinction: The basal surface contacts the basal lamina, and lateral surfaces contact neighboring cells.

3. E — Basal laminaIntermediate

Reasoning: Type IV collagen, laminin, and proteoglycans are core components of the supportive and filtering basal lamina.

Key distractor distinction: The lamina propria is connective tissue rather than the specialized extracellular sheet described.

4. A — Pseudostratified columnar epitheliumIn-house

Reasoning: Pseudostratified cells have nuclei at different heights, but all cells contact the basement membrane.

Key distractor distinction: True stratified epithelia contain cells that do not all reach the basement membrane.

5. C — Simple squamousIn-house

Reasoning: A single layer of flattened cells minimizes diffusion distance in alveoli.

Key distractor distinction: Cuboidal and columnar cells are thicker and better suited for absorption or secretion.

6. A — Simple cuboidalIntermediate

Reasoning: Kidney tubules commonly contain one layer of cube-shaped cells specialized for absorption and secretion.

Key distractor distinction: Stratified cuboidal epithelium has multiple layers and is found in larger ducts.

7. B — Simple columnarIn-house

Reasoning: Tall cells in a single layer with basally located nuclei describe simple columnar epithelium.

Key distractor distinction: Pseudostratified columnar cells have nuclei at different levels.

8. C — Nonkeratinized stratified squamousIntermediate

Reasoning: The esophagus requires abrasion resistance while retaining living, nucleated surface cells.

Key distractor distinction: Keratinized epithelium has dead, anucleate superficial cells.

9. E — Keratinized stratified squamousIn-house

Reasoning: Dead, anucleate, keratin-filled superficial cells characterize epidermal epithelium.

Key distractor distinction: Nonkeratinized surfaces retain viable nuclei at the surface.

10. A — Transitional epitheliumIntermediate

Reasoning: Urothelium changes thickness with distension and has dome-shaped umbrella cells when relaxed.

Key distractor distinction: Simple and pseudostratified epithelia do not have the same distensible architecture.

Surface Specializations and Junctions

11. D — Actin filamentsIn-house

Reasoning: Microvilli contain actin cores and form the intestinal brush border.

Key distractor distinction: Motile cilia contain microtubules rather than actin cores.

12. B — Dynein arms associated with a 9+2 microtubule arrangementStep 1 style

Reasoning: The findings indicate primary ciliary dyskinesia caused by defective ciliary motility.

Key distractor distinction: Microvilli are nonmotile actin-based projections and do not explain situs inversus.

13. D — StereociliaIntermediate

Reasoning: Stereocilia are long, nonmotile, actin-based projections in the epididymis and inner ear.

Key distractor distinction: Motile cilia contain a 9+2 microtubule axoneme.

14. C — Prevention of paracellular fluid movementStep 1 style

Reasoning: Claudins form tight junctions that seal the paracellular pathway.

Key distractor distinction: Gap junctions, not tight junctions, exchange ions and second messengers between cells.

15. C — Cadherins and catenins connected to actinIntermediate

Reasoning: Adherens junctions use cadherins linked by catenins to the actin cytoskeleton.

Key distractor distinction: Desmosomes link cadherins to intermediate filaments rather than actin.

16. C — Desmogleins with intermediate filamentsStep 1 style

Reasoning: Flaccid blisters and acantholysis indicate pemphigus vulgaris, which disrupts desmosomal desmogleins.

Key distractor distinction: Hemidesmosomal integrins attach cells to the basement membrane and cause tense blisters when disrupted.

17. E — Hemidesmosomes containing integrinsStep 1 style

Reasoning: Tense blisters with separation of the entire epithelial layer indicate loss of cell-matrix attachment.

Key distractor distinction: Desmosome disruption separates cells from one another rather than detaching the whole sheet.

18. C — ConnexinsIn-house

Reasoning: Connexins assemble into connexons that form gap-junction channels.

Key distractor distinction: Claudins form tight junctions and do not create intercellular communication channels.

Epithelial Change and Glands

19. B — Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transitionStep 1 style

Reasoning: Loss of E-cadherin, polarity, and adhesion with increased invasion describes EMT.

Key distractor distinction: Metaplasia is replacement of one mature cell type by another without the invasive program described.

20. B — Metaplasia that increases resistance to injury but impairs mucus clearanceIntermediate

Reasoning: Squamous metaplasia protects against smoke injury but sacrifices cilia and mucociliary clearance.

Key distractor distinction: Metaplasia is potentially reversible and does not by itself prove invasive cancer.

21. D — EndocrineIn-house

Reasoning: Endocrine glands are ductless and release products into blood.

Key distractor distinction: Exocrine glands deliver products through ducts to a surface.

22. E — HolocrineIntermediate

Reasoning: Holocrine secretion releases the entire disintegrating cell, as in sebaceous glands.

Key distractor distinction: Merocrine secretion uses exocytosis, and apocrine secretion releases only the apical portion.

Plasma, Electrophoresis, and Protein Function

23. D — SerumIn-house

Reasoning: Serum is the fluid remaining after clotting and therefore lacks fibrinogen and consumed clotting factors.

Key distractor distinction: Plasma is obtained from anticoagulated blood and retains clotting factors.

24. A — Fibrinogen coats erythrocytes, reduces their electrostatic repulsion, and promotes rouleaux formationIntermediate

Reasoning: Inflammatory fibrinogen promotes rouleaux, causing RBCs to settle more rapidly.

Key distractor distinction: Polycythemia and dehydration generally decrease ESR rather than increase it.

25. D — Immunoglobulins are produced by plasma cells, whereas CK-MB is an extrinsic marker released after cellular damageIn-house

Reasoning: Immunoglobulins are the major nonhepatic intrinsic plasma proteins, while CK-MB is normally intracellular.

Key distractor distinction: Albumin and fibrinogen are synthesized and secreted by the liver.

26. B — The protein is recognized by hepatic asialoglycoprotein receptors and removedIntermediate

Reasoning: Loss of terminal sialic acid marks many glycoproteins for hepatic clearance.

Key distractor distinction: Desialylation is a clearance signal, not a mechanism for conversion into fibrin or immunoglobulin.

27. A — AlbuminIntermediate

Reasoning: Albumin is small and highly negatively charged, so it migrates farthest toward the anode at alkaline pH.

Key distractor distinction: Gamma globulins remain closest to the origin.

28. A — A monoclonal immunoglobulinStep 1 style

Reasoning: A narrow gamma-region M-spike with bone pain and hypercalcemia indicates a single plasma-cell clone.

Key distractor distinction: Polyclonal immunoglobulin production causes a broad gamma elevation.

29. A — A broad, diffuse increase in the gamma regionIntermediate

Reasoning: Chronic inflammation stimulates many immunoglobulin clones, creating a broad gamma increase.

Key distractor distinction: A narrow spike suggests monoclonal production.

30. E — Decreased plasma oncotic pressureStep 1 style

Reasoning: Low albumin reduces the force retaining water within capillaries, causing edema and ascites.

Key distractor distinction: Increased plasma oncotic pressure would draw fluid into vessels.

31. B — Less calcium is bound to albumin, lowering total calcium without necessarily changing ionized calciumIntermediate

Reasoning: A substantial fraction of serum calcium is albumin-bound, so total calcium falls when albumin falls.

Key distractor distinction: Ionized calcium is the physiologically active fraction and may remain normal.

32. B — Alpha-1Step 1 style

Reasoning: Alpha-1 antitrypsin is an alpha-1 globulin that inhibits neutrophil elastase.

Key distractor distinction: Haptoglobin is an alpha-2 globulin and is associated with hemoglobin binding.

33. E — Haptoglobin in the alpha-2 fractionStep 1 style

Reasoning: Haptoglobin binds free hemoglobin and is consumed during intravascular hemolysis.

Key distractor distinction: Transferrin transports iron and is located mainly in the beta fraction.

34. B — Albumin and transferrinIn-house

Reasoning: Both albumin and transferrin decrease during the acute-phase response.

Key distractor distinction: CRP, fibrinogen, haptoglobin, ferritin, and hepcidin are positive acute-phase proteins.

35. E — Hepcidin, by blocking intestinal iron absorption and iron release from macrophagesIntermediate

Reasoning: Inflammatory hepcidin decreases circulating iron by trapping it in storage sites and reducing absorption.

Key distractor distinction: Transferrin is a negative acute-phase protein and does not cause macrophage iron release.

Serum Markers and Isoenzymes

36. E — CK-MMIn-house

Reasoning: CK-MM accounts for nearly all creatine kinase in skeletal muscle.

Key distractor distinction: CK-MB is enriched in cardiac muscle but is not the dominant skeletal-muscle isoenzyme.

37. D — CK-MBStep 1 style

Reasoning: CK-MB returns toward baseline within 48–72 hours, so a new rise can identify reinfarction.

Key distractor distinction: Troponin remains elevated for many days and may still reflect the first infarction.

38. A — AST/ALT ratio greater than 2Step 1 style

Reasoning: Alcoholic liver injury classically produces AST greater than ALT, often with a ratio above 2.

Key distractor distinction: In most other hepatocellular injuries ALT exceeds AST.

39. A — GGT and lipaseIntermediate

Reasoning: GGT supports a hepatobiliary source of elevated ALP, while lipase is more specific for pancreatitis than amylase.

Key distractor distinction: A normal GGT with elevated ALP favors a nonhepatic source such as bone.

40. A — CEAStep 1 style

Reasoning: CEA is used to monitor colorectal cancer for recurrence after treatment.

Key distractor distinction: Tumor markers are generally more useful for monitoring than for population screening.
