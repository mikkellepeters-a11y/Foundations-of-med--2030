25 image-based questions · structure → function → pathology

Difficulty: 4 in-house · 10 intermediate · 11 Step 1-style
Approach: image recognition is only the first step. Most questions require a second inference about function, junctional biology, adaptation, dysplasia, or invasion.
Lecture overlap: epithelial classification, polarity, apical specializations, cell junctions, glandular secretion, metaplasia/EMT, dysplasia, malignancy, H&E interpretation, and cytokeratin IHC.
Image note: The quiz page and scantron are local, but the micrographs are loaded from their cited open-license/public-domain source files on Wikimedia Commons, so the images require an internet connection. Full image attribution is intentionally placed after the answer key.

Part II: Answer Key & Explanations

1. B — Simple squamous — rapid diffusion and transport In-house

Reasoning pathway: A single layer of flattened cells creates the shortest epithelial diffusion path, which is why simple squamous epithelium is used at surfaces such as alveoli, endothelium, and mesothelium.

Important distractor distinction: Stratified squamous adds layers for protection, while cuboidal and columnar cells provide more cytoplasmic machinery for secretion or absorption.

2. C — Active secretion and absorption Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Simple cuboidal cells have more cytoplasm and organelles than simple squamous cells, supporting the secretion and absorption characteristic of renal tubules.

Important distractor distinction: Rapid diffusion favors a thinner squamous barrier; mucociliary clearance requires ciliated columnar epithelium.

3. D — Actin filaments Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: The fuzzy brush border is formed by microvilli, which increase absorptive surface area and contain an actin core.

Important distractor distinction: Motile cilia contain 9+2 microtubules, whereas type IV collagen is a basal-lamina component rather than an apical cytoskeleton.

4. A — Restriction of paracellular movement by tight junctions Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Claudins are core tight-junction proteins. Tight junctions form the most apical seal and limit paracellular leakage, so their disruption permits fluid loss into the lumen.

Important distractor distinction: Desmosomes link intermediate filaments, hemidesmosomes anchor cells to basal lamina, and gap junctions mediate direct cell-to-cell communication.

5. C — All epithelial cells contact the basement membrane In-house

Reasoning pathway: Pseudostratified epithelium appears multilayered because cells have different heights and nuclear positions, but every cell remains attached to the basement membrane.

Important distractor distinction: Not every cell must reach the lumen, which is precisely why the lining can appear stratified.

6. E — Greater resistance to chronic irritation but impaired mucociliary clearance and increased risk of dysplastic progression Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Smoking can drive squamous metaplasia of pseudostratified ciliated respiratory epithelium. The squamous lining tolerates irritation better but sacrifices cilia and goblet-cell function; persistent stress can progress to dysplasia and carcinoma.

Important distractor distinction: Metaplasia is initially adaptive and reversible; it does not improve ciliary transport or gas exchange.

7. B — A 9+2 microtubule axoneme with dynein-dependent motility Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Motile cilia contain a 9+2 microtubule arrangement and use dynein to generate motion along the epithelial surface.

Important distractor distinction: Actin cores characterize microvilli and stereocilia, which are not the motile cilia shown here.

8. C — Movement of the ovum along the tubal surface Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Fallopian-tube cilia move the ovum along the epithelial surface. Dynein dysfunction makes these cilia ineffective and can contribute to infertility.

Important distractor distinction: The tubal epithelium transports the ovum; it does not produce oocytes or form a keratinized barrier.

9. D — Nonkeratinized stratified squamous In-house

Reasoning pathway: The esophagus is lined by nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium: multiple protective layers with living, nucleated surface cells in a moist environment.

Important distractor distinction: Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium has a superficial layer of dead, anucleate keratin-filled cells, as in epidermis.

10. A — Metaplasia caused by stem-cell reprogramming Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Barrett esophagus is metaplasia: chronic stress reprograms local stem cells so one mature epithelial type is replaced by another better able to tolerate the environment.

Important distractor distinction: Hyperplasia increases cell number without changing the mature cell type; dysplasia adds cytologic and architectural atypia.

11. E — Transitional epithelium (urothelium) In-house

Reasoning pathway: Urothelium is a specialized stratified epithelium of the urinary tract. Its large superficial umbrella cells change shape as the bladder distends.

Important distractor distinction: Simple squamous is thin for diffusion rather than specialized for repeated distension.

12. B — The lining temporarily changes appearance while remaining a specialized stratified urothelium Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Transitional epithelium is defined by its specialized stratified organization and ability to change superficial-cell shape with distension. Flattening during stretch does not convert it into a one-layer simple squamous epithelium.

Important distractor distinction: Cell shape does matter, but epithelial classification must be interpreted in the context of layering and the tissue's dynamic state.

13. A — Stereocilia — actin core Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Epididymal stereocilia are extremely long, nonmotile microvilli with an actin core and an absorptive function.

Important distractor distinction: Despite their name, stereocilia are not true cilia and do not contain the 9+2 dynein-driven axoneme.

14. D — Its core is actin, so the same dynein-arm defect does not directly disable this specialization Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: The projections shown are stereocilia. They are actin-based, nonmotile extensions, so a dynein-arm defect that disables true cilia does not directly disrupt the stereociliary core.

Important distractor distinction: True cilia are microtubule-based and dynein-dependent; stereocilia are structurally closer to elongated microvilli.

15. B — Apocrine Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Apocrine secretion releases the secretory product together with part of the apical cytoplasm.

Important distractor distinction: Merocrine secretion uses exocytosis without cytoplasmic loss; holocrine secretion releases product when the entire cell disintegrates.

16. C — A reversible metaplastic adaptation to chronic stress Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Replacement of one mature epithelial type by another in response to chronic stress is metaplasia. Metaplasia can be reversible if the stress is removed.

Important distractor distinction: Metaplasia increases cancer risk when persistent, but the change itself is not equivalent to dysplasia or invasion.

17. E — Pleomorphism with increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and loss of normal architecture Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Dysplasia is disordered precancerous growth characterized by cytologic atypia such as pleomorphism and increased N/C ratio together with architectural disorganization.

Important distractor distinction: The presence of a different mature epithelial type alone describes metaplasia, not dysplasia.

18. A — High-grade dysplasia with hyperchromasia and loss of polarity Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Hyperchromasia, nuclear crowding/pleomorphism, and loss of polarity are classic features of dysplasia; in this image they support high-grade dysplasia.

Important distractor distinction: Hyperplasia can increase cell number while retaining orderly maturation and polarity, unlike the atypical architecture here.

19. D — Neoplastic epithelial cells breaching the basement membrane and entering stroma Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Basement-membrane invasion into underlying stroma establishes invasive carcinoma rather than an intraepithelial dysplastic process.

Important distractor distinction: High N/C ratio, hyperchromasia, nucleoli, and mitotic activity can all occur in severe dysplasia before invasion.

20. C — Desmoglein in desmosomes Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Pemphigus vulgaris involves autoantibodies against desmoglein in desmosomes, causing acantholysis and loss of cell-to-cell adhesion.

Important distractor distinction: Hemidesmosomal proteins anchor the entire epithelial layer to the basal lamina and are associated with bullous pemphigoid.

21. B — Keratin intermediate filaments through desmosomal cadherins Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Desmosomes act as spot welds that connect keratin intermediate-filament networks of neighboring epithelial cells through desmogleins/desmocollins.

Important distractor distinction: Adherens junctions connect actin through cadherins/catenins; hemidesmosomes use integrins to link intermediate filaments to basal lamina.

22. E — Anchoring of basal epithelial intermediate filaments to the basal lamina Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Bullous pemphigoid targets hemidesmosomal proteins, disrupting the attachment of basal epithelial cells to the underlying basement membrane and producing a subepidermal, tense blister.

Important distractor distinction: Pemphigus disrupts desmosomes and separates epithelial cells from one another, producing intraepidermal acantholysis and flaccid blisters.

23. A — High-grade dysplasia / carcinoma in situ Intermediate

Reasoning pathway: Full-thickness epithelial atypia without basement-membrane invasion corresponds to high-grade dysplasia/carcinoma in situ.

Important distractor distinction: Invasive carcinoma requires breach of the basement membrane and extension into underlying stroma.

24. D — Downregulation of E-cadherin with loss of polarity during epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: During epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, malignant epithelial cells downregulate junctional proteins such as E-cadherin, lose polarity, become motile, and gain the ability to invade basement membrane.

Important distractor distinction: Stronger junctions and basement-membrane anchoring would oppose migration and invasion.

25. B — Strong cytokeratin staining Step 1-style

Reasoning pathway: Cytokeratin is an epithelial marker used by immunohistochemistry to identify epithelial lineage and support a diagnosis of carcinoma.

Important distractor distinction: PAS, acid-fast, elastic, and reticulin stains highlight specific substances or organisms rather than specifically establishing epithelial lineage.
