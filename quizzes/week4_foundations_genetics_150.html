Part II: Answer Key & Explanations

1. B — High GC contentIn-houseLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: GC-rich DNA has a higher melting temperature because G-C base pairs have three hydrogen bonds, compared with two for A-T pairs.

Important distractor distinction: Low salt actually makes annealing less favorable, and uracil is not a normal base in DNA.

2. B — High temperature and low saltIntermediateLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: High-stringency hybridization uses high temperature and low salt so that only nearly perfectly complementary sequences remain annealed.

Important distractor distinction: Low temperature and high salt lowers stringency and permits more mismatched hybrids to remain paired.

3. C — Decrease temperature and increase saltStep 1-styleLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Low-stringency conditions use lower temperature and higher salt, allowing some mismatched base pairing and detection of related sequences.

Important distractor distinction: High temperature and low salt produces high stringency and would preferentially eliminate imperfect hybrids.

4. B — ATP-dependent transient double-strand cleavage by DNA gyraseIntermediateLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: DNA gyrase is a bacterial type II topoisomerase that uses ATP and transient double-strand breaks to control supercoiling; fluoroquinolones inhibit it.

Important distractor distinction: Eukaryotic telomerase and histone-dependent chromatin regulation are not the bacterial targets described.

5. B — TelomereIntermediateLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Human telomeres contain repeated TTAGGG sequences and terminate in a 3' overhang that protects chromosome ends.

Important distractor distinction: Centromeres are repetitive chromosome-segregation regions, whereas nucleosomes are DNA-histone packaging units.

6. B — VNTRs and STRsStep 1-styleLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Minisatellites (VNTRs) and microsatellites (STRs) vary in repeat number among individuals and are useful for DNA fingerprinting.

Important distractor distinction: CpG islands regulate transcription and are not the variable tandem-repeat markers emphasized for fingerprinting.

7. A — Expansion of microsatellite repeatsStep 1-styleLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Huntington disease and Fragile X syndrome are examples of disease caused by expansion of short tandem, or microsatellite, repeats.

Important distractor distinction: Their molecular mechanisms differ in detail, but the lecture groups both under trinucleotide-repeat expansion disorders.

8. B — Promoter DNA methylationIntermediateLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Methylation of CpG-rich promoter regions is generally associated with gene silencing because it blocks factor binding and promotes condensed chromatin.

Important distractor distinction: Histone acetylation favors open euchromatin and increased transcription, the opposite effect.

9. C — Neutralization of lysine charge and more open chromatinIntermediateLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Histone acetylation neutralizes positively charged lysine residues, weakens histone-DNA interactions, and favors transcriptionally active euchromatin.

Important distractor distinction: Condensation and silencing are favored by deacetylation or promoter DNA methylation.

10. B — H2A, H2B, H3, H4In-houseLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: The nucleosome core contains an octamer of two each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4.

Important distractor distinction: Histone H1 is a linker histone that helps lock DNA onto nucleosomes but is not one of the four core histones.

11. C — Pre-mRNA splicingStep 1-styleLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Small nuclear RNAs are components of the splicing machinery that removes introns from eukaryotic pre-mRNA.

Important distractor distinction: tRNA delivers amino acids, rRNA is part of the ribosome, and XIST is a long non-coding RNA.

12. B — Light, loosely packed euchromatinIn-houseLecture 1: Nucleic Acids

Reasoning pathway: Euchromatin is less condensed, appears lighter on electron microscopy, and is transcriptionally active.

Important distractor distinction: Heterochromatin and Barr bodies are condensed and transcriptionally inactive.

13. A — Cargo binding to importinIntermediateLecture 2: Nucleus Structure & Cell Cycle

Reasoning pathway: Large nuclear proteins require a basic nuclear localization signal that is recognized by importin for active transport through nuclear pores.

Important distractor distinction: COPII and SRP participate in endomembrane trafficking rather than nuclear import.

14. B — High Ran-GTP in the nucleus and high Ran-GDP in the cytoplasmStep 1-styleLecture 2: Nucleus Structure & Cell Cycle

Reasoning pathway: Nuclear transport directionality depends on high Ran-GTP in the nucleus and high Ran-GDP in the cytoplasm.

Important distractor distinction: RanGEF maintains nuclear Ran-GTP, whereas RanGAP promotes GTP hydrolysis in the cytoplasm.

15. B — Exportin and Ran-GTPIntermediateLecture 2: Nucleus Structure & Cell Cycle

Reasoning pathway: For nuclear export, exportin binds the cargo and Ran-GTP inside the nucleus; cytoplasmic GTP hydrolysis then dissociates the complex.

Important distractor distinction: Importin is used primarily for nuclear import.

16. B — Outer nuclear membraneIn-houseLecture 2: Nucleus Structure & Cell Cycle

Reasoning pathway: The outer nuclear membrane is continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum, and the perinuclear space is continuous with the ER lumen.

Important distractor distinction: The nuclear lamina is an intermediate-filament meshwork beneath the inner membrane, not a membrane continuous with the ER.

17. D — 5S rRNAIntermediateLecture 2: Nucleus Structure & Cell Cycle
