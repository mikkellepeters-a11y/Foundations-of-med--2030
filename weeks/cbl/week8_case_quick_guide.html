Week 8 CBL Case - Quick Guide

Foundations of Medicine • CBL • Acute Leukemia / Pediatric Critical Illness
Master chain: acute leukemia → marrow failure + blasts → severe anemia / thrombocytopenia / immunosuppression → shock, DIC, infection, and treatment-related tumor lysis become major immediate threats.

Acid–Base + Tachypnea

Case pattern
Acidic pH + low bicarbonate + low pCO₂ from hyperventilation = metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation.
Minute ventilation
VE = tidal volume × respiratory rate. Increasing ventilation lowers CO₂ and helps raise pH.
Why tachypneic? Severe anemia reduces oxygen-carrying capacity; severe metabolic acidosis also drives compensatory hyperventilation.

Cardiac / Respiratory Clues in Trisomy 21

FindingFactoid
Bilateral cracklesCan reflect left-heart dysfunction → pulmonary venous backup → pulmonary edema.
Hepatomegaly / JVDCan reflect right-sided dysfunction.
Congenital heart diseaseAV septal, ventricular septal, and atrial septal defects are common; source notes ≈50% of babies with trisomy 21 are affected.
ScreeningAll babies with trisomy 21 should receive an echocardiogram before nursery discharge.

Leukemia Lab Pattern

Red flags
Leukocytosis with circulating blasts • severe anemia with low reticulocyte response • thrombocytopenia • elevated anion-gap acidosis • elevated lactate • transaminitis.
Coagulation red flags
↑ D-dimer • prolonged PT • ↓ fibrinogen → think DIC.
Reticulocyte pearl: in severe anemia, a “normal-range” corrected reticulocyte count may still be inappropriately low, supporting marrow suppression.

DIC

DIC = systemic coagulation activation + microvascular thrombosis + simultaneous consumption of platelets and clotting proteins → organ dysfunction + bleeding risk.
Lab / conceptSource point
PlateletsLow from consumption.
D-dimer / fibrin markersIncreased.
PTProlonged.
FibrinogenLow.
TreatmentTreat the underlying condition. Blood components may be needed for major bleeding / high hemorrhage risk.

Fluids + Marrow + AML + TLS

Pediatric IV Fluid Phases

PhasePurpose
ResuscitativeRestore tissue perfusion and prevent/mitigate end-organ injury.
TitrationTransition from boluses toward maintenance; reassess repletion and fluid trajectory.
MaintenanceAchieve homeostatic balance between ongoing needs and losses.
ConvalescentStop exogenous fluids and return to intrinsic fluid regulation.
AAP maintenance pearl: patients 28 days–18 years requiring maintenance IVFs should receive isotonic solutions with appropriate KCl and dextrose to decrease hyponatremia risk.

Bone Marrow Morphology

Atypical megakaryocytes
Too numerous, darker nuclei than expected, and less cytoplasm than normal.
Immature mononuclear cells
High nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio • prominent nucleoli • irregular nuclear contours • variable pink-to-clear cytoplasm.

AML Essentials

FeatureHigh-yield fact
DefinitionClonal expansion of immature myeloid blasts in blood and marrow → ineffective erythropoiesis + marrow failure.
PopulationMost common leukemia among adults in the supplied document.
Risk factorsRadiation • prior chemotherapy • smoking.
Can evolve fromMyeloproliferative disorders • MDS • PNH • aplastic anemia.
7+3 regimenContinuous cytarabine ×7 days + anthracycline on days 1–3.
Case cautionAnthracycline deserves special consideration when cardiomyopathy is present.

Tumor Lysis Syndrome

Cell contents spill → ↑ uric acid + ↑ K⁺ + ↑ phosphate → ↓ Ca²⁺ → renal insufficiency, arrhythmias, seizures, and potentially multiorgan failure.

Pediatric Cardiomyopathy Snapshot

TypeSource pearl
DilatedProgressive; major indication for transplant; substantial 5-year mortality/transplant burden.
HypertrophicLV hypertrophy with nondilated chambers; can cause HF, pain, arrhythmia, sudden death.
RestrictiveLeast common, poorest prognosis; can be linked to amyloidosis, metabolic disease, sarcoid, scleroderma, parasites.
LV noncompactionDeep intertrabecular recesses + compacted/noncompacted myocardial layers; may cause arrhythmia, thromboembolism, or HF.
