Week 9 CBL Case - Quick Guide

Foundations of Medicine • CBL • HIV Testing, OIs, ART, and CMV
Master chain: screen correctly → interpret test performance and window periods → confirm HIV using the testing algorithm → stage immune risk by CD4 count → prevent/treat OIs → understand ART mechanisms and IRIS → use CMV-active therapy when indicated.

Interpreter Best Practices

Limited English proficiency: the supplied source says encounters should always use a trained medical interpreter—not family, untrained fluent staff, or another untrained individual.
Encounter setup: physician face-to-face with patient • interpreter next to or slightly behind patient • speak directly to the patient • maintain eye contact • keep questions simple and ask one at a time.

Test Performance

MetricMeaning / source value
SensitivityTP/(TP+FN). OraQuick value in source: 99.3%. Percentage positive when HIV is truly present.
SpecificityTN/(TN+FP). OraQuick value in source: 99.8%. Percentage negative when HIV is absent.
PPVTP/(TP+FP). Probability that a positive result is truly positive.
NPVAnalogous concept for negative results.
Prevalence matters: PPV and NPV depend on disease prevalence; sensitivity and specificity are intrinsic characteristics of the test in this discussion.

Testing Algorithm

4th-gen HIV-1/2 immunoassay → if positive, HIV-1/HIV-2 antibody differentiation immunoassay → if negative/indeterminate, HIV RNA testing.

IRIS

TypeMeaning
Paradoxical IRISWorsening/exacerbation of a recognized and treated OI after ART starts.
Unmasking IRISImmune recovery reveals a previously subclinical infection.
Caution: neurologic OIs such as CNS toxoplasmosis can produce dangerous inflammatory responses; the source notes corticosteroids may be considered in those cases.

CMV Therapy

Ganciclovir: guanosine analog that preferentially inhibits viral DNA polymerase. CMV lacks the viral thymidylate kinase needed to activate acyclovir-family agents.
Activation
Ganciclovir is converted to ganciclovir-monophosphate by a CMV viral kinase.
Valganciclovir
Prodrug of ganciclovir with better oral bioavailability; preferred for outpatient PO therapy.
Toxicity: myelosuppression + nephrotoxicity; source notes these agents are comparatively more toxic than acyclovir.
