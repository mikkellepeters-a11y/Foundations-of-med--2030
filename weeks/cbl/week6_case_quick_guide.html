Week 6 CBL Case - Quick Guide

Foundations of Medicine • CBL • Breast / BRCA Case
Master workflow: know the anatomy → evaluate a palpable mass with imaging → biopsy suspicious lesions with core tissue → interpret ER/PR/HER2 → distinguish in situ vs invasive patterns → stage with TNM.

Breast Anatomy

Mammary glands
Modified sweat glands made of lobules. Each lobule drains through a lactiferous duct that converges toward the nipple.
Lactiferous sinus
Dilated portion of a lactiferous duct deep to the areola where a small droplet of milk accumulates.
Lymph pearl: most retroareolar and deep interlobar breast lymph drains to the ipsilateral axillary lymph nodes, especially from the lateral breast quadrants.

Palpable Breast Mass Algorithm

FindingHigh-yield action
Likely benign mass / cystObservation for 1–2 menstrual cycles is an option.
Persistent or not clearly benignImaging is indicated. Physical exam alone is not specific enough.
Any palpable abnormalitySource recommends a breast ultrasound.
At-risk patientAdd bilateral mammography: all women >40, women <40 who are high risk (BRCA/strong family history), or suspicious ultrasound findings.
Simple cyst / lymph node / lipoma on USDefinite benign result → usual care.
Complicated cyst / probable fibroadenoma or hematomaRepeat US in 6 months; follow for 2 years to demonstrate stability.
Suspicious US or mammogramCore needle biopsy.
Why core, not FNA? If malignancy is present, core tissue can be used for biomarker testing.

Screening Pearl

USPSTF in supplied document
Biennial screening mammography for women 40–74 (B recommendation).
ACOG in supplied document
Average-risk women begin mammography at 40; every 1 or 2 years using shared decision-making; continue until at least 75.

ER / PR / HER2

MarkerMeaning in source
ERPrognostic + predictive. Strong ER expression → better short-term prognosis; ≥1% expression predicts greater likelihood of endocrine-therapy benefit.
PRPrognostic marker. Lower PR expression → worse outcomes, but endocrine benefit may still occur.
HER2Prognostic + predictive. Marker of aggressive biology / poor prognosis without appropriate targeted therapy; predicts survival benefit from HER2-targeted therapy added to chemotherapy.

Histology + Communication + Stage

DCIS

Ductal carcinoma in situ: malignant epithelial cells proliferate within the mammary ducts but do not invade the basement membrane or surrounding stroma; myoepithelial cells are retained.
DCIS patternSource feature
ComedoProminent central necrosis; necrotic material frequently calcifies; more often associated with invasion.
CribriformBack-to-back glands without intervening stroma.
MicropapillarySmall tufts projecting into lumina without fibrovascular cores.
PapillaryIntraluminal projections with fibrovascular cores.
SolidCells fill/distend involved spaces without significant necrosis, fenestrations, or papillations.

Major Invasive Patterns

Infiltrating ductal carcinoma
Most common invasive breast cancer. Begins in ducts; hard gray-white stellate masses; cords/nests of tumor cells; tends to metastasize earlier than lobular carcinoma.
Infiltrating lobular carcinoma
Second most common. Begins in lobules; often lacks E-cadherin staining; small cells invade in single-file pattern; frequent bilaterality / multicentricity.

Other High-Yield Patterns

TypeFactoid
MucinousOlder patients; soft gelatinous well-circumscribed tumor; tumor nests in extracellular mucus; favorable prognosis.
TubularWell-formed elongated tubules; low-grade cytology; favorable prognosis / low metastatic likelihood.
Inflammatory carcinomaClinical presentation, not morphologic subtype. Dermal lymphatic blockage by tumor → lymphedema → peau d’orange; aggressive, often node-positive.
Medullary featuresHigh-grade syncytial pattern with dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate; more common in younger patients and in BRCA1 mutation carriers.

Risk Factors

Source list: increasing age • female sex • white race • postmenopausal obesity • high endogenous estrogen • early menarche • later menopause • nulliparity • increasing age at first pregnancy • prior DCIS / invasive breast cancer • family history (especially first-degree relative diagnosed young) • inherited genetic mutations.

Grade vs Stage

Grade
How abnormal tumor cells/tissue look on histology compared with healthy cells; reflects the tumor’s aggressive potential.
Stage
How large the primary tumor is and how far disease has spread.
TNM: T = primary tumor type/size • N = regional lymph-node involvement • M = distant metastasis.
