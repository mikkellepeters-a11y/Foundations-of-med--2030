Week 7 CBL Case - Quick Guide

Foundations of Medicine • CBL • Angioedema + Medical Interpretation
Fast split: urticaria/pruritus + allergic trigger → think histamine/mast cells. Recurrent swelling without wheals, poor antihistamine response, ACE-inhibitor exposure, or family history → think bradykinin.

Acute Angioedema Differential

CategoryHigh-yield clue from source
Not angioedemaOther edema syndromes such as lymphedema.
Acute allergic angioedemaMast-cell mediator release via type I immediate hypersensitivity or other mast-cell activation; common triggers include medications, venoms, and foods; usually associated with urticaria.
Drug reactionACE inhibitors can cause angioedema on a non-IgE-mediated basis.
Hereditary / related angioedemaBradykinin release engaging the B2 receptor.

Medical Interpreter Rules

Routine care
Use a certified medical interpreter to maximize accuracy, impartiality, and correct handling of specialized vocabulary.
Emergency exception
If no interpreter is immediately available, a friend or family member may help acutely until formal interpreting services can be engaged.
During the encounter: face and speak directly to the patient • normal tone • eye contact • first-person speech • ask the patient to repeat instructions/explanations • do not direct questions to the interpreter.

Histamine-Directed Medications

Drug classSource fact
CorticosteroidsHydrocortisone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone. Reduce inflammation and immune-system activity.
H1 antagonistsDiphenhydramine + loratadine.
Sedation pearlDiphenhydramine is more lipophilic, crosses the BBB more readily, and is more sedating than loratadine.
H2 antagonistsFamotidine + ranitidine.

Receptor Signaling

H1
Gq/11 → phospholipase C → calcium / protein kinase C. Source tissues: smooth muscle of gut, bronchi, uterus, and vasculature.
H2
Gs/Gq. Gs → cAMP; Gq → DAG/IP3. Source tissues: gastric parietal cells, vascular smooth muscle, hepatocytes.

Mechanism + HAE Workup

Corticosteroid Mechanism

Lipophilic steroid → intracellular glucocorticoid receptor → receptor complex enters nucleus → transcriptional changes → downstream effects over hours to days.

When to Pivot Away from “Allergic” Angioedema

Clue: lack of prompt response to antihistamines and corticosteroids should raise concern for hereditary angioedema in the supplied case framework.

Type 1 vs Type 2 HAE

Type 1
Heterozygous SERPING1 variant → low C1-INH level.
Type 2
Heterozygous SERPING1 variant → low C1-INH function.
