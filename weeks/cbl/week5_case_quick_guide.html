Week 5 CBL Case - Quick Guide

Foundations of Medicine • CBL • Li-Fraumeni Syndrome
Master framework: germline TP53 pathogenic variant → impaired p53 tumor-suppressor response → susceptibility to a second hit → damaged cells escape arrest/apoptosis → multiple early cancers + lifelong surveillance.

Core Identity

Li-Fraumeni syndrome
Cancer-predisposition syndrome caused by an inherited or de novo germline pathogenic TP53 variant.
Inheritance
Autosomal dominant. Constitutional inherited or de novo variants carry a 50% offspring risk in the workup table.

Cancer Spectrum

Predominant cancers in the wrap-up: adrenocortical carcinoma • breast cancer • CNS tumors • osteosarcoma • soft-tissue sarcomas.

When to Suspect / Test

Classic pattern shown
Proband with sarcoma before 45 + first-degree relative with cancer before 45 + another first-/second-degree relative in same lineage with cancer before 45 or sarcoma at any age.
Standalone red flags in testing slide
ACC, choroid plexus carcinoma, or embryonal anaplastic rhabdomyosarcoma at any age • breast cancer <31 • pediatric hypodiploid ALL.
Chompret logic: LFS-spectrum tumor before 46 plus qualifying family history, or multiple tumors with at least two in the LFS spectrum and the first before 46, can prompt testing.

Diagnosing LFS

StepSource emphasis
Clinical vs molecularBoth matter.
Molecular testingSingle-gene vs multigene testing; sequencing vs copy-number analysis.
ReferralGenetics referral for counseling and education.
Do not assumeNot every reported TP53 mutation is germline.
InterrogateVariant allele frequency (VAF) + clinical/family history + tissue source.
Confirm constitutionalityTest non-hematopoietic tissue such as skin fibroblasts.

Diagnostic Traps + Surveillance

TP53 Result Pattern: What Does It Mean?

PatternInterpretation / pearl
Blood/saliva + and fibroblast +; one parent +Inherited LFS; offspring risk 50%.
Blood/saliva + and fibroblast +; both parents –De novo LFS; offspring risk 50%.
Positive in more than one tissue; parents –Constitutional post-zygotic mosaicism; expert LFS input recommended.
Blood +, fibroblast –Think blood-only hematologic neoplasm / precursor condition or clonal hematopoiesis rather than automatically calling LFS.
Advanced cancer, blood +, fibroblast –, tumor +Consider tumor-derived ctDNA / circulating tumor-cell interference.
CHIP trap: misinterpreting clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential as Li-Fraumeni can lead to unwarranted clinical interventions.
Other technical cautions: tissue source • ctDNA • VAF • non-quantitative assays / capture efficiency • orthogonal testing when needed.

Management Pearls

Radiation
Avoid therapeutic RT when possible unless benefit outweighs secondary-malignancy risk; minimize diagnostic radiation without sacrificing accuracy.
Breast cancer
Mastectomy is preferred over lumpectomy/radiation when appropriate to reduce radiation-induced sarcoma risk.
Cytotoxic chemotherapy
Chemoresistance may occur and secondary-malignancy risk matters, but treatment efficacy takes priority.
Care model
Because LFS is rare and complex, the wrap-up favors care at centers with LFS expertise.

Phenotype + Patient Experience

Variant biology: penetrance and expressivity differ; the speaker notes place six common loss-of-function / dominant-negative TP53 variants in the DNA-binding domain.
Do not miss the person: the wrap-up explicitly highlights health-related anxiety, financial toxicity, and disease burdens that may not be obvious on first encounter. Mental-health screening and psychosocial support are part of management.
Last 30 seconds: TP53 at 17p13.1 • AD • p53 = arrest/apoptosis after DNA damage • LFS spectrum = ACC/breast/CNS/osteosarcoma/soft tissue • do not call every blood TP53 variant germline • check VAF + fibroblasts • annual whole-body/brain MRI • breast MRI 20–29, MRI+mammogram 30–75 • minimize radiation • pediatric exam q3–4 months.
