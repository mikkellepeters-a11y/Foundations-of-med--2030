Slides 3–5: HMG-CoA Reductase Is Regulated on Three Timescales

Selected figure from slide 4. Hormonal phosphorylation, feedback, and statin inhibition converge on HMG-CoA reductase.

The lecture's central idea is not the entire synthetic pathway—it is how the cell decides whether cholesterol synthesis should be active. HMG-CoA reductase is the rate-limiting enzyme and integrates acute hormonal regulation, feedback by sterol abundance, and pharmacologic inhibition.

Short term

Insulin favors dephosphorylation and activation; glucagon favors phosphorylation and inactivation.

Long term

Low cholesterol activates the SREBP-SCAP trafficking/cleavage pathway, increasing transcription of cholesterol-synthesis genes.

Feedback

High cholesterol suppresses further synthesis and keeps SREBP signaling restrained.

Drug control

Statins directly inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and secondarily increase hepatic LDL-receptor expression.

Selected figure from slide 5. SREBP-SCAP-INSIG translates ER cholesterol abundance into transcriptional output.
SREBP logic
Low ER cholesterol → SREBP-SCAP travels to Golgi → proteolytic activation → nuclear transcription. High ER cholesterol → INSIG-mediated ER retention.

Slides 6–7: Bile Acids Are the Major Disposal Pathway for Cholesterol

Selected figure from slide 6. CYP7A1 initiates bile-acid synthesis; primary bile acids are subsequently conjugated.

The sterol nucleus of cholesterol cannot simply be oxidized away for energy. A major disposal route is conversion to bile acids. Cholesterol 7α-hydroxylase (CYP7A1) is the key rate-limiting step.

StageMeaning
Primary bile acidsCholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid arise from cholesterol.
ConjugationGlycine or taurine increases water compatibility and detergent function.
Secondary bile acidsIntestinal bacteria modify primary species.
Enterohepatic circulationMost bile acids are reabsorbed and returned to liver.
Fecal lossThe small unrecovered fraction is a net route of cholesterol elimination.

Slides 8–9: Cholesterol Gallstones Are a Solubility Failure

Selected figure from slide 8. When cholesterol exceeds the solubilizing capacity of bile salts/phospholipids, precipitation becomes favorable.

Cholesterol stays dissolved in bile because bile salts and phospholipids organize it into soluble structures. Gallstones develop when that balance is lost and bile becomes supersaturated with cholesterol.

Think ratio, not absolute amount. The problem is excess cholesterol relative to the molecules that keep it soluble.
