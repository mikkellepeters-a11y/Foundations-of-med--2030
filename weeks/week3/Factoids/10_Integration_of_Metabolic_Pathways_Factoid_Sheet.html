Integration of Metabolic Pathways - Factoid Sheet

Week 3 • Wednesday Lecture 10 • current deck primary
Master question: What fuel is available, what tissue is using it, and what is the insulin:glucagon state?

Organ Rules

TissueHigh yield
LiverMaintains blood glucose; glycogenolysis/GNG; FA metabolism; ketogenesis; urea synthesis.
AdiposeTG storage fed; FFA + glycerol release fasting.
MuscleOwn glycogen only; glucose/FA use; amino-acid contribution during fasting.
BrainGlucose normally; ketones in prolonged starvation; not circulating FFA.
RBCGlucose only, anaerobic glycolysis.
HeartContinuously aerobic; strong FA use, can use glucose/ketones.

Hormonal Switch

Fed
High insulin / low glucagon → dephosphorylated anabolic pathways, glucose use + storage.
Fasting
Low insulin / high glucagon → cAMP/PKA phosphorylation, catabolic pathways.

Fed State

  • Use glucose as dominant fuel.
  • Replenish glycogen.
  • Excess carbohydrate → FA/TG/cholesterol storage.
  • Protein synthesis favored.

Fasting / Starvation

StageLiverWhole-body consequence
Early fastingGlycogenolysisBlood glucose maintained
Later fastingGluconeogenesis + FA oxidationGlycerol/AAs supply carbon; FA oxidation supplies ATP
Prolonged starvationKetogenesis prominentBrain increasingly uses ketones; protein is spared
Critical integration rule: even-chain fatty acids do not provide net glucose carbon. Their oxidation powers gluconeogenesis instead.
Clue: prolonged fasting + very low glucose + absent ketones → suspect FA-oxidation / ketogenesis defect.
