Slides 3–5: Lipoproteins Solve a Solubility Problem

Selected figure from slide 3. The particle's hydrophobic core carries triglycerides and cholesterol esters while its amphipathic shell interfaces with plasma.

Free fatty acids can travel on albumin, but triglycerides and cholesterol species need lipoprotein particles. The more triglyceride-rich a particle is, the larger and less dense it tends to be; increasing protein content raises density.

Transport split
Free fatty acids → albumin. Complex lipids → lipoproteins.

Lipoprotein lipase (LPL) sits on capillary endothelium and hydrolyzes triglycerides from circulating TG-rich particles. ApoC-II is the activator that makes that unloading step work efficiently.

Slides 6–15: ApoB Particles Move Lipid Outward From Gut and Liver

Selected figure from slide 12. The chylomicron pathway connects intestinal ApoB-48 packaging, LPL unloading, and ApoE-mediated remnant clearance.

Dietary long-chain lipid leaves the intestine in chylomicrons built around ApoB-48. Endogenous lipid leaves the liver in VLDL built around ApoB-100. Both use LPL to unload triglyceride, but their final fates differ.

Exogenous route
Intestine → chylomicron (ApoB-48) → gains ApoC-II/ApoE → LPL removes TG → ApoE-rich remnant → liver.
Selected figure from slide 13. VLDL loses triglyceride, becomes IDL, and either returns to liver or is remodeled further into LDL.
Endogenous route: liver → VLDL (ApoB-100) → LPL → IDL → hepatic clearance via ApoE or further processing → LDL.
Selected figure from slide 15. LDL receptor-mediated uptake depends on ApoB-100 and allows receptor recycling after endocytosis.

Slides 16–19: HDL Moves Cholesterol Back Toward the Liver

Selected figure from slide 17. Nascent HDL collects cholesterol from peripheral cells through transporters and esterifies it during maturation.

ApoA-I starts the HDL pathway. ABCA1/ABCG1 help move cholesterol out of peripheral cells, and LCAT esterifies the free cholesterol so it can be sequestered inside the HDL core.

Reverse transport
ApoA-I → ABCA1/ABCG1 efflux → LCAT → mature HDL → SR-B1 direct hepatic delivery and/or CETP exchange.
