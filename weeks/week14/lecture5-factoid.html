Cutaneous Neoplasms — Factoid Sheet

Week 14 · Lecture 5 · Powell · Condensed Week 13-style main-lecture review
Diagnostic spine: epidermal horn pseudocysts = SK · follicle infundibulum vs isthmus = keratin/granular layer · HPV = koilocytes · AK basal atypia → SCC in situ full-thickness → invasive SCC dermis · BCC palisading/retraction · melanoma Breslow depth.

Seborrheic keratosis

  • Benign pigmented, well-demarcated exophytic lesion; can mimic melanoma.
  • Acanthosis + hyperkeratosis + horn pseudocysts; bland keratinocytes; variable melanin.

Follicular cysts

  • Epidermoid/EIC = infundibulum: granular layer present; loose lamellar keratin.
  • Pilar/trichilemmal = isthmus: NO granular layer; dense pilar keratin; adult scalp.

Warts & skin tags

  • Verruca (HPV): hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, koilocytosis (perinuclear halo).
  • Acrochordon: benign polypoid outgrowth of near-normal skin.

AK → SCC continuum

  • Actinic keratosis: UV; basal epidermal atypia + parakeratosis; may become SCC.
  • SCC in situ: full-thickness atypia; intact basement membrane.
  • Invasive SCC: dermal invasion + keratin pearls; immunosuppression major risk.

Basal cell carcinoma

  • Most common skin cancer; slow, locally destructive, metastasis very rare.
  • Histology: blue basaloid nests, high N:C, peripheral palisading, stromal retraction + mucin.
  • Superficial/nodular = nonaggressive; infiltrative/morpheaform/micronodular = aggressive patterns.

Merkel cell carcinoma

  • Rare, aggressive cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma; pink/purple nodule.
  • Polyomavirus/UV association; small round blue cells, salt-and-pepper chromatin; metastases common.

Ordinary melanocytic nevi

  • Junctional: junction only. Compound: junction + dermis. Intradermal: dermis only.
  • Congenital: deep around adnexa/vessels; larger lesions ↑ melanoma risk.

Named nevus variants

  • Spitz: pediatric pink/brown papule; large epithelioid + spindle cells mimic melanoma.
  • Blue: heavily pigmented dermal dendritic/spindle cells, no nests.
  • Halo: depigmentation from lymphocytic nevus destruction.

Dysplastic nevus

  • Often >5 mm, irregular edges/pigment; Clark nevus.
  • Rete ridge fusion, lamellar fibrosis, atypical nuclei; familial AD syndrome ↑ melanoma risk.

Melanoma red flags

  • ABCDE: asymmetry, borders, color, diameter >6mm, enlargement.
  • Histology: poor circumscription, confluent growth, pagetoid spread, nuclear atypia, lack of maturation.
  • Breslow thickness = #1 prognosis; ulceration worsens stage.

Melanoma variants

  • Superficial spreading/low CSD: BRAF, pagetoid, younger trunk/extremities.
  • Lentigo maligna/high CSD: older head/neck, long radial phase.
  • Acral lentiginous: palm/sole/nail unit; not driven by UV.

Soft-tissue lesions

  • Dermatofibroma: firm leg nodule, dimple; collagen trapping.
  • Neurofibroma: soft papule, wavy spindle nuclei; many may signal NF1.
  • Hemangioma: red/blue; cherry, infantile, congenital, pyogenic granuloma.

Adnexal: FYI only

  • Professor explicitly says not to memorize detailed catalog.
  • Pilomatricoma: child head/neck; syringoma: periocular tiny papules.
  • Sebaceous tumors → consider Muir–Torre / mismatch repair if multiple, extracephalic, age <65.
