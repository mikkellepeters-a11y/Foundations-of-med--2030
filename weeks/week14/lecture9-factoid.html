Musculoskeletal Imaging Basics — Factoid

Week 14 · Lecture 9 · Dr. Brandon Bentley · Condensed Week 13-style rapid review
Decision spine: X-ray first → MRI for soft tissue, marrow/stress or occult injury → CT for complex bone/detail. Read every film with ABCDs, report fractures precisely, and never rely on one view.

Choose the modality

  • X-ray: fracture, calcific tendon, foreign body, joint degeneration; usually first-line.
  • MRI: ligaments, tendons, menisci, marrow edema, occult scaphoid, stress reaction.
  • CT: complex fractures, cortical bone, occult bony detail, CT arthrogram.

X-ray ABCDs

  • A Adequacy + Alignment
  • B Bone cortex + density
  • C Cartilage / joint space
  • D Disc/joint space + soft tissue: edema, effusion, calcification.

Fracture vocabulary

  • Report bone → site → pattern → alignment.
  • Open vs closed; displaced vs nondisplaced; angulation, shortening, rotation, apposition.
  • Simple vs comminuted/segmental; transverse/oblique/spiral; avulsion; intra-articular; compression/impaction/pathologic.

Why multiple views

  • AP/PA, lateral, oblique as applicable.
  • Use perpendicular views for hidden fracture/angulation; one projection can miss displacement.

Special radiograph views

  • Shoulder: AP, scapular Y, Grashey, Zanca, West Point, Garth.
  • Wrist: PA, oblique, lateral, carpal tunnel, clenched fist.
  • Knee: AP, lateral, Merchant/sunrise, weightbearing, Rosenberg.
  • Ankle: AP, mortise, lateral ± stress.

MRI recognition

  • Magnet + RF signal from protons.
  • T1: fat white; water relatively dark.
  • T2: water bright (fat often also bright without suppression).
  • Axial / sagittal / coronal anatomy; check implant/metal safety.

CT recognition

  • Multiple X-ray projections → cross-sectional reconstruction.
  • Excellent for complex cortical and intra-articular bony detail.
  • Not a first-choice replacement for MRI when assessing menisci/ligaments.

Clinical knee questions

  • Older, mechanical aching: weightbearing knee X-ray → OA features.
  • Pivot + pop + immediate swelling: X-ray first, MRI for suspected ACL disruption.

Clinical wrist & shoulder

  • Snuffbox tenderness, X-ray negative: treat as occult scaphoid; thumb spica, repeat films/MRI (lecture).
  • Overhead shoulder pain: X-ray then MRI for suspected soft-tissue issue.
  • Repeated dislocation: Hill–Sachs = humeral head; Bankart = glenoid rim; CT defines bone injury.

Stress response

  • Runner with progressive tibial pain → plain films initially; MRI best detects/grades early bone stress.
  • Lecture model: repetitive stress → osteoclastic activity exceeds repair → bone weakens.
