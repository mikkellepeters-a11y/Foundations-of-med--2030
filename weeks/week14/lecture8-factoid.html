Drug & Infection Hypersensitivity — Factoid

Week 14 · Lecture 8 · Dr. Davis · Condensed Week 13-style study sheet
Sort by TIME + MORPHOLOGY + SICKNESS: wheal <24h = urticaria · early fixed maculopapules/well = simple drug eruption · 2–6 weeks + sick = DRESS · acral true targets = EM · generalized atypical targets + mucosa + skin loss = SJS/TEN · exact recurrent location = fixed drug eruption.

Urticaria (hives)

  • Transient: each individual wheal <24 h; pink/flesh-colored, pruritic; can be annular/arcuate.
  • Mast cells → histamine/heparin → capillary leakage → dermal edema separating collagen.
  • Acute <6 wk; chronic >6 wk; physical forms (cold/sun/exercise); antihistamines, anti-IgE options.

Hypersensitivity timing

  • IgE-dependent: minutes–36 h (lecture).
  • Direct mast cell/complement: minutes–hours (aspirin).
  • Serum sickness: 4–12 days (antivenom).

Morbilliform drug eruption

  • Fixed blanchable macules + papules, can become confluent.
  • Usually not systemically ill; often <1 week after new drug; lasts ~1–2 wk.
  • Penicillins, sulfa, phenytoin, carbamazepine, allopurinol; EBV/HIV association.

DRESS: delayed + sick

  • 2–6 weeks after drug; severe T-cell-mediated reaction.
  • Facial edema, fever, lymphadenopathy, hepatitis, eosinophilia, atypical lymphocytes.
  • Anticonvulsants/sulfonamides; HLA/HHV-6 reactivation; RegiSCAR; fulminant hepatitis risk.

Erythema multiforme

  • 3-zone true targets, acral hands/feet; HSV common trigger.
  • Lecture minor: ≤1 mucosal site; major: ≥2, but usually not systemically sick like SJS.
  • MIRM = Mycoplasma-induced; RIME = infection-linked mucocutaneous eruption.

SJS / TEN

  • Flat atypical targets, purpuric blisters, hemorrhagic lips, multiple mucosae, sick patient.
  • SJS <10%; overlap 10–30%; TEN >30% skin loss.
  • Positive Nikolsky, necrotic keratinocytes, minimal dermal inflammation.
  • Drugs: sulfonamides, anticonvulsants, oxicam NSAIDs, allopurinol.

SJS/TEN management & risk

  • Stop offending drug; hospitalize / burn-level supportive care; ophthalmology.
  • Do not aggressively debride (lecture emphasis).
  • Sequelae: ocular damage, pigment changes, nail loss, esophageal/genital scarring.

Fixed drug eruption

  • Round, sharply defined erythematous/edematous plaques ± blister.
  • Returns to exactly same site on re-exposure; heals very dark.
  • Sulfas, tetracyclines, NSAIDs, barbiturates, carbamazepine; stop culprit.
