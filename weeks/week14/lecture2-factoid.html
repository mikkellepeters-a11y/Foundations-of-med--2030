Anterior Arm & Cubital Fossa — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Week 14 · Lecture 2 · M. Johnson · Course-first rapid review
Last-minute framework: anterior arm = biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis; musculocutaneous nerve (C5–C7); cubital fossa lateral → medial = TAN (biceps Tendon, brachial Artery, Median Nerve). Radial nerve sits lateral to TAN.

Humerus & elbow bony landmarks

  • Humerus: head, anatomical/surgical necks, greater/lesser tubercles, intertubercular groove, deltoid tuberosity, radial groove.
  • Distal humerus: capitulum ↔ radial head; trochlea ↔ trochlear notch of ulna.
  • Fossae: radial & coronoid anterior; olecranon posterior.
  • Ulna: olecranon, coronoid process, trochlear notch, radial notch, ulnar tuberosity.
  • Radius: head, neck, radial tuberosity (biceps insertion).

Arm fascial compartments

  • Brachial fascia + medial/lateral intermuscular septa partition compartments.
  • Anterior: biceps brachii, brachialis, coracobrachialis; brachial a./vv.; musculocutaneous, median & initially ulnar nerves.
  • Posterior: triceps; radial n. + profunda brachii a. (middle/radial collateral branches).
  • Functional contrast: anterior flexion/supination; posterior extension.

Biceps brachii

  • Short head: coracoid process; long head: supraglenoid tubercle.
  • Insertion: radial tuberosity; tendon gives bicipital aponeurosis.
  • Nerve: musculocutaneous.
  • Actions: forearm supination and elbow flexion (especially strong supinator with elbow flexed).
  • Reflex: biceps tendon reflex tests musculocutaneous nerve / C5–C6.

Brachialis & coracobrachialis

  • Brachialis: distal half of humerus → coronoid process & ulnar tuberosity; elbow flexion. Musculocutaneous n. plus radial n. contribution (lecture).
  • Coracobrachialis: coracoid process → middle third of humeral shaft; shoulder flexion/adduction; musculocutaneous n. pierces muscle.
  • Lesion: musculocutaneous injury → weak elbow flexion/supination and reduced lateral forearm sensation.

Major nerve paths

  • Musculocutaneous: lateral cord → pierces coracobrachialis → between biceps/brachialis → lateral antebrachial cutaneous n. (lateral forearm skin).
  • Median: no arm branches; alongside brachial a. to cubital fossa.
  • Ulnar: no arm branches; pierces medial septum → behind medial epicondyle → medial to olecranon.
  • Radial: posterior arm → through lateral septum → divides into superficial/deep branches near elbow.

Brachial artery & collateral circulation

  • Axillary → brachial: inferior border of teres major.
  • Branches: profunda brachii → radial + middle collateral; superior ulnar collateral (posterior to medial epicondyle with ulnar n.); inferior ulnar collateral (anterior).
  • Terminal: radial + ulnar arteries.
  • Elbow anastomosis: collateral ↔ recurrent arteries maintain joint perfusion during motion/compression.
  • Pulse: brachial artery medial to biceps tendon in cubital fossa.

Superficial & deep veins

  • Superficial: cephalic (lateral), basilic (medial), median cubital (connector; venipuncture).
  • Deep: radial + ulnar vv. → paired brachial vv.; brachial vv. + basilic v. → axillary v.
  • Safety: bicipital aponeurosis lies between superficial venous structures and deeper brachial a./median n.

Cubital fossa — boundaries & contents

  • Superior: epicondyle-to-epicondyle line; medial: pronator teres; lateral: brachioradialis.
  • Floor: brachialis + supinator.
  • Roof: brachial/antebrachial fascia + bicipital aponeurosis.
  • Lateral → medial TAN: biceps Tendon → brachial Artery → Median Nerve.
  • Radial n.: lateral part, separates into superficial and deep branches.

Biceps tendon pathology

  • Long-head tendinopathy: anterior shoulder pain + point tenderness in bicipital groove.
  • Ultrasound: abnormal thickened/heterogeneous long-head tendon and increased Doppler vascularity with inflammation (lecture image).
  • Long-head rupture: wear/degeneration → proximal tendon tear → Popeye deformity from distal muscle belly retraction.

High-yield anatomy associations

  • Surgical neck fracture → axillary nerve risk; midshaft radial groove → radial n. risk; medial epicondyle → ulnar n. risk. BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT
  • Radial head subluxation: annular ligament displacement after traction on pronated extended forearm. BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT
  • Understand where labeled bony structures/nerve courses should appear on imaging; slides list imaging as an objective but include no dedicated arm CT/MRI panel.
