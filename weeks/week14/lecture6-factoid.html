Skin & Soft Tissue Infections — Factoid Sheet

Week 14 · Lecture 6 · Fisher · Condensed Week 13-style review
Map: honey crust = impetigo · Dsg1 = bullous/SSSS · deep ulcer/scar = ecthyma · sharp peau d’orange = erysipelas · streak = lymphangitis · pus = drain · rapid fascial destruction = emergency surgery.

Depth & hallmark patterns

  • Impetigo = honey crust; ecthyma = deeper scarring ulcer.
  • Erysipelas = sharp margin/peau d’orange; lymphangitis = red streak and nodes.
  • Cellulitis = deep dermis/fat; abscess = fluctuant pus.

Nonbullous impetigo

  • S. aureus most common; S. pyogenes or mixed.
  • Few lesions → topical mupirocin/retapamulin/fusidic acid; widespread → culture and antibiotics (lecture).

Bullous impetigo / SSSS

  • S. aureus exfoliative toxins → Dsg1 cleavage.
  • Local bullae = bullous impetigo; circulating toxin → SSSS, widespread peeling, mucosa spared.

Ecthyma

  • S. pyogenes ± S. aureus; vesicle after bite/trauma → ulcer and scar.

Erysipelas & lymphangitis

  • Erysipelas: S. pyogenes, well-demarcated, peau d’orange, fever; PCN G per slides.
  • Lymphangitis: GAS, red proximal streak + tender lymph nodes.

Furuncles & carbuncles

  • S. aureus often MRSA; fluctuant tender abscess.
  • Warm compresses and incision/drainage; adjunct antibiotics by severity.

Hidradenitis suppurativa

  • Follicular occlusion → rupture → TNF-α/IL-17A inflammation, sinus tracts, scars.
  • Hurley I no tracts; II separated tracts; III diffuse interconnected tracts.
  • Risk factors: obesity, smoking, genetic predisposition; treatment escalates from topical clindamycin/tetracyclines to biologics and surgery.

Cellulitis & water injuries

  • Deep dermis/fat: usually S. pyogenes nonpurulent; S. aureus after trauma.
  • Freshwater = Aeromonas hydrophila (fluoroquinolone in slide).
  • Saltwater = Vibrio vulnificus, esp. liver disease (ceftriaxone + doxycycline in slide).

Preseptal vs orbital cellulitis

  • Preseptal: anterior to septum, normal eye movements.
  • Postseptal: proptosis, impaired EOM, ethmoid sinus origin, sight/life emergency.

Mimics & HSV

  • Pyoderma gangrenosum: undermined painful ulcer + IBD.
  • Sweet syndrome: febrile tender plaques/nodules.
  • Herpetic whitlow: recurrent grouped vesicles on finger; antiviral therapy (slide).

Necrotizing fasciitis I–V

  • I: polymicrobial; Fournier perineal variant.
  • II: S. pyogenes; penicillin G + clindamycin (slide).
  • III: Vibrio/Aeromonas water injuries; IV: community MRSA; V: Klebsiella.
  • All suspected cases: emergency debridement + IV antimicrobials.

Gas gangrene

  • Clostridium perfringens (~80%) after trauma/surgery.
  • Crepitus, toxicity, edema, noncontractile brick-red muscle.
  • Urgent surgery; penicillin G + clindamycin shown.

Brown recluse vs MRSA

  • Loxosceles sphingomyelinase → pain, necrosis, dry eschar.
  • Fluctuant purulent supposed spider bite often MRSA.
  • Dapsone appears in slide but is not standard routine therapy.
