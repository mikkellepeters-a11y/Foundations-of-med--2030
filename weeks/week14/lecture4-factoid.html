The Spectrum of Eczema — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Week 14 · Lecture 4 · Loretta Davis · Condensed course review
Framework: itchy + poorly defined + spongiosis → eczema; acute oozing/vesicles → chronic lichenification. Allergic = type IV; irritant = barrier injury. Atopic = itch + age-dependent distribution.

Eczema fundamentals

  • Inflammatory, intensely itchy, usually poorly defined.
  • Spongiosis = epidermal intercellular edema; can form vesicles.
  • Acute ooze → subacute crust → chronic lichenification.

Acute vs chronic histology

  • Acute: normal stratum corneum; no acanthosis; spongiosis, intraepidermal vesicles, papillary edema.
  • Chronic: hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, mild spongiosis, no vesicles; thickened vertical collagen streaks.
  • Itch → scratch/rub → lichenification.

Allergic contact dermatitis

  • Type IV T-cell reaction: sensitization + re-exposure.
  • Poison ivy: plant resin remains on nails, clothing, tools, pet fur; blister fluid is not contagious; mango skin cross-reacts.
  • Nickel: jewelry, snaps, buckles, tools; avoid exposure.
  • Patch test: 48 and 96 hours; preservatives/fragrances common.

Irritant contact dermatitis

  • No immune sensitization: direct barrier injury.
  • Dishpan hands, excessive sanitizer, diaper rash, lip-licking from saliva.
  • Reduce exposure; gentle cleansers, moisturizers, barrier creams.

Topical corticosteroids

  • Potency depends on molecule and vehicle, not percent concentration.
  • 0.05% clobetasol ≫ 2.5% hydrocortisone.
  • Ointment > cream > lotion > solution.
  • Thick palms/soles → high potency; trunk/extremities → medium (triamcinolone); face/folds/children → low (hydrocortisone).

Steroid pitfalls

  • Select usable vehicle: scalp solution/foam; folds lower-strength formulation.
  • Atrophy and permanent striae (new red, old silvery).
  • Perioral dermatitis, tinea incognito, possible adrenal suppression.
  • Avoid prolonged high-potency use.

Atopic dermatitis

  • Itch that rashes; asthma/allergies and sensitive skin association.
  • Infants = face; older children/adults = antecubital/popliteal creases.
  • Lichenification, infraorbital pleats, hyperlinear palms; stress/temperature/humidity triggers.
  • Gentle skin care; moisturizers ointment > cream > lotion; topical steroids or tacrolimus/pimecrolimus.

Biologics and infections

  • Dupilumab: IL-4/IL-13; tralokinumab: IL-13; nemolizumab: IL-31 receptor.
  • JAK inhibitors block cytokine signaling; significant infection, malignancy, CV and thrombosis risks.
  • S. aureus → impetiginized eczema; HSV → grouped eczema herpeticum lesions.

Characteristic patterns

  • Dyshidrotic: deep tapioca-like vesicles, lateral fingers > toes; palms/soles.
  • Nummular: coin-shaped subacute plaques, arms/legs.
  • Stasis: edema + erythema/scaling + brown lower legs ± ulcers; venous insufficiency; neomycin allergy.
  • Asteatotic: xerotic winter eczema, craquelé = cracked porcelain/riverbed.
  • Lichen simplex chronicus: scratch/rub → thick plaque; prurigo nodularis: picking → nodules.
