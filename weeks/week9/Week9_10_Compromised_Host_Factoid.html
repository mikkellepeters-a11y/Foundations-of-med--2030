Vascular Access Infection

Most common
Gram+ cocci, especially coagulase-negative staph.
Local clues
Erythema · warmth · tenderness · induration.
Central catheters
Deck: >40% of hospital bloodstream infections; mortality 12–25%.

CMI Defects

Causes shown
Primary immune disorders · malignancy · HIV · iatrogenic.
CMV syndrome
~2 mo after renal transplant; fever/malaise; mild ↑ liver enzymes; ↓ WBC/platelets; QNAT DNA. Mild valganciclovir; severe IV ganciclovir.
Other deck patterns
Miliary TB; cryptococcal meningitis. Crypto: liposomal AmB + flucytosine → fluconazole.

Exam Traps

Coag− staph ≠ S. aureus. Coag− staph is the common catheter organism; S. aureus pushes aggressive removal + metastatic/endocarditis evaluation.
Colonization ≠ invasive burn infection. The deck uses tissue burden + systemic sepsis findings for deep burn disease.
Humoral and splenic defects overlap. Both point to encapsulated bacteria; the spleen slide explicitly adds meningococcus.
CGD cue is mechanism-first. NADPH oxidase → absent respiratory burst → catalase-positive susceptibility.
