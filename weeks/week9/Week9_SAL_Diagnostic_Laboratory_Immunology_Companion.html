A1.1 · Measuring Antibody and Antigen

Clinical lab frame: the Dolen/Bickel deck emphasizes that U.S. clinical testing is tightly regulated through CMS/CLIA, with proficiency testing and on-site inspection. The teaching target is not memorizing every assay ever invented; it is recognizing what the common methods measure and how their results are reported.

ELISA / EIA

Can measure antibody or antigen qualitatively, semi-quantitatively, or quantitatively. The deck names four basic forms: direct, indirect, sandwich, and competitive. Modern clinical methods usually report quantitative mass units or semi-quantitative titers.

Traditional titers

Serial dilutions are tested until the signal disappears. The extinction point becomes the titer. A larger terminal dilution implies more antibody. The lecture uses IgM/IgG titers to reinforce that IgM tends to support recent/ongoing primary infection, whereas IgG may reflect prior infection or immunization.

Dolen deck, page 12. Direct ELISA example: antigen-coated well → enzyme-labeled detection antibody → substrate color change → compare with a standard curve.

Rate nephelometry

Used in the case to measure IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, and C4. Patient protein and a specific antibody form immune complexes; changing light scatter is measured and converted into a concentration.

Case application

Bobby’s deck results: IgG and IgA markedly low, IgM at the lower limit of normal, total IgE normal, C3/C4 normal, and CH50/AH50 normal.

Dolen deck, page 16. Rate nephelometry converts changing laser-light scatter from immune-complex formation into a quantitative result.
Anchor: ELISA/EIA = binding + signal generation. Nephelometry = immune complexes + light scatter. Titer = highest dilution that still gives the defined endpoint.

A1.2 · Allergy Testing: Sensitization Is Not the Same as Disease

Faculty warning: a positive allergen-specific IgE result by itself does not diagnose clinically relevant allergic disease. The deck repeatedly puts the history and clinical context ahead of the test.
History → physical examination → differential diagnosis → ordinary testing as indicated → revise differential → ask whether allergy testing adds clinically useful information.

Why test?

To determine whether clinically relevant allergen-specific IgE is demonstrable, guide specific avoidance, identify extracts for immunotherapy, or support epidemiologic studies.

Methods emphasized

Skin testing is presented as the method of choice for allergy specialists. Specific-IgE EIA is widely used clinically and reports quantitative values such as kU/L. Basophil histamine release is listed mainly as a research method.

Interpretation rule: allergy testing searches for specific IgE, not “allergy” as an abstract diagnosis. The same positive honeybee-specific IgE result can have very different meaning in a patient with pain only, a large local reaction, or systemic anaphylaxis.
Do not let the lab result outrank the patient. In this SAL, clinical relevance requires clinical context.

A1.3 · TREC Newborn Screening

T cell matures in thymus → TCR gene rearrangement → excised DNA byproducts form T-cell receptor excision circles (TRECs) → PCR can count TRECs in peripheral blood → low count signals impaired T-cell production.
Dolen deck, page 36. The lecture’s TREC logic: rearranging germline DNA during T-cell receptor formation produces excision circles that can be counted by PCR.

David case

Five-week-old infant with repeatedly very low TREC counts on newborn screening. The SAL then uses flow cytometry to move from a screening abnormality to a lymphocyte-subset phenotype.

Flow result in the case

The Dolen deck reports low CD3+ T-cell populations, preserved B-cell numbers, and low CD56+ NK cells, with low IgA/IgM and likely maternal IgG.

Exam trap: TREC is a screen for T-cell production, not a one-step etiologic diagnosis. A low value tells you that something is wrong with T-cell output; follow-up testing determines what.

A1.4–A1.5 · Flow Cytometry Deep Dive

The separate Bickel flow-cytometry deck is not a second SAL session. It is the technical deep dive for the flow objectives inside this same Diagnostic Laboratory Immunology SAL.

1 · Fluidics

Hydrodynamic focusing uses laminar flow, a central sample core, and faster sheath fluid to line cells up in single file for interrogation.

2 · Optics / Detection

Lasers provide light; lenses, mirrors, and filters focus/direct it; photodetectors capture scattered or emitted light.

3 · Electronics / Processing

Computer/software converts optical signals into digital event values and displays populations as histograms and dot plots.

Bickel deck, page 6. The three systems: fluidics, optics/detection, and electronics/processing.

Forward scatter (FSC)

Tracks cell size. Larger cells generate a higher FSC signal in the lecture’s simplified model.

Side scatter (SSC)

Tracks granularity / internal complexity. Fluorescent emissions from labeled antibodies are captured by dedicated detectors as additional channels.

Bickel deck, page 14. FSC is tied to size; SSC to granularity/complexity, with fluorescence adding marker-specific information.
Immunophenotyping: cells can be tagged with monoclonal antibodies conjugated to fluorophores. Binding to defined surface markers lets the instrument identify and quantify cell populations using multiple markers per cell.

Bickel deck, page 29. A two-fluorophore dot plot places individual events into regions based on the two measured marker signals.
Flow cytometry one-liner: line them up → shine light → measure scatter/fluorescence → turn each cell into an event → plot populations.
Faculty application: the flow deck applies lymphocyte-subset patterns to SCID phenotypes, including T−B+NK−, T−B−NK+, T−B+NK+, and T−B−NK− patterns. Treat this as an application of immunophenotyping, not as a separate new SAL objective.

A1.6 · Mitogen and Antigen Stimulation

Isolate lymphocytes → culture with or without stimulus → add tritiated thymidine → dividing cells incorporate label into DNA → harvest → scintillation counting → compare proliferation with control.

Mitogens

Plant lectins that nonspecifically activate lymphocytes. The deck emphasizes PHA, ConA, and PWM. PHA and ConA are shown as T-cell stimuli; PWM stimulates T and B cells.

Antigen stimulation

The same proliferation framework is used with specific antigens such as Candida and tetanus instead of mitogens. The Dolen deck explicitly states that these antigen stimulation assays evaluate B-cell function, not T-cell function; this companion preserves that faculty wording rather than silently changing it.

Dolen deck, page 42. PHA and ConA are listed as T-cell mitogens; PWM is the “poke both” anchor for T- and B-cell proliferation.
Interpreting suppressed proliferation: the deck associates very low responses with severe combined immunodeficiency, profound T-cell lymphopenia/complete DiGeorge syndrome, or advanced HIV disease. Partial or absent responses can also appear in milder combined defects, humoral deficiency, viral suppression, malnutrition, cancer, surgery, shock, or immunosuppressive-drug effects.

How the SAL Cases Tie the Lab Together

Bobby Bruton

Recurrent sinopulmonary infections + asthma/rhinitis → quantitative immunoglobulins/complement → allergy testing → normal lymphocyte subset counts by flow → normal mitogen/antigen stimulation. The deck ultimately frames Bobby as fitting loose criteria for CVID versus a much less likely hyper-IgM syndrome, with genetic testing as a next step.

David

Abnormal newborn TREC screen → low T-cell production signal → flow cytometry demonstrates a lymphocyte-subset abnormality. The case illustrates why a screening assay and an immunophenotyping assay answer different questions.

The SAL is really one diagnostic chain: What is present? How much? Is it clinically relevant? Which immune-cell populations are present? Are those cells functioning?
