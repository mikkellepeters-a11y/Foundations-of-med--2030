1 · What the Microbiome Is

Fisher's opening frame: ~30 trillion human cells coexist with ~39 trillion bacterial cells representing ~2,000 species; anaerobes dominate approximately 1000:1. Up to 90% belong to Bacteroidetes and Firmicutes.

Microbiota

Collection of microorganisms living in humans: bacteria, fungi, viruses, and others.

Microbiome

Microorganisms plus their collective genes, interactions, functions, products, and effects on the host. Fisher prefers this term for the course.

Health-promoting functions emphasized in the handout

Metabolizes otherwise indigestible plant carbohydrates.
Contributes to fat storage/regulation and synthesis of essential vitamins and amino acids.
Protects against invasive microbes and is crucial to innate/adaptive immune-system development.

2 · Mucosal Immune System

Localization: Peyer's patches in the ileum + abundant lymphoid tissue in the GI lamina propria + intraepithelial lymphocytes along the gut lining.

Barrier side

  • Inner mucus layer limits direct invasion.
  • Outer mucus layer contacts bacteria and participates in innate/adaptive immunity.
  • Tight junctions regulate paracellular movement of fluid and solutes.
  • Desmosomes provide intercellular/intracellular filamentous junctions.

Immune-sampling side

  • Enterocytes, enteroendocrine cells, goblet cells, and Paneth cells contribute antimicrobial proteins.
  • Dendritic cells sample normal flora/pathogens and present antigens.
  • Lymphocytes differentiate toward helper-T functions or Tregs to balance defense with tolerance.
ResponseRole in Fisher handout
Th1Eliminates intracellular pathogens.
Th2Protects against parasites.
Th17Helps clear other foreign pathogens.
TregProduces anti-inflammatory cytokines, modulates immunity, and decreases Th1 activation.

3 · How the Microbiome Changes Through Life

Delivery mode

Vaginal: exposure to maternal vaginal flora, especially Lactobacillus and Prevotella.

C-section: exposure to maternal skin flora, including Staphylococcus, Corynebacterium, and Propionibacterium. Fisher associates C-section with less diversity and later increased risks of several immune/metabolic conditions.

Early life

The first 3 years are presented as crucial. Healthy infants are initially rich in Bifidobacterium/Lactobacillus, then become increasingly enriched in Bacteroidetes and Firmicutes as the microbiome matures and solid foods are introduced.

Stage / factorHandout pattern
Breast milk/colostrumIntroduces large numbers of bacteria; supports milk-oligosaccharide processing and immune maturation.
Formula feedingAssociated with more C. difficile, B. fragilis, E. coli and fewer bifidobacteria.
Broad-spectrum antibiotics in first yearDecrease microbiome diversity; associated with later inflammatory disease/asthma risk.
PubertyGender-specific microbiome emerges; aerobes decrease and anaerobes increase.
AdulthoodMore stable, but diet can still rapidly change composition.
>65 yearsShift from Firmicutes toward Bacteroidetes; diet/living setting influences diversity and inflammation.
Important source boundary: the handout explicitly states that dysbiosis is associated with several diseases but that proof of causation is not established for many of those associations.

4 · Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs) + Diet

Acetate (C2) · propionate (C3) · butyrate (C4). Produced by colonic bacterial fermentation of undigested high-fiber food.
Energy
Major energy source for intestinal epithelial cells / colonocytes.
Barrier
Support intestinal epithelial barrier function and limit pathogenic/endotoxin translocation.
Immune tone
Promote Tregs and anti-inflammatory signaling, decreasing intestinal inflammation.

Mediterranean-style pattern

High fiber, fruits/vegetables/legumes/nuts/minimally processed cereals, moderate fish, lower saturated fat/meat/dairy → handout describes higher SCFA levels.

Western-style pattern

Higher saturated fat, meat, and dairy → handout describes relatively lower gut SCFA concentrations, associated more often with disease.

Do not overstate this section: Fisher repeatedly frames many of these findings as associations, candidate mechanisms, or areas requiring further study.

6 · Microbiome + Obesity

Human/animal pattern in handout

  • Obesity associated with a microbiome dominated by Firmicutes.
  • Germ-free mice have less body fat than specific-pathogen-free mice.
  • Obese-mouse microbiota are enriched for genes that harvest energy from indigestible polysaccharides.
  • Transfer of feces from obese animals to germ-free mice increases body fat in recipients.

Metabolic links

  • Type 2 diabetes section describes increased Firmicutes and decreased Bacteroidetes.
  • High-fat diet increases plasma LPS in the handout's model of low-grade inflammation and insulin resistance.
  • Early antibiotic exposure is associated with later adiposity/BMI changes.

Rapid Review

MICROBIOME = organisms + genes + interactions/functions. MUCOSA = Peyer's patches + lamina propria lymphoid tissue + intraepithelial lymphocytes. SCFA = acetate/propionate/butyrate → colonocyte energy + barrier + Tregs. EARLY LIFE = delivery + feeding + antibiotics strongly shape diversity. DYSBIOSIS = associated with disease; association ≠ proven causation. OBESITY = Firmicutes-heavy pattern + energy-harvest/inflammation associations in the handout.

Fiber fermentation → acetate/propionate/butyrate → epithelial energy + barrier support + anti-inflammatory/Treg effects.
