Slides 3–8 · Stewardship, Susceptibility Testing, MIC & MBC

Stewardship = the 5 rights: right dose · right drug · right patient · right route · right duration.

The deck starts by placing susceptibility testing inside antimicrobial stewardship. It then shows four established ways to determine susceptibility plus an emerging method:

Phenotypic methods

  • Broth dilution (often automated)
  • Epsilometer / E-test
  • Agar diffusion

Genotypic / emerging

  • PCR-based genotypic testing
  • Mass spectrometry is shown separately as emerging
LO wording nuance: the official objective asks for 4 ways. The current slide displays broth dilution, E-test, agar diffusion, and PCR-based genotypic testing as the established methods, while mass spectrometry is explicitly labeled “emerging.”
Page 5. The deck’s susceptibility-testing overview: broth dilution, E-test, agar diffusion, genotypic PCR, and emerging mass spectrometry.
MIC = lowest antimicrobial concentration that prevents visible growth. MBC = lowest concentration that kills sufficiently that no colonies grow when subcultured.
Page 7. MIC versus MBC logic. Growth can be inhibited before the organism is actually killed.

Bactericidal

MBC:MIC ≤ 4.

Bacteriostatic

MBC:MIC > 4.

Faculty emphasis: after defining the classical terms, the deck explicitly says the bactericidal/bacteriostatic distinction is not clinically relevant as a stand-alone treatment rule.

Slides 12–16 · Cell Wall Target + β-Lactam Mechanism

Bind penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) → inhibit transpeptidation → block peptidoglycan cell-wall synthesis → cell lysis.
Page 13. The lecture’s four-step β-lactam mechanism. This is the central MOA sequence to know.

Penicillins share a β-lactam ring and thiazolidine ring; the R1 side chain is where structure-activity changes matter.

Page 16. The lecture links R-group chemistry to three exam-facing properties: acid stability, porin permeability, and β-lactamase stability.

Electron-withdrawing group

Improves stability in an acidic environment.

Polar group

Improves porin-channel permeability.

Bulky group

Improves stability against β-lactamase.

The deck groups β-lactamase inhibitors by chemical scaffold: β-lactam structure (clavulanate, sulbactam, tazobactam, enmetazobactam), non-β-lactam structure (avibactam, relebactam, durlobactam), and boric-acid structure (vaborbactam).

Common combinations: amoxicillin/clavulanate (PO) · ampicillin/sulbactam (IV) · piperacillin/tazobactam (IV).

Slides 25–34 · Cephalosporins by Generation

Cephalosporin R1 drives antimicrobial activity and allergy cross-reactivity; the dihydrothiazine ring improves stability; the R2/X side chain modifies pharmacokinetics.

GenerationExamplesFaculty emphasis
1stCefazolin, cephalexin, cefadroxilExcellent Gram-positive coverage except Enterococcus; modest Enterobacterales. Cefazolin = MSSA + pre-op prophylaxis.
2ndCefoxitin, cefuroximeSeldom used; more Gram-negative / less Gram-positive than 1st; cefoxitin can provide anaerobic coverage for bowel/contaminated surgery.
3rdCeftriaxone, ceftazidime, cefdinir, cefpodoximeCeftriaxone: Strep/MSSA + Enterobacterales, long t½. Ceftazidime: Pseudomonas but little Gram-positive coverage.
4thCefepimeEnterobacterales + Pseudomonas + Gram-positive except Enterococcus/MRSA; zwitterion gives AmpC stability.
5thCeftarolineMRSA through increased affinity for PBP2a; Gram-negative activity is less than cefepime/ceftriaxone/ceftazidime.
Page 33. Faculty’s generation-by-generation cephalosporin spectrum and coverage-gap summary. Use it as the master comparison table.
Do not use “higher generation = better at everything.” The deck repeatedly teaches tradeoffs. Ceftazidime buys Pseudomonas at the cost of appreciable Gram-positive coverage; ceftaroline buys MRSA but not the Gram-negative breadth of cefepime.

Slides 35–43 · Carbapenems, Cefiderocol, Novel BL/BLI & Aztreonam

Meropenem

Broad; includes Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter on the deck’s comparison.

Imipenem

Broad; E. faecalis, Pseudomonas; better NTM coverage; handout flags neurotoxicity.

Ertapenem

Enterobacterales and anaerobes, but not Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, or Enterococcus.

Page 36. The carbapenem Venn diagram. Ertapenem’s missing anti-Pseudomonal activity is a recurrent test point.
Carbapenem–valproic acid interaction: the deck shows carbapenems disrupting the valproate ↔ valproate-glucuronide recycling pathway, a clinically important interaction to remember.

Cefiderocol is taught as the iron-binding “Trojan horse” siderophore cephalosporin. Novel β-lactam/BLI combinations include ceftazidime/avibactam, ceftolozane/tazobactam, and meropenem/vaborbactam.

Aztreonam

Monobactam with no conjoined ring; historically used as an anti-Pseudomonal β-lactam in penicillin allergy. The deck emphasizes very high resistance rates, stability against metallo-β-lactamases, and states it is never used as monotherapy.

Slides 44–46 · β-Lactam Allergy & Cross-Reactivity

The lecture shifts cross-reactivity away from a simplistic “same class” idea and toward R1 side-chain similarity. That is also why the R1 side chain was introduced earlier as a determinant of allergy-related cross-reactivity.

Page 45. Risk of cross-reactivity rises with increasing R1 side-chain similarity. The important conceptual point is structural similarity, not simply the cephalosporin generation number.
Use the local WMCG chart for operational decisions. The deck includes an institution-specific guidance matrix on page 46; for exam study, the transferable mechanism is R1-side-chain similarity.

Slides 48–51 · Faculty Practice Questions

Q1. Gram-positive cocci in chains in community-acquired pneumonia: which β-lactam class will not cover it?
Answer: monobactams. Aztreonam is Gram-negative focused and does not cover the Gram-positive organism described.
Q2. MIC for drug G on the microdilution plate?
Answer: 4 mg/L. Row G is inhibited through the 4 mg/L well; visible growth begins at 2 mg/L.
Q3. Which listed β-lactams cover Pseudomonas?
Answer: meropenem, piperacillin/tazobactam, and cefepime. Ertapenem and ceftriaxone do not.
Q4. ICU patient with E. coli expressing CTX-M while receiving vancomycin + piperacillin/tazobactam?
Answer: switch piperacillin/tazobactam to meropenem and stop vancomycin. The deck classifies CTX-M as ESBL and points to carbapenems.

Rapid Recall · What to Be Able to Say Out Loud

Core definitions

  • MIC = lowest concentration with no visible growth.
  • MBC = lowest concentration that kills on subculture.
  • β-lactams = time-dependent; target fT>MIC.
  • PBP binding → transpeptidation blocked → wall synthesis blocked → lysis.

Coverage anchors

  • Nafcillin/oxacillin → MSSA.
  • Ampicillin → E. faecalis.
  • Pip/tazo → Pseudomonas + anaerobes + broad GNR.
  • Cefazolin → MSSA / surgical prophylaxis.
  • Ceftazidime, cefepime → Pseudomonas.
  • Ceftaroline → MRSA.
  • Ertapenem → no Pseudomonas.
