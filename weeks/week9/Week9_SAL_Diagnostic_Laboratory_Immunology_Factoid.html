A1.1 · Antibody & Antigen Testing

ELISA / EIA
Can detect antibody or antigen.
Formats named in deck: direct, indirect, sandwich, competitive.
Signal intensity can be qualitative, semi-quantitative, or quantitative.
Rate nephelometry
Patient protein + specific antibody → immune complexes → changing light scatter → concentration.
Deck case: IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, C4.
Titer: serially dilute until the defined signal disappears. The highest dilution still meeting the endpoint is reported as the titer. Higher terminal dilution = more antibody.
Question clueThink
Quantitative IgG / IgA / IgM / C3 / C4Rate nephelometry
Antibody or antigen binding + enzyme-generated signalELISA / EIA

Serology anchor: the deck uses IgM as a clue to recent/ongoing primary infection and IgG as a clue to prior infection or immunization. Treat this as a pattern, not a stand-alone diagnosis.

A1.2 · Allergy Testing

Positive specific IgE ≠ clinically important allergy. It shows sensitization; the history and clinical context decide whether that sensitization matters.
Preferred specialist method
Skin testing.
Common lab method
Specific-IgE EIA, often reported quantitatively (e.g., kU/L).
Why test? Demonstrate clinically relevant specific IgE, guide avoidance, select extracts for immunotherapy, or support epidemiologic questions. Do not let the lab result outrank the patient history.

A1.3 · TREC Newborn Screening

What a TREC is
T-cell receptor rearrangement in the thymus creates excised DNA circles. PCR can quantify these circles in blood.
What low TREC means
Low T-cell output. It is a screening signal, not a one-step etiologic diagnosis.
Follow-up logic: abnormal TREC → evaluate lymphocyte subsets by flow cytometry → characterize phenotype → continue immune workup.

A1.4 · Three Flow Cytometer Systems

1 · Fluidics
Hydrodynamic focusing aligns cells in single file using sheath fluid.
2 · Optics / detection
Lasers + lenses/mirrors/filters + photodetectors capture scatter and fluorescence.
3 · Electronics / processing
Signals become digital event values displayed as histograms or dot plots.
Flow one-liner: line cells up → shine light → measure scatter/fluorescence → turn each cell into an event → plot populations.

A1.6 · Lymphocyte Functional Testing

Workflow: isolate lymphocytes → culture ± stimulus → add tritiated thymidine → proliferating cells incorporate label into DNA → harvest → scintillation count → compare with control.
StimulusDeck anchor
PHAT-cell mitogen
ConAT-cell mitogen
PWMStimulates T + B cells
Specific antigensCandida / tetanus examples in the deck
Low proliferation: think severe combined immune defects, profound T-cell lymphopenia/complete DiGeorge, advanced HIV, or other states that suppress lymphocyte responses. The deck also notes milder combined/humoral defects, viral suppression, malnutrition, cancer, surgery/shock, and immunosuppressive drugs.

Exam Traps

Trap 1
Low TREC does not name the disease. It flags low T-cell production.
Trap 2
Positive allergen-specific IgE does not equal symptomatic allergy without matching history.
Trap 3
FSC = size; SSC = granularity/complexity. Do not flip them.
Trap 4
Histogram = one parameter; dot plot = multidimensional event display.
