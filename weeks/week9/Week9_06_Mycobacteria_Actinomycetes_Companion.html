1. What Makes Mycobacteria Different?

Mycobacteria

Classified by acid-fastness, mycolic acids with 70–90 carbons, and high G+C DNA content. Prominent examples named in the deck are M. tuberculosis, M. leprae, and NTM.

Actinomycete emphasis in this deck

Partially acid-fast organisms with shorter mycolic acids (50–60 carbons) and high G+C content. The deck's prominent example is Nocardia.

General mycobacterial characteristics: ubiquitous in water/soil; nonmotile, non-spore-forming aerobic bacilli or filaments; lipid-rich hydrophobic wall; difficult to stain; resistant to disinfectants; slow-growing, sometimes up to 8 weeks; cord factor (trehalose dimycolate) is listed as a virulence factor.
Deck p. 5: side-by-side Gram-positive vs mycobacterial cell wall. The mycobacterial wall adds arabinogalactan, mycolic acid, lipoarabinomannan, and free mycolic acids/polypeptides over peptidoglycan.
Acid-fast rule: once stained, AFB resist acid-alcohol decolorization and remain red with carbol fuchsin; non-AFB decolorize and take the blue methylene-blue counterstain.
Deck p. 6: NTM, Nocardia asteroides, M. tuberculosis, and M. leprae are listed as acid-fast examples, with Nocardia later specified as weakly/partially acid-fast.

2. NTM Pathogenesis: Cell-Mediated Immunity Is the Center

Macrophage phagocytosis → IL-12 + TNF-α → Th1 cells → IFN-γ → macrophage activation → intracellular killing of NTM

Who disseminates?

The deck highlights AIDS with CD4 < 50/µL and genetic defects in the IFN-γ / IL-12 axis. It explicitly calls the positive feedback loop between IFN-γ and IL-12 critical for NTM control.

Histologic response

Granulomatous inflammation is the hallmark. The deck emphasizes multinucleated giant cells with nuclei arranged in arcs, labeled Langhans cells.

Deck p. 11: H&E examples of granulomatous inflammation with Langhans-type multinucleated giant cells.
Do not mix the histology: NTM → granulomatous inflammation. Nocardia later in this same deck is explicitly described as no granuloma, with a suppurative process instead.

3. Sorting the NTM by Growth + Pigment

Slow growers: SMACK

Scrofulaceum · Marinum · Avium complex · C? (mnemonic as written in deck) · Kansasii. The deck's explicit list is M. scrofulaceum, M. marinum, MAC, and M. kansasii.

Rapid growers

M. abscessus · M. chelonae · M. fortuitum. Rapid growers are defined as <7 days; slow growers are ~14 days.

4. NTM Clinical Patterns

Most common localized NTM pulmonary disease in HIV-negative patients: MAC · M. abscessus · M. kansasii. Deck mnemonic: Mac & Mak.

MAC: M. avium + M. intracellulare

Slow-growing non-chromogens; smooth/whitish colonies; ubiquitous in soil, water, dust, domestic animals; water is identified as the primary mode of transmission; prevalent in Southeast/Gulf Coast.

Disseminated disease is associated with AIDS/T-cell immunodeficiency. Constitutional symptoms listed: fever, night sweats, weight loss. The deck notes resistance to commonly used anti-TB drugs.

M. kansasii

Slow-growing photochromogen. Deck geography: South, Midwest, California. Rare in natural water but consistently isolated from tap water in endemic areas.

Older men with smoking/COPD, previous TB, pneumoconiosis, bronchiectasis. Clinical picture is most like TB, with right-upper-lobe predilection and cavitation listed at 90%.

Deck p. 20: M. kansasii colony before and after light exposure, demonstrating photochromogenic yellow pigment.

M. abscessus + cervical lymphadenitis + disseminated MAC

M. abscessus lung disease

Elderly female nonsmokers, no underlying lung disease; resembles non-cavitary MAC; nodular infiltrates + bronchiectasis.

Cervical lymphadenitis

Previously healthy children: MAC 60–85%; M. scrofulaceum 10–20%. Deck treatment: excision.

Disseminated MAC in AIDS

Hallmark: abundant AFB within macrophages; involvement of mononuclear phagocyte system including lymph nodes, liver, spleen.

5. Leprosy: The Clinical Spectrum Tracks Cell-Mediated Immunity

M. leprae: has not been cultured in vitro; the deck notes growth in mouse footpads and armadillos. Hosts listed are humans and armadillos. Optimal growth temperature is 32–34°C, matching cooler skin/peripheral tissues.
Deck p. 31: tuberculoid disease at the strong-CMI end and lepromatous disease at the absent/weak-CMI end, with borderline forms between them.

6. Nocardia: Branching, Weakly Acid-Fast, Opportunistic

Identity

Aerobic Gram-positive, weakly acid-fast bacteria in water, decaying organic matter, and soil. Despite mold-like morphology, Nocardia is a true bacterium. Culture can show aerial hyphae.

Microscopy

Modified acid-fast stain (Fite-Faraco). Thin, filamentous, branching “hyphae” stain irregularly on Gram stain. The deck states no other acid-fast organism forms branching filaments.

Deck p. 44: true branching filaments with irregularly distributed, non-touching “beads,” a deck clue used to distinguish Nocardia from streptococci.
Inhalation (or less commonly skin injury) → opportunistic infection → pulmonary disease → possible CNS dissemination / brain abscess

Pathogenesis

Cord factor; prevention of phagosome acidification and phagolysosome fusion; T-cell immunity is crucial. No granuloma. Instead, the deck describes suppurative infection with liquefaction plus surrounding granulation/fibrosis.

Disease + treatment

N. asteroides: respiratory infection in immunocompromised patients and dissemination to CNS/brain abscess. N. brasiliensis: cellulitis, lymphocutaneous disease, actinomycetoma with chronic draining fistulae. Deck Rx: sulfa drugs, meropenem, 3rd-gen cephalosporins, linezolid.

High-yield contrast: NTM/MAC → granulomatous inflammation. Nocardia → no granuloma; suppurative infection. Both can interfere with phagolysosomal killing.

7. HYI Wrap-Up

Deck p. 49: the faculty summary table pairs M. kansasii with TB-like cavitary disease, MAC with disseminated AIDS infection, M. leprae phenotype with strength of CMI, and N. asteroides with lung infection/brain abscess.

Recognition anchors

M. kansasii = slow photochromogen + TB-like RUL cavitation. MAC = slow non-chromogen + AIDS dissemination. M. leprae = never cultured in vitro + phenotype depends on CMI. Nocardia = Gram+ weakly acid-fast branching filaments.

Mechanism anchors

NTM control = IL-12 → Th1 IFN-γ → activated macrophages. NTM histology = granulomas/Langhans cells. Nocardia = phagosome/phagolysosome interference but no granuloma.

Last 60 seconds: mycobacteria = mycolic acid + acid-fast · AFB stay red · NTM control = IL-12/IFN-γ/Th1/macrophage · granuloma = NTM hallmark · kansasii = photochromogen + TB-like RUL cavity · MAC = nonchromogen + AIDS / nodular-bronchiectatic patterns · M. leprae outcome tracks CMI · Nocardia = GP branching weakly acid-fast + lung→brain + no granuloma.
