Pages 4–15 · Neisseria gonorrhoeae

Identification

Gram-negative diplococcus; glucose positive, maltose negative, sucrose negative. The deck uses Thayer-Martin agar for culture and highlights Gram stain of urethral discharge plus a DNA probe.

Major virulence factors

Pili mediate attachment and undergo antigenic variation → lack of durable immunity. Por protein prevents phagolysosomal fusion. LOS is endotoxin; Lipid A is the toxic moiety. The organism also produces IgA protease.

Deck page 5. Pili, Por protein, LOS/Lipid A, and IgA protease form the lecture's core virulence map.

Epidemiology

Humans are the only host. The deck emphasizes increased disease burden in adolescents, asymptomatic carriage in women more than men, and overt disease in men more than women.

Disease map

Men: urethritis. Women: endocervical inflammation, purulent discharge, irregular bleeding, PID → scarring → infertility. Also disseminated gonococcal infection, ophthalmia neonatorum, proctitis, pharyngitis, and arthritis.

Treatment/prevention exactly as taught in this deck: ceftriaxone is highlighted; the slide says to add tetracycline for mixed infection with Chlamydia trachomatis; prevention is nonspecific (e.g., condoms). The resistance slide lists plasmid-mediated β-lactamase, efflux pumps, PenA/PenB changes, gyrase mutations, and common multidrug resistance.
Exam split: N. gonorrhoeae = glucose + / maltose − / no capsule. N. meningitidis = glucose + / maltose + / capsule +.

Pages 16–25 · Neisseria meningitidis

Virulence

Capsule is the major virulence factor, defines serogroups, can be detected in CSF, and is used in vaccines. The deck also emphasizes outer-membrane protein, LOS/endotoxin with toxic Lipid A, and IgA protease.

Pathogenesis

LOS damages ciliated epithelial cells → submucosal invasion. Disease may present as meningitis or meningococcemia, with DIC, shock, and Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome.

Deck page 19. Capsule + LOS underpin the lecture's meningitis/meningococcemia pathway.

Who is at higher risk?

The deck highlights complement deficiency, especially the late pathway, and splenectomy as major risks for severe/disseminated disease.

Laboratory diagnosis

CSF specimen; kidney-shaped Gram-negative diplococci; latex agglutination; blood or chocolate agar; oxidase positive; glucose positive; maltose positive.

Deck page 23. CSF, kidney-shaped diplococci, latex agglutination, BAP/CHOC, oxidase+, glucose+, maltose+.
Deck page 24. Vaccine strategy and limitations are explicitly part of the lecture's prevention emphasis.
Treatment/prevention as taught: the deck lists ceftriaxone, high-dose penicillin G, a carbapenem, or chloramphenicol if allergic. Close-contact prophylaxis includes rifampin, with ceftriaxone in pregnancy and ciprofloxacin/azithromycin alternatives. Vaccine slides distinguish conjugate/pentavalent strategies and note limitations of serogroup B protection.

Pages 26–35 · Haemophilus influenzae

General traits

Gram-negative, pleomorphic, nonmotile coccobacillus with an X and V factor requirement.

Virulence

LPS (Lipid A toxic moiety), outer-membrane protein, capsule, and IgA protease. The capsule defines serotype, can be detected in CSF, and is included in the vaccine.

Disease spread from the nasopharynx

Middle ear → otitis media · sinus → sinusitis · bronchi → bronchitis · alveoli → pneumonia · epiglottis → epiglottitis · meninges → meningitis. The deck also lists cellulitis and arthritis.

Deck page 33. Pediatric epiglottitis: the “4 D's” (dysphagia, dysphonia, drooling, distress), abrupt progression, toxic appearance, airway obstruction, hot-potato voice, and tripod posture.
H. influenzae ID: pleomorphic G− coccobacillus + X factor (hemin) + V factor (NAD). The deck ties vaccine impact strongly to decreased Hib disease.
Treatment/prevention as taught: otitis/sinusitis options listed are ampicillin, Augmentin, or TMP-SMX; meningitis uses a third-generation cephalosporin such as ceftriaxone or cefotaxime; Hib vaccine is the prevention anchor.

Pages 36–42 · Bordetella pertussis

Identity + attachment

Small Gram-negative encapsulated coccobacillus and obligate pathogen. Filamentous hemagglutinin on pili mediates attachment to ciliated upper-airway epithelium.

Key toxin pathway

No invasion. Pertussis toxin inactivates Gαi → loss of inhibitory control on adenylate cyclase → ↑ adenylate cyclase activity → ↑ cAMP → mucus secretion and lymphocytosis.

Deck page 39. This Gαi → adenylate cyclase → cAMP pathway is explicitly marked HYI.
StageDeck features
Catarrhal1–2 weeks: low-grade fever, malaise, conjunctivitis, rhinitis, sneezing, lacrimation.
Paroxysmal cough2–4 weeks: spasmodic cough, cyanosis/vomiting, inspiratory whoop (not heard in adults), fine rales, cough syncope.
Convalescent1–2 weeks; coughing spasms resolve over 1–3 months.
Lab: very high WBC with ~50% lymphocytosis is marked HYI; nasopharyngeal swab; Regan-Lowe charcoal medium + horse blood; culture described as gold standard/100% specificity; PCR 0–3 weeks after cough onset; serology 2–8 weeks and useful in outbreaks. Treatment/prevention: erythromycin and the acellular pertussis vaccine are emphasized; the deck labels the vaccine “DaPT.”

Pages 43–45 · Moraxella catarrhalis

Deck page 43. Moraxella is a Gram-negative diplococcus, catalase+ and oxidase+, that does not utilize carbohydrates.

Recognition

Can look like Neisseria. Culture on blood or chocolate agar. Adhesins bind epithelial cells; A2 surface protein protects from phagocytosis; LOS is the key virulence determinant and drives inflammatory cytokines.

Clinical associations

Colonization is much higher in young children than adults. Contiguous spread from oral mucosa leads to otitis media and pneumonia, especially in patients with COPD.

Treatment as taught: most strains are β-lactamase producers; the slide lists amoxicillin/clavulanate, β-lactamase-stable cephalosporins, macrolides, or fluoroquinolones.

Pages 46–47 · Kingella kingae

Clinical pattern

Osteoarticular infection, especially septic arthritis in children 6 months–4 years, and endocarditis at all ages. Colonizes the oropharynx and has increased incidence in daycare centers.

Microbiology

Gram-negative coccobacillus with tapered ends, pairs/short chains, oxidase positive. Virulence determinants: pili, capsule, cytotoxin. Difficult to culture → PCR-based assays preferred for joint fluid.

Deck page 47. The slide emphasizes morphology, oxidase positivity, virulence factors, PCR preference, and broad treatment options.
HACEK: the deck places K. kingae among HACEK organisms associated with “culture-negative” endocarditis. Most isolates are described as β-lactamase negative.

LO Review · What You Should Be Able to Do

Differentiate Neisseria

Meningitidis = glucose+/maltose+/capsule. Gonorrhoeae = glucose+/maltose−/no capsule.

Match site + organism

Urogenital/PID/DGI → gonococcus; meningitis/meningococcemia → meningococcus; epiglottitis/X+V factors → H. influenzae; paroxysmal cough/lymphocytosis → pertussis; otitis/COPD pneumonia → Moraxella; toddler septic arthritis → Kingella.

Know toxin logic

Neisseria LOS/Lipid A; H. influenzae LPS/Lipid A; pertussis toxin disables Gαi and raises cAMP; Moraxella LOS drives inflammation.

Use vaccination appropriately

The deck specifically contrasts meningococcal vaccine strategies/limitations, Hib vaccine impact, and acellular pertussis vaccination.

Last 60 seconds: meningococcus = maltose+ capsule+ · gonococcus = maltose− capsule− · both oxidase+ glucose+ · H. flu = pleomorphic coccobacillus + X/V · pertussis = no invasion, Gαi off → cAMP up + lymphocytosis · Moraxella = oxidase+/catalase+/no carbs · Kingella = toddler bone/joint + HACEK + PCR.
