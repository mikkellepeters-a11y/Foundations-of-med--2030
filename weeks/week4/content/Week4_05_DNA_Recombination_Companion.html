Chromosomal Translocations, Deletions & Inversions
Slides 3-6What recombination does and the four major typesLO 1

Recombination is the physical exchange or rearrangement of DNA sequences. It is essential for DNA repair, restart of damaged replication forks, meiotic chromosome pairing/segregation, generation of genetic variation, integration of incoming DNA in bacteria, immune-receptor diversity, and genome evolution.

TypeDefining featureHigh-yield role
Homologous recombination (HR)Uses long stretches of homologous sequenceHigh-fidelity DSB repair; meiotic crossing over
Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)Directly rejoins broken ends with little/no homologyMajor somatic-cell DSB repair; V(D)J joining; translocation risk
Transpositional recombinationMoves mobile DNA to new genomic sitesDNA transposons and retrotransposons; repetitive DNA
Conservative site-specific recombinationCutting and rejoining at defined sequence sitesResearch/engineering systems; precise rearrangement
Big distinction: HR is sequence/homology driven and comparatively conservative. NHEJ is junction driven and can be imprecise. Transposition changes genomic position. Site-specific recombination requires defined target sequences.
Slides 7-11Homologous recombination: template-directed exchangeLO 2

When HR is favored

Requires relatively long homologous regions and is most important when a homologous template is available, especially in S/G2 and during meiosis.

Why meiosis needs it

Programmed double-strand breaks initiated by Spo11 create recombination events that help homologous chromosomes pair and segregate correctly.

DSB->5' end resection->3' strand invasion->Holliday junction->branch migration->resolution
Consequences: crossover/reassortment, gene conversion, generation of new alleles, and accurate repair of broken DNA. In germ cells these changes can become heritable.
Do not over-memorize enzyme minutiae: the deck explicitly emphasizes the overall logic - break, resection, strand invasion/Holliday junction, migration, resolution.
Slides 12-13Crossover frequency measures genetic distanceLO 3

Genes close together on the same chromosome recombine less often and therefore tend to be inherited together as a haplotype. Greater physical separation generally increases the chance of a crossover occurring between loci.

HLA anchor from the deck: HLA loci are physically close and usually inherited en bloc. The lecture uses the classic sibling-transplant relationship: a sibling has a 25% chance of sharing the same two parental HLA haplotypes; recombination within the HLA region is relatively uncommon but can break the expected haplotype.

Slides 17-21NHEJ: fast DSB repair with a genomic priceLO 2 & 5

Core mechanism

Broken ends are processed and directly ligated without a homologous template. Very short microhomologies can help alignment.

Cell-cycle context

Prominent in somatic cells and especially important in G1, when a sister chromatid is not available.

NHEJ is inherently error prone: trimming or filling DNA ends can create small insertions/deletions. Joining ends from different chromosomes can create oncogenic translocations.

Cancer examples in the deck: t(9;22) BCR-ABL in chronic myeloid leukemia and t(8;14) involving MYC in Burkitt lymphoma illustrate how DSB misjoining can generate oncogenic fusion/regulatory events.
Immune connection: NHEJ repairs the DSBs created during antigen-receptor rearrangement. Defective NHEJ proteins can therefore impair V(D)J recombination and produce severe immunodeficiency phenotypes.

LINE-1

Long interspersed element; autonomous because it encodes proteins needed for its own reverse-transcription/integration pathway.

Alu / SINE

Short interspersed element; non-autonomous and borrows LINE-1 machinery. High copy number makes Alu sequences important substrates for ectopic recombination.

Processed pseudogene signature: reverse-transcribed cellular mRNA reinserted into DNA - therefore it typically lacks introns and its native promoter, may retain a 3' poly(A)-derived sequence, and can be flanked by target-site duplications.
Slide 28Recombination creates antibody diversityLO 5
RAG1/RAG2 recognize RSS->DNA cleavage / DSB->NHEJ rejoins coding ends->junctional imprecision adds diversity

V(D)J recombination uses a cleavage chemistry related to ancient transposases. The intervening DNA is excised as a circle, while NHEJ joins selected antigen-receptor gene segments. Variation in segment choice plus imprecise end joining greatly expands receptor diversity.

Exam trapsProtect the mechanism-to-outcome linksAll LOs
Trap 1: HR requires substantial homology; NHEJ does not.
Trap 2: HR is not synonymous with "crossing over" - it also performs template-directed DSB repair.
Trap 3: LINE-1 is autonomous; Alu/SINE is non-autonomous.
Trap 4: Processed pseudogenes come from RNA intermediates, so they usually lack introns.
Trap 5: V(D)J cleavage is RAG-mediated; joining is NHEJ-mediated.
30-second finish
Four types: HR, NHEJ, transposition, site-specific. HR uses homology and Holliday-junction logic; NHEJ directly rejoins ends. Repeats predispose to NAHR. HLA genes are tightly linked. Retrotransposons expand repetitive DNA. RAG cleavage + NHEJ joining generates receptor diversity.
