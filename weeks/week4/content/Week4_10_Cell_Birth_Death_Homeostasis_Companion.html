Official Week 4 Objectives

1. Explain how retinoblastoma (Rb) protein controls the mammalian cell cycle and how loss of Rb function contributes to human disease.
2. Describe the molecular basis of flow cytometry and use DNA content to identify cell-cycle status.
3. Describe p53 regulation of the G1 checkpoint and distinguish p53-driven growth arrest from apoptosis.
4. Explain the intrinsic pathway of apoptosis and the roles of mitochondria, caspases, and Bcl-2 family proteins including Bax, Bak, Bid, and Bim.
5. Identify morphological, biochemical, and molecular features of apoptosis and techniques used to detect it.
6. Describe the extrinsic pathway of apoptosis and contrast it with the intrinsic pathway.
Slides 3-7Tissue homeostasis is a balance between proliferation and cell deathOverview

Tissues maintain stable structure and function by balancing production of new cells with elimination of damaged, unnecessary, or dangerous cells. Growth-factor signaling pushes cells toward proliferation; checkpoint and death pathways prevent damaged cells from propagating.

Lecture organizing idea: Rb connects extracellular growth signals to the restriction point, while p53 responds to internal damage and can choose cytostasis or cytotoxicity.
Slides 8-13Rb restrains E2F until growth signals activate Cyclin D-CDK4/6LO 1
growth factor signaling->Cyclin D + CDK4/6->Rb phosphorylation/inactivation->E2F released->S-phase genes

Active, hypophosphorylated Rb binds E2F and blocks G1-to-S progression. Growth-factor-dependent CDK activity phosphorylates Rb, releases E2F, and lets the cell pass the restriction point.

Tumor-suppressor logic: loss of both functional Rb alleles removes this brake. Tumors can also functionally inactivate Rb by overactivating Cyclin D-CDK4/6. This is why CDK4/6 inhibitors can restore a cell-cycle brake in selected cancers.

Fixed cells are stained with propidium iodide, which intercalates into DNA. Fluorescence intensity is proportional to cellular DNA content. The lecture uses CDK4/6 inhibition to illustrate G1 accumulation and taxol to illustrate G2/M arrest followed by mitotic death.

Slides 20-28p53 converts DNA damage into either growth arrest or apoptosisLO 3

Lower damage signal

p53 induces p21, a CDK inhibitor → G1 arrest and time for repair. This is a cytostatic response.

Higher/persistent damage

p53 induces pro-apoptotic effectors such as Bax → mitochondrial permeabilization and cell death. This is a cytotoxic response.

p53 is a tumor suppressor: its activation blocks propagation of damaged genomes. Loss of p53 removes both the repair pause and an important route to apoptosis.

Slides 33-47Apoptosis has recognizable membrane, nuclear, and DNA signaturesLO 5

Membrane

Plasma membrane remains intact until late, but phosphatidylserine (PS) flips to the outer leaflet, flagging the cell for engulfment.

Nucleus

Chromatin condenses and fragments; nuclear lamins and other structural proteins are cleaved by executioner caspases.

DNA

Caspase-activated endonucleases cut chromatin into nucleosomal fragments, producing a ladder-like pattern and potentially a sub-G1 flow peak.

Exam trapsProtect checkpoint, cytometry, and caspase relationshipsAll LOs
Trap 1: Active Rb binds E2F and blocks S phase; phosphorylated/inactive Rb releases E2F.
Trap 2: G1 = 2N; G2/M = 4N; S lies between.
Trap 3: p21 mediates p53 growth arrest; Bax helps mediate p53 apoptosis.
Trap 4: Intrinsic initiator = caspase-9; extrinsic initiators = caspase-8/10.
Trap 5: Bcl-2 is anti-apoptotic; Bax/Bak are pro-apoptotic.
Trap 6: Annexin V recognizes externalized phosphatidylserine.
30-second finish
Growth factor → Cyclin D/CDK4/6 → Rb OFF → E2F ON. PI flow: G1 2N, S 2-4N, G2/M 4N. p53 low damage → p21 arrest; high damage → Bax death. Intrinsic: mitochondria/cytochrome c/caspase-9. Extrinsic: Fas/FADD/caspase-8/10. Both converge on executioner caspases.
