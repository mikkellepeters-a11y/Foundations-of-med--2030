Transformation

Competent cell takes up free DNA → integrates by recombination or maintains plasmid. Naked DNA is exposed, so extracellular DNase abolishes transformation.

Conjugation

F plasmid
Classic Gram-negative donor-recipient transfer through direct contact/sex pilus.
Hfr
F factor integrated into chromosome; adjacent chromosomal genes can transfer.
Enterococcus twist: Gram-positive conjugation can be pheromone-driven; recipient peptide induces donor adhesins/clumping rather than a classic sex pilus.

Transduction

TypeRule
GeneralizedRandom host DNA accidentally packaged
SpecializedGenes adjacent to prophage insertion site move after imprecise excision

Bacterial Genetics - Fast Recall

Resistance

Horizontal transfer spreads resistance faster than independent mutation in every lineage. Deck anchors: mecA in MRSA; vanA in VRE.

Lysogenic Conversion

Temperate phage integrates as a prophage; a phage gene changes bacterial phenotype, often by adding toxin production.

EHEC Clinical Logic

Prophage induction can increase Shiga toxin. Deck emphasizes hydration + HUS monitoring and avoidance of reflex antibiotics/antimotility therapy in suspected EHEC.

Historical Mechanism Anchors

Griffith/Avery: transformation and DNA as transforming principle.
Hershey-Chase: phage DNA enters bacteria.
Lederberg-Tatum: conjugation.
Zinder-Lederberg: transduction.

Must-Not-Miss Clues

Free DNA + DNase sensitive = transformation. Contact/pilus = conjugation. Phage = transduction. Prophage-encoded toxin = lysogenic conversion. Random transduced genes = generalized; adjacent-to-prophage genes = specialized.
