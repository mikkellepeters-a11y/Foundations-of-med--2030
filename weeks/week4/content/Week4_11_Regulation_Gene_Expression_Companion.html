Official Week 4 Objectives

1. Define gene expression; distinguish constitutive, facultative, regulated, temporal, and spatial expression; distinguish cis-acting regulatory elements from trans-acting factors and apply them to lac-operon regulation.
2. Identify the multiple levels at which eukaryotic gene expression can be regulated.
3. Describe the basic architecture of eukaryotic transcription factors, identify the four conserved DNA-binding motifs, and explain estrogen-receptor function.
4. Explain DNA methylation and histone modification in gene regulation and apply genomic imprinting to Prader-Willi, Angelman, and Beckwith-Wiedemann syndromes.
5. Describe regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs, including miRNA/siRNA RNA interference, and explain X-chromosome inactivation.

Deck pp. 3-7The regulatory language: expression patterns, cis elements, and trans factorsLO 1

Constitutive

Housekeeping genes are expressed continuously because their products are needed broadly, such as core metabolic machinery.

Facultative / regulated

Expression is switched on or altered when a product is needed or in response to environmental, hormonal, or developmental signals.

Temporal

A gene is expressed at a particular time or developmental stage.

Spatial

A gene is expressed only in a particular tissue, cell type, or location.

Cis-acting elements are nucleotide sequences

They regulate nearby nucleic-acid sequences and do not need to encode a diffusible product. Examples in the deck include promoters, enhancers, silencers/operators, and response elements.

Trans-acting factors are diffusible products

Proteins or regulatory RNAs can move through the cell and interact with cis elements. Examples include transcription factors, activators, repressors, coactivators, corepressors, miRNAs, and lncRNAs.

Deck pp. 8-10Lac operon: lactose releases the brake; low glucose presses the acceleratorLO 1

Bacterial transcription and translation are coupled, so regulation is primarily transcriptional. Functionally related genes can be clustered into an operon and transcribed as one polycistronic mRNA.

Do not collapse the two controls into one. Lactose controls the repressor; glucose availability is sensed through cAMP-CRP/CAP. Maximal expression needs lactose present and glucose low.
Deck pp. 11-12Eukaryotes regulate expression at multiple checkpointsLO 2

Genome / chromatin

Change whether regulatory DNA is physically accessible.

Transcription

Promoters, enhancers, silencers, response elements, and transcription factors determine RNA synthesis.

RNA processing / export

Splicing, transcript processing, stability, and nuclear export change the available mRNA pool.

Translation

Regulatory RNAs and translation-initiation pathways alter protein production from existing mRNA.

Post-translational

Modification, trafficking, activation, and degradation change protein abundance or activity.

Exam framing: eukaryotic regulation is not synonymous with transcriptional regulation. First identify which level is being altered.

Trap: histone methylation is not the same rule as DNA methylation. The deck explicitly emphasizes that histone methylation can have different effects depending on the site.

DNA methylation is dynamic. Development includes waves of demethylation and remethylation, while imprinted loci preserve parent-of-origin information until germline reprogramming resets the imprint for the next generation.

Deck pp. 17-26Genomic imprinting makes parental origin matterLO 4
Imprinted allele = methylated / silenced allele in the deck's framework. If the only remaining copy of a gene is the normally imprinted copy, there may be no functional expression despite the gene still being physically present.

PWS = paternal expression lost. AS = maternal expression lost. Do not answer imprinting questions by asking only which chromosome is deleted; ask which parent's normally active allele has been lost.
Deck pp. 27-28Eukaryotic transcription factors are modular proteinsLO 3

Typical modules include a DNA-binding domain, a transactivation / regulatory domain, a nuclear localization signal, dimerization regions, and - for ligand-regulated nuclear receptors - a ligand-binding domain.

Deck pp. 29-30Estrogen receptor links a lipophilic signal directly to transcriptionLO 3
Estrogen enters cell→binds intracellular receptor→receptor dimerizes / exposes nuclear localization→binds estrogen-response element→coactivator recruitment changes transcription

The deck classifies estrogen receptor as a Type I nuclear receptor. In the absence of ligand, Type I receptors are cytoplasmic and associated with heat-shock proteins. Ligand binding changes receptor conformation, supports dimerization and nuclear entry, and allows response-element binding.

Tamoxifen: competes at the estrogen-receptor ligand-binding site but produces a receptor conformation that does not support the same coactivator interaction, thereby antagonizing estrogen-responsive transcription in the deck's model.

Deck pp. 35-36X-inactivation is dosage compensation, not total silencing of every X-linked geneLO 5
One X selected→XIST coats that chromosome→DNA hypermethylation + histone deacetylation→stable heterochromatin / Barr body
  • Selection is random between maternal and paternal X chromosomes in different embryonic cells.
  • Descendants maintain the same inactive X, creating a mosaic pattern of X-linked gene expression.
  • Some genes escape inactivation; pseudoautosomal-region genes are an important example in the deck.
Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes - 1 in the simple counting framework, but remember that genes escaping inactivation can still create dosage effects in sex-chromosome aneuploidy.
Exam trapsProtect the relationships most likely to be reversedAll LOs
Trap 1: cis = sequence; trans = diffusible factor.
Trap 2: maximal lac expression requires lactose present and glucose low.
Trap 3: HAT generally opens chromatin; HDAC closes it. Histone methylation is context-dependent.
Trap 4: PWS = loss of paternal expression; Angelman = loss of maternal expression.
Trap 5: steroid receptors are transcription factors with DNA-binding and ligand-binding domains; zinc fingers are the DBD motif emphasized.
Trap 6: miRNA and siRNA both use RISC, but target complementarity and dominant outcomes differ.
Trap 7: X-inactivation is incomplete; pseudoautosomal genes can escape.
30-second finish
Cis is sequence; trans is diffusible. Lactose removes repression; low glucose activates CRP. Eukaryotes regulate chromatin, transcription, RNA, translation, and protein. HAT opens; DNA methylation usually silences. PWS loses paternal expression; AS loses maternal. TF motifs: HTH, HLH, leucine zipper, zinc finger. miRNA/siRNA use RISC. XIST creates the inactive X.
