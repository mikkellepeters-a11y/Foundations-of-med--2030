Official Week 4 Objectives

1. Explain nucleic-acid denaturation and renaturation (annealing), including how these processes are used in laboratory hybridization techniques.
2. Discuss salient features of prokaryotic DNA and the importance of DNA gyrase.
3. Discuss salient features of eukaryotic DNA and distinguish telomeres, repetitive versus non-repetitive sequences, heterochromatin, and euchromatin.
4. Describe molecular mechanisms that switch DNA between heterochromatin and euchromatin and summarize chromatin remodeling required for DNA accessibility.
5. Discuss structure and function of RNAs common to prokaryotes and eukaryotes and the additional functional/regulatory RNAs found in eukaryotes.
Assigned ScholarRx
DNA Structure
Slides 3-5Nucleic-acid structure, melting, annealing, and hybridizationLO 1

Start with the invariant rules

  • DNA and RNA are heteropolymers of nucleotides joined by phosphodiester bonds.
  • Nucleic-acid sequences are written 5' to 3'.
  • Complementary strands pair only when they are antiparallel.
  • Denaturation separates paired strands and disrupts helical structure without breaking the covalent phosphodiester backbone.

Denaturation

Favored by increased temperature, extreme/high pH, low ionic strength, and chemical denaturants. Duplex disruption produces a hyperchromic shift: increased absorbance at 260 nm.

Renaturation / annealing

Complementary strands reassociate when conditions again favor base pairing. Gradual cooling and higher salt favor annealing because ionic shielding stabilizes the duplex.

Tm and GC content
Tm is the temperature at which 50% of the helix is denatured. Higher GC content increases Tm.
Stringency
High stringency = high temperature + low salt and therefore requires close complementarity. Low stringency = lower temperature + higher salt and allows related but imperfectly matched sequences to hybridize.
Why hybridization matters: a labeled nucleic-acid probe can identify complementary or related sequences. Because related sequences can still anneal under lower-stringency conditions, hybridization is generally a poor stand-alone method for distinguishing a single-base mutation or a very small insertion/deletion.
Slide 6Prokaryotic DNA and gyraseLO 2

Architecture

Bacterial chromosomal DNA is covalently closed, circular, relatively "naked," and maintained in a negatively supercoiled state.

Gyrase

DNA gyrase is a bacterial type II topoisomerase. It makes a transient double-strand break, passes DNA through the break, reseals it, and uses ATP.

Gyrase creates negative supercoils->DNA packing improves->Replication/transcription can proceed
Drug connection: ciprofloxacin and novobiocin target gyrase. The lecture emphasizes that these drugs interfere after DNA cleavage and before resealing, so DNA double-strand breaks accumulate.
Slides 7-9The eukaryotic genome: linear DNA, telomeres, and repetitive sequencesLO 3
  • Nuclear chromosomes are linear; mitochondrial DNA is circular.
  • Most eukaryotic genes are segmented into exons retained in mature mRNA and introns removed during splicing.
  • Chromosome ends contain a 3' overhang and long stretches of repeated hexameric telomere sequence.

Slides 10-14Chromatin is DNA plus histones, and it is dynamicLO 3-4

Histones are basic, lysine/arginine-rich proteins. Two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4 form the histone octamer; roughly 150 bp of DNA wraps around the core to form a nucleosome. Linker DNA between nucleosomes associates with H1. Scaffold proteins support higher-order compaction.

Euchromatin

Open/lightly condensed, accessible, and generally transcriptionally active.

Heterochromatin

Highly condensed, less accessible, and generally transcriptionally silent.

Key concept: the euchromatin/heterochromatin state is reversible and can respond to hormones, nutrients, drugs, xenobiotics, metals, and infectious stimuli. These changes can be inherited through somatic cell divisions without changing the DNA sequence itself.

Methylation closes; acetylation opens. Do not apply that shortcut to histone methylation, which is residue-specific.
Slides 15-24RNA structure, major RNA classes, and eukaryote-specific RNAsLO 5

RNA is usually single stranded, contains ribose and U instead of T, and folds through intramolecular base pairing. RNA can carry information, form structural scaffolds, regulate gene expression, and catalyze reactions.

RNALecture anchorHigh-yield features
tRNAAdaptor for translation75-85 nt; many modified bases; cloverleaf secondary structure; L-shaped tertiary structure; 3' CCA amino-acid attachment site; anticodon at opposite end
rRNAStructural/catalytic core of ribosomeMost abundant RNA; prokaryotes 23S/16S/5S; eukaryotes 28S/18S/5.8S/5S; 23S/28S peptidyl-transferase function
mRNAProtein-coding informationLeast abundant major RNA; short half-life; eukaryotic mRNA has 5' 7-methylguanosine cap and 3' poly(A) tail; bacteria are commonly polycistronic, eukaryotic mRNAs generally monocistronic

Functional eukaryotic RNAs

hnRNA = mRNA precursor. snRNA = nuclear, U-rich RNA used in pre-mRNA maturation/splicing. scRNA = cytosolic RNA associated with proteins involved in signal-peptide recognition.

Regulatory eukaryotic RNAs

lncRNA >200 nt; can recruit chromatin-remodeling machinery. Xist silences the X chromosome from which it is transcribed. miRNA ~19-25 nt and mediates post-transcriptional silencing by decreasing mRNA stability and/or translation.

RNA is not just a messenger. The lecture explicitly uses RNA as information (mRNA), adaptor (tRNA), structure/catalyst (rRNA), processing machinery (snRNA), and regulator (miRNA/lncRNA).

Exam trapsDetails worth protecting from mix-upsAll LOs
Trap 1: Denaturation breaks hydrogen-bonding/base-stacking interactions, not the phosphodiester backbone.
Trap 2: High stringency is not "high salt." It is higher temperature and lower salt.
Trap 3: DNA methylation generally represses promoter activity, but histone methylation is not uniformly activating or repressing.
Trap 4: Acetylation of lysine removes its positive charge; lysine methylation does not.
Trap 5: Eukaryotic mRNA cap and poly(A) tail are post-transcriptional modifications.
30-second finish
Gyrase = bacterial type II topo. Telomeres/repeats distinguish key eukaryotic chromosome features. Euchromatin is open; heterochromatin is closed. DNMT closes; HAT opens; HDAC closes. tRNA adapts, rRNA builds/catalyzes, mRNA encodes, Xist silences, miRNA suppresses after transcription.
