Bacterial Genetics & Virulence Factors
Slides 5-10Horizontal transfer breaks the family treeLO 1

Vertical transfer passes genes from parent cell to daughter cells. Horizontal gene transfer moves DNA between cells and can rapidly spread traits such as resistance or virulence across a bacterial population.

Deck mnemonic: transformation = USB stick (free DNA), conjugation = direct cable (cell contact), transduction = virus email (bacteriophage vector).

Slides 11-16Transformation: naked DNA uptakeLO 1
Donor DNA released->competent recipient takes up DNA->recombines into chromosome or persists as plasmid
Diagnostic clue: extracellular DNA is exposed, so DNase blocks transformation. The deck contrasts this with conjugation and transduction, which remain DNase resistant.
Historical logic: Griffith demonstrated a transferable transforming principle; Avery-MacLeod-McCarty identified DNA as that principle; Hershey-Chase independently showed that phage DNA, not protein, enters bacteria.
Slides 17-22Conjugation: direct cell-to-cell DNA transferLO 1-2

Gram-negative classic

F plasmid promotes direct contact through the sex pilus; DNA is copied from donor to recipient.

Hfr

When the F factor integrates into the bacterial chromosome, conjugation can drag adjacent chromosomal genes into the recipient.

Gram-positive twist: the deck emphasizes Enterococcus faecalis, where peptide pheromones induce donor adhesins and cell clumping instead of a classic sex pilus.
Conjugation clues: requires contact, is DNase resistant, and is especially effective at moving large plasmids carrying multiple resistance determinants.
Slides 23-28Transduction: bacteriophage-mediated gene transferLO 1 & 3

A bacteriophage packages bacterial DNA or carries its own accessory genes, then delivers DNA into another bacterium. Bacterial cells do not need to touch.

FormMechanismCargo pattern
Generalized transductionAccidental packaging of host DNA during phage productionPotentially random bacterial genes
Specialized transductionImprecise excision of a temperate prophageGenes adjacent to the prophage insertion site

Lytic phage

Replicates, assembles progeny, and lyses the host cell.

Temperate phage

Can integrate into the bacterial chromosome as a prophage; induction can later return it to a lytic cycle.

DNase does not stop transduction: the transferred DNA is physically protected within the phage particle.
Slides 29-32Horizontal transfer accelerates antibiotic resistanceLO 2
Resistance gene appears in one lineage->plasmid/mobile element captures it->horizontal transfer->rapid population spread
Deck anchors: MRSA carries mecA; vancomycin-resistant enterococci can carry vanA. Clinically important resistance often spreads by acquired mobile DNA rather than by waiting for each lineage to evolve the same mutation independently.
Slides 33-40Lysogenic conversion turns phage genes into virulence factorsLO 3
Lysogenic conversion occurs when a temperate phage integrates as a prophage and a phage-encoded gene changes the bacterial phenotype - classically by adding toxin production.

Slides 38-45Genetics changes treatment decisionsLO 2-3
Suspected EHEC/STEC: the lecture emphasizes supportive care and HUS monitoring rather than reflex antibiotic therapy, because prophage induction can increase Shiga-toxin output. Antimotility agents are also avoided in the deck's clinical framing.

Phage therapy

The deck closes by contrasting phages as agents of horizontal gene transfer with their potential therapeutic use against difficult bacterial infections. The clinical-development point is secondary to the exam-relevant transfer/virulence mechanisms.

Exam trapsIdentify the route from the vignette clueAll LOs
Trap 1: DNase sensitivity strongly points to transformation.
Trap 2: A pilus or direct donor-recipient contact points to conjugation.
Trap 3: A bacteriophage vector points to transduction; a prophage toxin points to lysogenic conversion.
Trap 4: Hfr means the F factor is integrated into the bacterial chromosome.
Trap 5: Generalized = random bacterial DNA; specialized = genes near the prophage site.
30-second finish
Transformation = naked DNA/DNase sensitive. Conjugation = contact/plasmids/DNase resistant. Transduction = phage/DNase resistant. These same routes spread resistance. Temperate phages can also add toxin genes by lysogenic conversion.
