Official Week 4 Objectives

1. Summarize initiation, elongation, and termination of transcription and compare prokaryotic with eukaryotic transcription.
2. Identify the molecular targets of rifampin and alpha-amanitin.
3. Describe post-transcriptional processing of eukaryotic mRNA and explain how defects in processing can cause disease.
4. Identify transcription/RNA-processing defects relevant to systemic lupus erythematosus and autosomal-dominant retinitis pigmentosa and connect them to therapeutic thinking.

  • The deck emphasizes that RNA polymerase lacks the 3' to 5' exonuclease proofreading system characteristic of replicative DNA polymerases.

Slides 7-12, 45-46Prokaryotic transcription: one RNAP, sigma-directed initiation, coupled translationLO 1-2
Bacterial RNAP holoenzyme = core enzyme (2 alpha, beta, beta-prime, omega) + sigma factor. Sigma directs promoter recognition; beta is the catalytic subunit emphasized by the rifampin mechanism.

Typical bacterial promoters contain -35 and -10 (Pribnow box) cis-regulatory elements recognized by the RNAP holoenzyme. Because bacteria lack a nucleus, transcription and translation are spatially and temporally coupled.

Termination modeCore logic
Rho-independent (intrinsic)RNA forms a terminator hairpin followed by a U-rich tract; the transcription complex destabilizes and RNA is released.
Rho-dependentRho helicase binds a rut site, uses ATP to move along RNA, catches the paused polymerase, and separates RNA from the DNA/template complex.

Promoter / enhancer logic

Core promoter elements and transcription-factor binding sites recruit the transcription machinery. Enhancers can act at a distance by DNA looping; silencers/repressors decrease transcription.

Pol II initiation

TBP helps initiate assembly at the promoter. TFIIH has helicase activity that opens DNA and kinase activity that phosphorylates the Pol II CTD, promoting transition into elongation.

Eukaryotic compartment rule: transcription occurs in the nucleus while translation occurs in the cytoplasm, creating space and time for RNA processing before translation.

Slides 23-25Eukaryotic pre-mRNA must be capped, spliced, polyadenylated, and exportedLO 3

5' m7G cap

Added by a capping complex associated with Pol II. Supports nuclear export, protects against degradation, and promotes translation.

Splicing

Introns removed and exons joined by the spliceosome.

3' poly(A) tail

Pre-mRNA is cleaved downstream of the AAUAAA signal and poly(A) polymerase adds the tail.

Nuclear export

Only appropriately processed transcripts are efficiently exported for cytoplasmic translation.

Cap functions: export, stability, translation. The deck notes that nuclear transcripts are capped; prokaryotic and mitochondrial mRNAs do not use this nuclear Pol II capping system.
Slides 26-34Splicing creates mature mRNA and enables proteome diversityLO 3-4

The spliceosome is built from small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs). Splice-site recognition depends on the 5' donor site, an intronic branch site, and the 3' acceptor site. Alternative splicing allows one gene to produce multiple mRNA/protein isoforms.

Disease anchorRNA-processing connection in deck
Autosomal-dominant retinitis pigmentosaSeveral adRP genes encode pre-mRNA processing/splicing factors, including PRPF-family proteins; photoreceptors have high transcript/protein-processing demands.
Systemic lupus erythematosusAutoantibodies target nuclear RNA-processing components, including snRNP/Sm-associated antigens discussed in the deck.
SLE framing: the high-yield relationship here is the spliceosomal/snRNP antigen connection, not that every case of SLE is caused by a primary germline spliceosome mutation.
Slides 35-40Polyadenylation controls stability, translation, and 3' UTR regulationLO 3
AAUAAA signal transcribed->endonucleolytic cleavage ~10-30 nt downstream->poly(A) polymerase adds ~80-250 A residues
Poly(A) tail functions: supports transcription termination, nuclear export, mRNA stability, and translation. Progressive tail shortening is associated with reduced translation and eventual mRNA degradation.
Alternative polyadenylation: using different cleavage/poly(A) sites changes 3' UTR length. A shorter 3' UTR may remove miRNA-binding sites and increase protein expression; a longer 3' UTR can preserve more regulatory sites.
Exam trapsProtect polymerase, drug, and processing relationshipsAll LOs
Trap 1: RNA polymerase does not require a primer.
Trap 2: Bacterial sigma factor selects promoters; it is not the catalytic subunit.
Trap 3: Rifampin targets bacterial RNAP beta; alpha-amanitin targets eukaryotic Pol II.
Trap 4: Pol I makes 45S pre-rRNA; Pol II makes mRNA; Pol III makes tRNA + 5S rRNA.
Trap 5: 5' cap is 7-methylguanosine; the poly(A) signal is AAUAAA near the 3' end.
Trap 6: Alternative splicing changes exon composition; alternative polyadenylation changes the 3' end/UTR.
30-second finish
RNA synthesis is 5' to 3' and primer independent. Bacteria: one RNAP + sigma, -10/-35 promoters, rho-dependent/independent termination. Eukaryotes: Pol I rRNA, Pol II mRNA, Pol III tRNA/5S. Rifampin hits bacterial beta; alpha-amanitin hits Pol II. Mature mRNA gets cap + splice + poly(A) tail.
