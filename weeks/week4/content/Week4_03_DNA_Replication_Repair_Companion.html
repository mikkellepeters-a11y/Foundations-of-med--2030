Slides 3-7Replication framework and polymerase logicLO 1
  • Replication occurs in S phase and is semiconservative: each daughter duplex contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand.
  • Prokaryotes use a single principal origin; eukaryotes use multiple origins. Replication proceeds bidirectionally from each origin.
  • Helicase separates strands using ATP. DNA polymerase extends only from a pre-existing 3'-OH and synthesizes 5' to 3'.
  • Leading strand is continuous; lagging strand is discontinuous and contains Okazaki fragments that must be joined by ligase.

Proofreading is 3' to 5' exonuclease activity. The wrong terminal nucleotide is removed so synthesis can continue. Eukaryotic RNA primers are removed by RNase H.
Slides 4-9Replication enzymes as drug targetsLO 1

Chain termination

DNA polymerase adds the incoming nucleotide to the 3'-OH of the growing strand. Nucleoside analogs lacking a usable 3'-OH can terminate elongation.

Topoisomerase trapping

Replication creates positive supercoils ahead of the fork. Topoisomerases transiently break DNA, allow strand rotation/passage, and reseal it.

TargetDrug examples from deckConsequence
Bacterial gyrase / topo IICiprofloxacin, novobiocinReplication/transcription block; DNA double-strand breaks accumulate
Human topo IIDoxorubicin, etoposideStabilize cleavage complex after DNA break; DSBs
Human topo ICamptothecinSingle-strand breaks
Topo I versus II: type I makes a single-strand nick and is ATP independent; type II breaks both strands, relaxes two supercoils at a time, and uses ATP.
Slides 10-11End-replication problem, telomerase, aging, and cancerLO 2
Linear chromosome end->Terminal RNA primer removed->No upstream 3'-OH to fill final gap->Telomere shortens without telomerase

Telomerase is a ribonucleoprotein reverse transcriptase: its RNA component provides the template, and its protein component catalyzes RNA-dependent DNA synthesis to extend chromosome ends.

Somatic cells

The lecture emphasizes low/absent telomerase in most somatic cells, progressive telomere shortening, and replicative senescence after a finite number of divisions.

Stem/cancer cells

Stem cells maintain telomerase. The deck notes telomerase activity in a large majority of human cancers, supporting continued proliferation and making telomerase an anticancer target.

Telomerase is RNA-dependent DNA polymerase. It is not an RNA polymerase and does not copy DNA into RNA.

Slides 21-32DNA repair pathways: recognize the lesion, then choose the right repair logicLO 4

General repair logic: identify the lesion, remove or reverse damaged material, restore correct sequence with DNA synthesis when needed, and ligate the backbone.

PathwayBest lesion anchorMechanism / key proteins
Direct reversalO6-alkylguanineMGMT transfers alkyl group to itself and is then inactivated/degraded
Mismatch repair (MMR)Replication mismatch; small insertion/deletion loopMLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, EPCAM-associated pathway; scans for mismatches after replication

Nucleotide excision repair (NER)Bulky helix distortion, thymine dimer, bulky adductExcises a short oligonucleotide containing lesion; polymerase fills; ligase seals
Homologous recombination (HR)Double-strand break with sister-template availableConservative, high-fidelity template-directed repair; late S/G2; BRCA1/2 promote HR

Translesion synthesis (TLS)Replication-blocking lesionSpecialized bypass synthesis lets replication continue across damaged template
BER = bad base. NER = bulky distortion. MMR = replication mismatch. HR/NHEJ = double-strand break.

MMR disease logic: mismatch repair defects do not create one specific mutation. They create a mutator phenotype, especially microsatellite instability, so many genes across multiple tissues can acquire pathogenic mutations.
Slides 33-34Why DNA-repair genes are tumor suppressorsLO 5

Cells experience enormous numbers of DNA modifications. Repair pathways normally prevent these lesions from becoming fixed mutations. When damage is substantial, checkpoint systems can pause the cell cycle; p53-centered pathways can promote senescence or apoptosis if damage cannot be safely repaired.

Repair-gene loss->Mutation burden rises->Genome instability->Oncogenic/tumor-suppressor hits accumulate->Cancer risk rises
Repair proteins are "guardians of the genome." Their normal function suppresses tumor formation; loss of function therefore behaves as tumor-suppressor loss.
Exam trapsKeep pathway and enzyme directionality straightAll LOs
Trap 1: DNA synthesis is 5' to 3'; proofreading is 3' to 5' exonuclease.
Trap 2: Topo I creates SSBs; topo II creates DSBs.
Trap 3: Telomerase uses RNA as a template to synthesize DNA.
Trap 4: BER removes a damaged base first; NER removes an oligonucleotide around a bulky lesion.
Trap 5: HR is high fidelity and template dependent; NHEJ is template independent and more error prone.
Trap 6: Lynch = MMR; XP/Cockayne = NER; BRCA1/2 = HR support.
30-second finish
3'-OH required for extension; 3' to 5' proofreads. Topo inhibitors trap DNA breaks. Telomerase solves chromosome-end replication. UV makes pyrimidine dimers. MMR fixes replication errors, BER fixes small base lesions, NER fixes bulky distortions, HR/NHEJ fix DSBs. Repair-gene loss increases genome instability and cancer risk.
