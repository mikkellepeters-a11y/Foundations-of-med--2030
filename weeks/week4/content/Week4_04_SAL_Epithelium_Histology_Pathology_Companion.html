Official Week 4 SAL Objectives

1. Identify the parts of a light microscope and explain the operation of a microscope for viewing glass slides.
2. Demonstrate familiarity with Proscia software for viewing digital slides.
3. Identify simple squamous epithelium and distinguish the terms endothelium and mesothelium.
4. Identify simple cuboidal epithelium, locate its basement membrane, and explain basement-membrane function and appearance.
5. Identify simple columnar, pseudostratified columnar, and stratified squamous epithelia; identify cilia/goblet cells and explain how plane of section alters appearance.
6. Identify loose (areolar), dense irregular, dense regular, elastic, and adipose connective tissue and discuss composition/function.
7. Compare normal tissue with tissue involved by pathologic processes.
8. Recognize basic stains and preparation methods used to evaluate cellular macromolecules and tissue specimens.
9. Apply basic histopathology terminology and recognize techniques used for direct tissue sampling.
SAL Skill 1Light microscope: orient first, then increase magnificationLO 1

Core parts

Eyepiece/ocular, objective lenses, revolving nosepiece, stage/mechanical stage, condenser, iris diaphragm, light source, coarse-focus control, and fine-focus control.

Safe workflow

Start on the lowest-power objective, center the specimen, bring the image into focus, then move to higher power. Use fine focus at high magnification; avoid driving the objective into the slide.

Field-of-view rule: increasing objective magnification decreases the field of view and depth of field. Always locate the structure at low power before zooming in.
Plane-of-section trap: a three-dimensional tube cut transversely can look circular, while the same tube cut obliquely/longitudinally looks elongated. Do not reclassify an epithelium solely from one oddly oriented profile.
Deck pp. 12-17How a specimen becomes a slideLO 8-9
Fixation→Gross examination / tissue selection→Dehydration→Clearing→Paraffin infiltration→Embedding→Microtome sectioning→Staining

Fixation

The deck emphasizes buffered formalin as the usual fixative. Fixation preserves tissue architecture and limits degradation.

Grossing and selection

Specimens are weighed/measured, serially sectioned, palpated, photographed when useful, and sampled from key areas. The goal is to document the lesion and its relationship to surrounding structures.

Embedding and sectioning

Processed tissue is embedded in paraffin wax, then a microtome cuts thin sections (deck: approximately 4-10 μm). Histology is a 2-D slice of a 3-D structure, so orientation matters.

White space is information: it may represent a true lumen/void or material lost during routine processing. Fat is commonly dissolved during clearing, so adipocytes often appear as large empty spaces with thin peripheral cytoplasm.

IHC / IF

Antibodies detect specific antigens. Immunohistochemistry produces a visible chromogenic reaction; immunofluorescence uses a fluorescent label.

Electron microscopy

Electrons have a shorter wavelength than visible light and therefore higher resolving power. SEM emphasizes surfaces; TEM emphasizes intracellular ultrastructure.

Objective-driven tissue IDEpithelia: classify by layers, then cell shapeLO 3-5
EpitheliumMorphologyCommon recognition clue / role
Simple squamousOne layer of flattened cells with flattened nucleiThin exchange/lining surface. Endothelium lines vessels; mesothelium covers serosal surfaces.
Simple cuboidalOne layer; cells about as tall as wide; round central nucleiCommon in small ducts/tubules. Basement membrane is at the basal interface.
Simple columnarOne layer of tall cells; nuclei usually aligned toward the basal halfAbsorption/secretion; goblet cells may be present.
Pseudostratified columnarNuclei at different levels, but all epithelial cells contact basement membraneOften ciliated with goblet cells in respiratory tract.
Stratified squamousMultiple layers; surface-most cells are flattenedProtection from abrasion; may be keratinized or nonkeratinized.
Basement membrane: extracellular matrix interface that anchors/supports epithelium, helps organize polarity, and forms a barrier between epithelium and underlying connective tissue. It may appear as a thin eosinophilic line in some H&E sections, but ultrastructure is best resolved by electron microscopy; PAS can accentuate basement membranes.
Invasion is a basement-membrane problem: epithelial dysplasia confined above the basement membrane is non-invasive; malignant epithelial cells that breach it are invasive carcinoma.

Deck pp. 27-33Histopathology language: recognize what changedLO 7, 9

Adaptation

Hypertrophy = increased cell size. Atrophy = decreased cell size. Hyperplasia = increased cell number. Metaplasia = replacement of one mature cell type by another.

Neoplasia

Neoplasia is proliferation beyond normal regulation. A neoplasm is a tumor; behavior can be benign, premalignant/dysplastic, or malignant depending on the lesion.

TermMeaning / morphology
DysplasiaDisordered/abnormal growth with altered architecture, cytologic atypia, and increased turnover. Severe epithelial dysplasia may be termed carcinoma in situ.
PleomorphismVariation in cell/nuclear size and shape.
AnaplasiaMarked loss of differentiation; cells look very unlike their tissue of origin.
HyperchromasiaNucleus appears abnormally dark due to increased/coarse chromatin.
High N:C ratioLarge nucleus with relatively scant cytoplasm; common cytologic warning sign.
InvasionGrowth beyond normal confines, especially epithelial breach of basement membrane.
DesmoplasiaReactive fibrotic stromal response that may accompany invasive carcinoma.
MetastasisSpread of malignant cells to noncontiguous organs/tissues.
Carcinoma language
Carcinoma = malignant epithelial neoplasm. Adenocarcinoma = malignant epithelial tumor showing gland formation. Squamous cell carcinoma = malignant tumor derived from squamous epithelium. Adenoma = gland-forming neoplasm, often benign or premalignant depending on context.
Deck pp. 34-37Proscia: digital-slide workflowLO 2

What you need to be able to do

  • Open the assigned group/repository and select a digital slide.
  • Use the thumbnail/minimap for rapid navigation.
  • Zoom in/out with the magnification controls, mouse wheel, or trackpad gesture.
  • Toggle annotations on/off and read the associated text.
  • Move from low-power architecture to higher-power cellular detail, just as you would with a physical microscope.
Digital histology is still histology: begin with overall architecture. If you start at maximum zoom, you can lose the relationship between epithelium, basement membrane, stroma, and lesion boundaries.
Deck pp. 38-41Pathology case anchors: cervix and colonLO 7, 9

Cervix / colposcopy / LEEP

Colposcopy examines the cervix for vascular and surface abnormalities. Acetic acid can produce acetowhite change in abnormal squamous epithelium. LEEP uses an electrified wire loop to excise a pathologic region for diagnosis/treatment.

LAST terminology

LSIL = low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion with atypia limited to the lower portion of the epithelium. HSIL = high-grade lesion with atypia extending through a larger fraction of epithelial thickness.

Colon

Colonic adenomas are dysplastic/premalignant glandular lesions that often appear as polyps. Colonoscopy can both detect and remove them before invasion develops.

Direct tissue sampling

Biopsy, excision, LEEP, endoscopic biopsy/polypectomy, cytology, and surgical resection are examples of methods that provide tissue/cells for pathologic evaluation.

Final passSAL exam traps and master recallLO 1-9
Trap 1: simple vs stratified is based on the number of layers contacting the surface, not how many nuclei you can see.
Trap 2: pseudostratified epithelium looks multilayered, but every epithelial cell reaches the basement membrane.
Trap 3: endothelium and mesothelium are specialized names for simple squamous epithelium based on location.
Trap 4: hematoxylin stains nucleic-acid-rich structures blue-purple; eosin stains protein-rich structures pink-red.
Trap 5: dysplasia/carcinoma in situ is not the same as invasion. Breach of the basement membrane changes the category.
Trap 6: hypertrophy = bigger cells; hyperplasia = more cells.
Trap 7: high magnification is not the first step. Architecture at low power prevents misidentification.
30-second finish
Start low power. Think in 3D. Formalin → processing → paraffin → microtome → stain. H&E: nuclei blue-purple, proteins/collagen pink. PAS marks glycogen/mucin/basement membranes/fungi. Endothelium/mesothelium = simple squamous. Pseudostratified cells all touch basement membrane. Dense regular = parallel; dense irregular = multidirectional. Dysplasia is atypical growth; invasion crosses the basement membrane; metastasis spreads.
