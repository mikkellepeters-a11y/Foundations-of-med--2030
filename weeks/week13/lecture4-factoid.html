Connective Tissue Histology — Factoid Sheet

Week 13 · MSK–Skin · Lecture + professor handout · Rapid review
Master framework: connective tissue = cells + ECM • collagen → tensile strength • elastic fibers → recoil • GAG-bound water → compression • fibroblasts → repair/fibrosis.

Rapid orientation

CT = cells + ECM

ECM = fibers (collagen, elastic) + ground substance (GAGs, proteoglycans, glycoproteins).

Mechanics: collagen → tension; elastin → recoil; GAG-bound water → compression.

Identify the tissue

Loose CT: open matrix, scattered cells. Dense regular: parallel bundles, tendon/ligament. Dense irregular: multidirectional dermis. Adipose: large clear spaces with peripheral nuclei.

MECHANISM CHAINS · Allergen → mast-cell activation → histamine release → permeability → edema/inflammatory recruitment.

Macrophage disease: TB resists killing by blocking phagosome–lysosome fusion; Leishmania survives in lysosomal compartment; foam cells connect lipid overload to plaque inflammation.

Collagen: synthesis, types, and recognition

3 α-chains → triple helix → extracellular fibrils → fibers → bundles. Glycine every third position helps tightly pack helix. Hydroxyproline stabilizes.

EXAM TRAP · Inside: procollagen, propeptides retained → no fibril assembly. Outside: propeptides cleaved → tropocollagen → fibril assembly.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

1. Collagen hydroxylation → vitamin C requirement → scurvy (bleeding/poor healing). 2. Type IV collagen → glomerular basement membrane → Alport (hematuria, ear/eye findings). 3. Fibrillin → aortic elastic structure → Marfan association. 4. Dystrophin–glycoprotein complex → sarcolemmal mechanical integrity → Duchenne. These are selective bridge points; only some were taught explicitly in the lecture/handout.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

1. Most abundant fibrillar collagen in tendons and dermis?
Answer: Type I.
2. Intracellular collagen triple-helix precursor?
Answer: Procollagen, with terminal propeptides.
3. Why glycine every third collagen residue?
Answer: Its tiny side chain fits the tight core of the triple helix.
4. What does aggrecan accomplish in cartilage?
Answer: Negatively charged GAGs retain water, resisting compression.
5. Which collagen supports glomerular basement membrane?
Answer: Type IV.
6. What is a key white-adipocyte histology clue?
Answer: Apparently empty lipid space with a peripheral nucleus.
7. Tissue cell releasing histamine?
Answer: Mast cell.
8. What form of connective tissue is tendon?
Answer: Dense regular connective tissue.
9. What histologic change signals tendinopathy?
Answer: Disorganized collagen, increased rounded cells, and sometimes new vessels/nerves.
10. What is the major cell depositing collagen in wound repair?
Answer: Activated fibroblast.
Last 30 seconds: fibroblast makes ECM • I = dermis/tendon/bone • II = cartilage • III = reticular • IV = basal lamina/Alport • IX/XII = FACIT • fibrillin organizes elastin • aggrecan holds water • wound repair relies on fibroblast collagen.
