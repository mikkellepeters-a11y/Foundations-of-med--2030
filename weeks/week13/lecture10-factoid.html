Acneiform Disorders, Pigment, and Hair — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Dermatology · Matthew Belcher, MD, FAAD, FACMS · Rapid pre-exam retrieval
Mental model: morphology + distribution → mechanism → discriminator → treatment.

Acne: diagnostic anchor

  • Comedones = acne. Often + papules/pustules.
  • Typical sites: face, chest, back.
  • Sequelae: scarring + post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
  • Pathogenesis: follicular keratinization + sebum → plugging/comedone → bacterial/inflammatory progression.

Acne treatment ladder

  • Comedonal: topical retinoid; ≥6 wk.
  • Mild inflammatory: retinoid + benzoyl peroxide/topical antibiotic.
  • Moderate: retinoid + BPO + oral antibiotic (lecture: doxycycline 100 mg BID).
  • Hormonal female pattern: OCP or spironolactone; often chin/jawline, premenstrual.
  • Nodulocystic: often isotretinoin.

Isotretinoin

  • Oral retinoid; severe nodulocystic acne.
  • ~6–8 months, weight-based goal dose.
  • Monitor: LFTs + lipids + monthly pregnancy test.
  • Course AE list: dryness, photosensitivity, IBD, hair loss, joint pain, depression, LFT/TG elevation, severe birth defects.
  • iPLEDGE + severe teratogen.

Rosacea: no comedones

  • Immune + neurovascular dysregulation; possible Demodex.
  • Triggers: alcohol, sun, spicy foods.
  • ETT: erythema/telangiectasia → triggers, sunscreen, gentle care, laser, β-blocker, brimonidine.
  • Papulopustular: metronidazole, ivermectin, low-dose doxycycline.
  • Phymatous: sebaceous hypertrophy/fibrosis → surgery/laser.
  • Ocular: blepharitis/dry eyes → tears + low-dose doxycycline.

Hidradenitis suppurativa

  • AKA acne inversa.
  • Follicular occlusion → sterile abscesses → sinus tracts/scars.
  • Sites: axilla, inframammary, groin, buttocks.
  • Risks: obesity + tobacco. Starts around puberty.
  • All stages: weight loss + smoking cessation.
  • I: no tracts/scars. II: few. III: interconnected/extensive.

HS treatment by stage

  • I: topical antibiotics, antiseptic wash, intralesional steroid, oral antibiotics.
  • II: oral antibiotics; spironolactone/finasteride; adalimumab; excision.
  • III: biologics listed in lecture + excision.
  • Chronic/progressive; early therapy minimizes complications.

Primary hyperhidrosis

  • Excess sweating with no underlying disease.
  • Palms, soles, axillae; ~80% family history in lecture.
  • Focal visible ≥6 mo, no secondary cause + ≥2: bilateral/symmetric, impairs activity, ≥1/wk, onset <25, family history, stops in sleep.

Hyperhidrosis treatment

  • Aluminum chloride 12–20%: occludes eccrine glands.
  • Glycopyrrolate / oxybutynin: muscarinic blockade.
  • Botulinum toxin: blocks ACh release; repeat ~6–9 mo.
  • Secondary examples: fever, hyperthyroidism, lymphoma/cancer, listed medications.

Pigment: three-way sort

  • Vitiligo: depigmentation; autoimmune melanocyte loss; acral/periorificial + autoimmune associations.
  • Pityriasis alba: child + face + round hypopigmented patch + slight scale + atopy.
  • Tinea versicolor: Malassezia; usually hypopigmented trunk/back patches; summer contrast.

Pigment treatments

  • Vitiligo: topical steroids, narrow-band UVB, ruxolitinib.
  • Pityriasis alba: moisturizers or 1% hydrocortisone; resolves with age.
  • Tinea versicolor: topical azoles, fluconazole, or OTC dandruff shampoo.

Hair loss: pattern shortcut

  • Smooth circular patch, no scale: alopecia areata.
  • Scaly/itchy patch: tinea capitis.
  • Diffuse sudden shedding after stressor: telogen effluvium.
  • Frontal/vertex loss or widening part: androgenic alopecia.

Alopecia areata

  • Non-scarring; T-cell autoimmune follicle destruction.
  • Totalis = all scalp hair; universalis = all body hair.
  • Associations: atopy, Hashimoto thyroiditis, vitiligo.
  • Localized: topical/intralesional steroid, topical calcineurin inhibitor.
  • Widespread/rapid: oral steroid or tofacitinib per lecture.

Tinea capitis

  • Dermatophyte infection of hair follicles.
  • Scale + itch distinguish from alopecia areata.
  • Dx: scraping/microscopy or fungal culture.
  • Tx: oral terbinafine, griseofulvin, or fluconazole for 4–8 wk.

Telogen effluvium

  • Diffuse sudden hair loss after illness, hospitalization, pregnancy, surgery, stress.
  • Rapid hair cycling → increased shedding.
  • ~100 hairs/day normal anchor.
  • Anagen → catagen → telogen.
  • Self-resolves in 6–12 mo.

Androgenic alopecia

  • Often familial.
  • Men: frontal + vertex loss.
  • Women: widening part line.
  • Androgens → follicular miniaturization.
  • Tx: minoxidil; finasteride, spironolactone, dutasteride.

Drug / mechanism pairs

  • Retinoid → normalize follicular keratinization.
  • Spironolactone → anti-androgen; ↓ hormone-driven sebum.
  • Brimonidine → α2 agonist; vasoconstriction.
  • Metronidazole in rosacea → anti-inflammatory emphasis.
  • Ivermectin → targets Demodex.
  • Glycopyrrolate/oxybutynin → muscarinic blockade.
  • Botulinum toxin → blocks ACh release.

Sequela / urgency anchors

  • Acne: permanent scarring + PIH.
  • HS: sinus tracts, scars, pain, disfigurement, malodor.
  • Vitiligo: autoimmune association pattern.
  • Tinea capitis: infection, not autoimmune alopecia.
  • Hair loss can have major emotional/psychological impact.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  1. Acne: sebum/androgens + abnormal desquamation + C. acnes + inflammation.
  2. Isotretinoin: vitamin A derivative; classic teratogenicity/toxicity connection.
  3. Vitiligo: autoimmune melanocyte destruction.
  4. Tinea versicolor: Malassezia + “spaghetti and meatballs” microscopy.
  5. Androgenetic alopecia: DHT contributes; finasteride blocks 5α-reductase; minoxidil treats.
