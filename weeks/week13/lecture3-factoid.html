Ion Channels and Action Potentials — Factoid Sheet

MSK–Skin · Week 13 · Lecture 3 · Foundations-style rapid pre-exam retrieval
Mental model: Gradient × permeability → voltage → graded summation → Na⁺ upstroke → K⁺ recovery → refractory period → impulse propagation.

Ion distributions

  • K⁺ and impermeant proteins: predominantly inside
  • Na⁺ and Cl⁻: predominantly outside
  • Resting permeability PK >> PNa
  • Ion influx/efflux follows electrochemical gradients

Nernst equilibrium

  • Eion=RT/zF × ln(out/in)
  • For monovalent cation at 37°C: 61.5 × log10(out/in)
  • No net flux at Eion
  • ECl sign must account for negative valence

Resting Vm / GHK

  • Increased permeability → Vm closer to E of that ion
  • Lecture PK:PNa:PCl ≈ 50:1:10
  • K⁺ leak dominates neuronal rest
  • Pump uses ATP: 3 Na⁺ OUT / 2 K⁺ IN

Channel gating

  • Leak: resting conductivity
  • Voltage-gated: membrane potential change
  • Ligand-gated: chemical messenger binds
  • Mechanical: membrane deformation
  • Signal-gated: intracellular messenger

K⁺ disturbances

  • ↓ extracellular K⁺ → more negative EK → hyperpolarization
  • ↑ extracellular K⁺ → less negative EK → depolarization
  • Severe hyperkalemia → Na⁺ inactivation → depolarization block
  • Clinical manifestations: weakness, dysrhythmias, paresthesias

Potentials

  • Depolarization = more positive
  • Hyperpolarization = more negative
  • Overshoot = positive Vm
  • Repolarization = toward rest
  • Graded = variable, decremental, summates; AP = all-or-none

Action potential phases

  • Threshold → voltage-gated Na⁺ opens
  • Na⁺ IN → rapid upstroke / overshoot
  • Na⁺ inactivates + delayed K⁺ opens
  • K⁺ OUT → repolarization
  • Persistent PK → after-hyperpolarization

Summation / refractory

  • Temporal: repeated activity at one input
  • Spatial: simultaneous inputs at different sites
  • Absolute refractory: Na⁺ channels unavailable
  • Relative refractory: stronger stimulus may succeed

Conduction & response

  • Myelin insulates internodes
  • Nodes of Ranvier regenerate AP: saltatory conduction
  • Demyelination → slowed/failed conduction
  • Terminal Ca²⁺ entry triggers release: board enrichment
  • Muscle excitation → Ca²⁺-mediated contraction: board enrichment

Toxins / clinical

  • Tetrodotoxin (pufferfish) → blocks voltage-gated Na⁺: board link
  • Apamin → Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ channel blocker (slide 58)
  • Capsaicin → sensory ending activation (slide 59)
  • TRP channels → temperature/chemical sensing
  • Ion channel abnormalities → channelopathies

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • Potassium imbalance → voltage change → muscle and heart excitability disturbances.
  • Severe hyperkalemia → persistent depolarization → Na⁺ channel inactivation / conduction block.
  • Tetrodotoxin → voltage-gated Na⁺ blockade → paralysis.
  • Myelin loss → failed efficient saltatory conduction.
