Integument — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Histology · Patricia V. Schoenlein · Rapid pre-exam retrieval
Mental model: basal renewal → differentiation → barrier • cell identity → pigment disorder • gland secretion → duct modification • receptor depth → sensation • failed junction → blister plane.

Skin: the three-layer map

  • Epidermis: avascular, stratified squamous cornifying epithelium; keratinocytes dominate.
  • Dermis: vascular connective tissue, glands, follicles, receptors.
  • Hypodermis: adipose-rich subcutis → cushioning, insulation, triglyceride energy storage, sliding.
  • Functions: barrier, sensation, thermoregulation, nutrient storage/synthesis, repair.
  • Vitamin D3: begins from 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin.

Epidermal layers: deep → superficial

  • Basale: one cuboidal/low-columnar row; stem cells + mitoses.
  • Spinosum: polyhedral cells + “spines” from desmosomal attachments.
  • Granulosum: 1–3 flattened layers; basophilic keratohyalin granules.
  • Lucidum: clear dead-cell layer in thick skin.
  • Corneum: anucleate squames, no organelles; most differentiated.

Junctions: attachment direction

  • Desmosome: cell ↔ cell; desmogleins/desmocollins; anchors keratin filaments.
  • Hemidesmosome: basal cell ↔ basal lamina.
  • Mechanics: junction anchors + keratin cables → resilience.
  • Clinical: desmosome failure → pemphigus; basal attachment failure → pemphigoid.

Granules → cornified barrier

  • Keratohyalin: not membrane-bound; protein/keratin reinforcement.
  • Odland/lamellar body: membrane-bound; exocytoses barrier lipids.
  • Envelope: lecture names loricrin, filaggrin, transglutaminase cross-linking; keratin core reinforces it.
  • Granulosum prepares → corneum protects.
  • Slide 19 mutation table: information, not memorization unless taught separately.

Turnover, thick skin, psoriasis

  • Basal division → differentiation → cornification → shedding. Renewal: ~28–45 days.
  • Thick/acral: palms/soles; prominent corneum + lucidum; no hair follicles; sweat glands present.
  • Thin/non-acral: most body skin; follicles + sebaceous glands; sweat glands present.
  • Psoriasis: red, silvery-scaled lesions; elbows/knees/scalp/trunk.
  • Parakeratosis = corneal nuclei; elongated rete ridges. Course: failed terminal differentiation + division above basale.

Epidermal cells: job ≠ neighborhood

  • Keratinocyte: barrier cell; receives melanin.
  • Melanocyte: basale; processes into spinosum; tyrosine → melanin → transfer to keratinocytes.
  • Langerhans: antigen sentinel → draining node → T-cell presentation; course calls macrophage-like.
  • Merkel: basal sensory cell, fingertips; light touch.
  • H&E trap: pigment does not prove melanocyte identity; targeted detection needed for definitive specialized-cell identification.

UV → pigment signaling

  • UV → keratinocyte DNA damage → p53 → POMC → MSH (+ β-endorphin).
  • MSH → melanocyte MC1 receptor → melanin production → transfer.
  • Pigment caps sun-facing side of keratinocyte nucleus → UV shielding.
  • Handout: reversible darkening → synthesis → longer-lasting tanning.

Pigment: three-way sort

  • Albinism: cells retained; impaired pigment synthesis/tyrosinase activity or transport; inherited.
  • OCA: course describes AR inheritance; skin/hair/eye pigment reduction, visual problems, ↑ skin-cancer risk.
  • Vitiligo: melanocytes destroyed → irregular complete depigmentation; probably autoimmune.
  • Melasma/chloasma: acquired hyperpigmentation; hormones + UV; “mask of pregnancy.”

Cancer: originating cell

  • BCC: basal cells/follicles; slow growing, locally invasive, rarely metastasizes.
  • SCC: epidermal keratinocytes.
  • Melanoma: malignant melanocyte; neural-crest origin; can be nonpigmented or noncutaneous.
  • Merkel-cell cancer: named only in this lecture.
  • FIRST AID: ABCDE recognition; Breslow depth correlates with metastatic risk (p. 494).

Dermis: papillary vs reticular

  • Papillary: superficial loose CT, fine type III collagen + elastic fibers, capillaries, nerve endings.
  • Reticular: deeper dense irregular CT, coarse type I collagen → multidirectional strength.
  • Papilla points up; rete ridge points down. Dovetail interlock → shear resistance + fingerprint architecture.
  • Superficial renewal preserves fingerprints; deep injury can permanently alter them.

Sebaceous gland: whole-cell secretion

  • Pale, lipid-rich lobules beside hair follicle.
  • Holocrine: entire degenerating cell + contents → follicle.
  • Sebum: fatty acids, cholesterol, squalene, triglycerides.
  • Arrector pili: adjacent smooth muscle; raises hair, not a gland.

Eccrine coil: three cell jobs

  • Coiled tubular gland in dermis/deep skin; opens onto surface; thick + thin skin.
  • Clear cells: bulk fluid/ultrafiltrate.
  • Dark cells: protein-containing secretion by exocytosis.
  • Myoepithelial: contraction expels product.
  • Merocrine: secretion without cell loss; sweat proteins contribute to chemical/antibacterial defense.

Ductal transport → salty sweat

  • Secretory coil makes fluid → duct removes NaCl → hypotonic surface sweat.
  • Duct: stratified cuboidal epithelium.
  • CFTR: ductal chloride reabsorption → dermal interstitium.
  • Defective CFTR → failed salt reabsorption → salty sweat.
  • Apocrine gland detail deferred by the lecture to GIGU/Endocrine.

Blood flow + evaporation → heat balance

  • Cutaneous plexus: dermis/hypodermis boundary.
  • Subpapillary plexus: papillary/reticular boundary; superficial loops.
  • Cold: AV shunting/reduced superficial flow → less heat loss.
  • Sweat evaporation: heat of vaporization → cooling.
  • Avascular epidermis: dermal nutrients reach it by diffusion.
  • Injection depth: intradermal → dermis; subcutaneous → hypodermis; IM → muscle; IV → vessel.

Hair: product + growth apparatus

  • Root: below surface; shaft: above; mature hair = dead cornified cells.
  • Follicle: epidermal invagination; bulb surrounds supporting dermal papilla.
  • Cuticle → cortex → medulla: protection → structure/color → loose inner core (only thick hairs).
  • Follicular melanocytes → color; arrector pili → erection.
  • Hair has stronger keratin cross-linking than surface squames.

Nails and derivative identity

  • Nail plate: hard, highly cross-linked cornified epidermis.
  • Germinal matrix at proximal root: produces plate.
  • Practical: plate, root, fold, matrix, eponychium/cuticle, lunula, hyponychium, distal phalanx.
  • Origin ≠ location: hair, nails, sebaceous/eccrine glands remain epidermal derivatives even within dermis.

Sensory receptors: depth → signal

  • Merkel: basal epidermal cell → sensitive light touch.
  • Meissner: papillary dermis; small elongated capsule, stacked Schwann cells → light touch.
  • Pacinian: deep dermis/hypodermis; onion-like lamellae → pressure/vibration.
  • Free endings: dermis into middle epidermis; unencapsulated → pain, hot/cold, light touch.

Cleavage plane → blister roof

  • Pemphigus: IgG to desmogleins → acantholysis → intraepidermal split.
  • Pemphigoid: BP180/type XVII collagen ± BP230/plakin → basal detachment → subepidermal split.
  • Course BP mechanism: antibody/T-cell signaling → complement/inflammatory recruitment → proteolytic injury.
  • FIRST AID: partial roof → flaccid; full epidermal roof → tense (p. 489).

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Which epidermal layer is the most differentiated?
  2. What connects a basal keratinocyte to basal lamina?
  3. Which granule releases barrier lipids?
  4. Why are nuclei in stratum corneum abnormal?
  5. How can a pigmented epidermal cell be a keratinocyte?
  6. Absent pigment with preserved melanocyte number suggests what?
  7. Why does defective CFTR produce salty sweat?
  8. Where are Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles found?
  9. What generates the nail plate, and what raises a hair?
  10. An epidermis-wide roof sits above a blister: which attachment failed?
Reveal rapid-fire answers
  1. Stratum corneum: anucleate, cornified squames.
  2. Hemidesmosomes; desmosomes instead connect neighboring keratinocytes.
  3. Membrane-bound lamellar/Odland body; keratohyalin granules support protein/keratin reinforcement.
  4. Normal squames lose nuclei; retention is parakeratosis, emphasized in psoriasis.
  5. It received melanin granules from a melanocyte.
  6. Albinism; vitiligo destroys melanocytes.
  7. Ductal chloride reabsorption is impaired; more salt remains in the final sweat.
  8. Meissner: dermal papillae/light touch. Pacinian: deep dermis/hypodermis/deep pressure and vibration.
  9. Germinal nail matrix generates the plate; smooth-muscle arrector pili raises hair.
  10. Basal cell-to-matrix attachment, classically hemidesmosomal injury in bullous pemphigoid.

COURSE-SOURCE NOTES

Course wording and source distinctions
  • Handout p. 75: states no epidermal tight junctions; BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: standard histology recognizes granular-layer tight junctions. Core shared fact: epidermis is avascular.
  • Handout p. 77: includes melanocyte number among normal pigment determinants; BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: normal skin-color variation mainly reflects melanin/melanosome production and distribution.
  • Handout p. 79: says hair cell division occurs “in the dermal papilla”; diagram distinguishes epithelium from papilla. BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: dividing hair matrix surrounds the supporting CT papilla.
  • BP terminology: handout heading “Bullous Pemphigus” refers to pemphigoid. BP180 = collagen XVII; BP230 = plakin; NC16A domain wording pertains to BP180.
  • Langerhans wording: course calls macrophage-like; BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: commonly classified as antigen-presenting dendritic cells.
  • Lipid storage: slides mention “cholesterol conversion”; slide 28 and handout separately identify triglyceride/fat storage. Do not infer a taught conversion pathway.

Sources: Integument slides pp. 1–72 + professor handout pp. 73–82. First Aid 2025 printed pp. 482, 484, 489, 494. Full explanations and lecture figures: companion.
