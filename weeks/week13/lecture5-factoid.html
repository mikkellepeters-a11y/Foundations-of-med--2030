Peripheral Nervous System & Spinal Cord Histology

MSK · WEEK 13 · LECTURE 05 · Rapid-Review Factoid · Schoenlein
ONE-LINE MEMORY MAP: soma/Nissl + dendrites receive → axon hillock/initial segment fires → axon transports and conducts → synapse/NMJ signals → Schwann myelination speeds → Wallerian degeneration + Schwann bands may repair → spinal horn + ganglion histology localize.

1 · Identify a neuron

  • Neuron soma: large euchromatic nucleus, conspicuous nucleolus, basophilic Nissl (RER).
  • Dendrites: receptive, spines form synaptic sites; may contain Nissl.
  • Axon hillock: pale; Nissl-negative; initial segment fires AP.
  • Glial nuclei: small, darker; neuropil = fine neuroglial process/synapse mesh.

2 · Neuron morphology

  • Multipolar: many dendrites + one axon; motor/interneurons.
  • Bipolar: one dendrite + one axon; special senses.
  • Pseudounipolar: bifurcating single process; DRG primary sensory.
  • PNS ganglion / nerve vs CNS nucleus / tract.

3 · Synapse and NMJ

  • Chemical: vesicle-rich presynaptic bouton → cleft → receptor-rich postsynapse.
  • Electrical: gap junction, direct fast ion flow.
  • NMJ: ACh from motor neuron → folded motor end plate → ACh receptors; AChE terminates.
  • Motor unit: one motor neuron + all fibers it supplies.

4 · Schwann / myelin

  • Schwann membrane wraps axon via mesaxon → compact myelin.
  • One Schwann = one myelinated internode / axon.
  • Nodes of Ranvier allow saltatory conduction.
  • Unmyelinated PNS axons still enclosed in Schwann cell; continuous conduction.
  • H&E: lipid myelin dissolves → white ring around axon.

5 · Nerve connective sheaths

  • Epineurium: entire nerve; dense CT, vessels.
  • Perineurium: each fascicle; blood–nerve barrier.
  • Endoneurium: thin loose CT around individual axons/Schwann units.
  • Practical: trace whole nerve → perineurial fascicle ring → single axonal profile.

6 · Axonal transport

  • Anterograde: kinesin; soma → axon terminal; vesicle components, organelles.
  • Retrograde: dynein; terminal → soma; growth factors, damage/vesicle return.
  • Not the same as the direction an electrical impulse travels.

7 · Peripheral nerve injury

  • Wallerian: distal axon + myelin fragmented → myelin ovoids.
  • Schwann cells + macrophages clear debris.
  • Schwann Bands of Büngner guide proximal regrowth.
  • Chromatolysis: soma RER/Nissl disperses during injury response.
  • Axonal vs demyelinating neuropathy: loss of axons vs myelin segments.

8 · Key diseases

  • CMT: hereditary, years; distal weakness, foot drop, pes cavus, sensory loss, low ankle reflexes.
  • GBS: acute postinfectious immune neuropathy; ascending weakness, areflexia, respiratory failure risk.
  • Chemo neuropathy: taxane / platinum / vinca classes are listed as possible causes.

9 · Spinal cord: locate

  • Gray central butterfly; white peripheral tracts.
  • Anterior horn: large lower motor-neuron somata.
  • Posterior horn: secondary sensory relay interneurons.
  • Lateral horn: sympathetic preganglionic neurons in thoracolumbar regions.
  • Central canal: ependymal cells.

10 · CNS glia / PNS ganglia

  • Oligodendrocyte CNS myelin; astrocyte BBB/homeostasis; microglia phagocyte; ependymal CSF lining.
  • DRG: packed pseudounipolar neurons + complete satellite cuffs; no local synapse.
  • Autonomic: scattered multipolar neurons + patchy cuffs; synapses.
  • Myenteric: ganglion between intestinal smooth muscle layers.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

GBS / AIDP

  • Ascending weakness, absent reflexes, respiratory risk.
  • FA enrichment: CSF high protein with normal cell count; IVIG / plasma exchange in AIDP.

CMT1A and nerve repair

  • FA enrichment: PMP22 duplication classic CMT1A association.
  • Distal Wallerian degeneration; proximal soma undergoes chromatolysis.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Where is Nissl substance missing? Axon hillock and axon.
  2. Axonal motor for retrograde transport? Dynein.
  3. Histologic fascicle covering? Perineurium.
  4. One myelinated PNS internode is made by? One Schwann cell.
  5. Wallerian degeneration is on which side? Distal to severe axonal injury.
  6. Cells forming Bands of Büngner? Schwann cells.
  7. Large motor somata in cord? Anterior horn.
  8. Complete satellite cuff around pseudounipolar soma? Dorsal-root ganglion.
  9. Intrinsic gut neural plexus between muscle layers? Myenteric (Auerbach).
  10. Chronic pes cavus vs rapid ascending weakness? CMT vs GBS.
