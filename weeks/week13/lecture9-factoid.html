Dermatology & Vesiculobullous Diseases — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Loretta S. Davis, MD · Rapid pre-exam retrieval
Mental model: lesion morphology → depth/content → time + distribution → mechanical versus immune injury → cleavage plane → DIF pattern.

Describe first, diagnose second

  • Primary: basic morphology of an individual lesion.
  • Secondary: time/manipulation changes the original lesion.
  • Ask flat/raised → solid/fluid → size/depth, then configuration, location, symptoms, evolution.
  • Lecture photos are recognition anchors; not full disease-management lessons.

Flat versus solid raised

  • Macule: flat small discoloration; ash-leaf/tuberous sclerosis = hypopigmented.
  • Patch: flat >1 cm; vitiligo = depigmented.
  • Papule: small raised solid lesion; cerebriform intradermal nevus.
  • Plaque: flat-topped elevation >1 cm; psoriasis well-demarcated; seborrheic keratosis stuck-on.
  • Visible small-lesion figures: ≤1 cm; text/First Aid uses <1 cm.

Wheal versus blister

  • Wheal: dermal edema → edematous papule/plaque.
  • Urticaria: each individual lesion <24 h; eruption can recur longer.
  • Vesicle: small fluid-filled blister; bulla: >1 cm.
  • Thin roof → rupture → erosion/crust. Full epidermal roof → tense/resists rupture.
  • Diabetic bulla: tense without erythema in lecture; tense ≠ automatically autoimmune.

Contents and palpation

  • Pustule: leukocytes + fluid; acne mandibular / tinea occipital scalp; ask about lymph nodes.
  • Nodule: firm, solid, deep; calcinosis rock-hard, erythema nodosum tender.
  • Cyst: epithelial-lined sac with fluid/debris.
  • Epidermal inclusion cyst: keratin-filled; not “sebaceous.”
  • Ask single/multiple, mobile/fixed, tender, inflamed, growing/stable.

Scale versus crust

  • Scale: abnormal stratum-corneum accumulation.
  • Psoriasis = white micaceous; seborrheic dermatitis = yellow greasy, cradle cap.
  • Crust/scab: dried serum/blood/exudate.
  • Impetigo = honey-colored; SJS photo = hemorrhagic crust.
  • Herpes simplex: grouped vesicles, erythematous base; clear new/cloudy old; fragile roof.

Surface loss and tissue depth

  • Atrophy: intact surface with epidermal/dermal/subcutis loss; discoid lupus; bound-down linear morphea.
  • Fissure: linear crack into dermis; irritant contact dermatitis.
  • Erosion: focal epidermal loss above junction; PCT example.
  • Ulcer: dermis ± subcutis lost → scar; venous stasis example.

Itch → scratch → change

  • Excoriation: linear/punctate scratch trauma.
  • Lichenification: chronic rubbing → thickened epidermis + prominent lines.
  • Examples: atopic dermatitis, lichen simplex chronicus.
  • DH: severe itch can remove vesicles before examination.

Blood compartment → blanching

  • Erythema: vascular dilation, not one mandatory color; pink rosacea/lilac lichen planus.
  • Purpura: blood outside vessel; petechia small macule, ecchymosis larger patch.
  • Diascopy: glass-slide compression empties vessels.
  • Intravascular → blanches. Extravasated → persists.

Special lesions: exam anchors

  • Closed comedo: whitehead; open: blackhead; acne or sun damage.
  • Telangiectasia: superficial dilated vessel; cheek/rosacea vs periungual/dermatomyositis.
  • Spider angioma: arteriolar, pulsates; PDF lacks playable clip.
  • Scar/cicatrix: permanent fibrosis after dermal injury; keloid exceeds wound.
  • Induration: firm dermal edema; cellulitis example.
  • Named visual examples: blue-purple venous lake; yellow-orange juvenile xanthogranuloma.

Inherited EB: structural defect

  • Mechanobullous: minor trauma → shearing; noninflammatory origin.
  • Simplex: mostly AD; K5/K14; marching/friction.
  • Junctional: AR; laminin 332; neonatal shearing; course emphasizes early death.
  • Dystrophic: AD/AR; collagen VII; scarring; recessive mitten/sock deformities.
  • Care principles: avoid trauma, dress wounds, infection risk.
  • Recessive dystrophic: SCC mortality concern > infection in lecture.

Diagnostic specimen → question

  • Lesional biopsy: routine histology → split/infiltrate.
  • Perilesional normal-looking skin: DIF → deposited patient antibodies.
  • Serum + nonpatient substrate: IIF → circulating antibodies.
  • Serum antigen-specific ELISA: antibodies to named targets.
  • DIF and IIF both use fluorescent anti-immunoglobulin; specimen/source distinguishes them.

Pemphigus vulgaris: Dsg3 ± Dsg1

  • Dsg3: typically oral onset; skin Dsg1 + Dsg3.
  • 5th–6th decades; oral + scalp/face/creases.
  • Acantholysis → intraepidermal split → fragile roof → erosions/crust.
  • Tombstones: basal cells remain attached below split.
  • Nikolsky +: lateral pressure extends blister.
  • DIF: intercellular chicken wire; IIF/ELISA Dsg3/Dsg1.

Pemphigus: treatment + foliaceus

  • Course: “big time” immunosuppression.
  • Prednisone, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil.
  • Rituximab: anti-CD20; B-cell target; course notes FDA approval.
  • Foliaceus: Dsg1 only → superficial “cornflake” peeling; no mucosal involvement.

Bullous pemphigoid: basal attachment

  • IgG → BPAg1/BPAg2 hemidesmosomal targets.
  • Subepidermal split → full epidermal roof → tense, Nikolsky −.
  • Elderly; itch can precede bullae; urticarial plaques; flexor forearms/axilla/groin.
  • Mucosa rare; eosinophils; linear junctional DIF.
  • Tx: topical/systemic steroids; steroid-sparing agents, dupilumab, rituximab.
  • MSK link: BPAg1/BP230; BPAg2/BP180 (collagen XVII).

DH: grouped ≠ herpes infection

  • Chronic, relapsing, severe itch; elbows/knees.
  • HLA-B8; gluten-sensitive enteropathy; TG3 autoantigen.
  • Grouped “herpes-like” vesicles often scratched away.
  • Papillary-tip neutrophils/eosinophils/fibrin → microabscesses → subepidermal blister.
  • DIF: granular IgA at papillary tips.
  • Dapsone: inhibits neutrophil migration/function; gluten-free diet.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. What separates a patch from a plaque?
  2. Why can crust conceal a primary blister?
  3. Which lesion lasts <24 hours individually?
  4. Why does purpura resist blanching?
  5. Match EB subtype to K5/K14, laminin 332, collagen VII.
  6. Which specimen is used for DIF versus IIF?
  7. Which disorder produces acantholysis and tombstones?
  8. What separates BP fluorescence from DH fluorescence?
  9. How does dapsone connect to DH histology?
Reveal rapid-fire answers
  1. Both are large; patch is flat, plaque is elevated.
  2. The roof ruptures and exudate dries into crust.
  3. An urticarial wheal.
  4. Blood is outside vessels and cannot be emptied by compression.
  5. Simplex, junctional, dystrophic, respectively.
  6. DIF: normal-looking perilesional skin; IIF: serum on nonpatient substrate.
  7. Pemphigus vulgaris: basal matrix attachments persist below cell-to-cell failure.
  8. BP: linear junctional IgG; DH: granular IgA at papillary tips.
  9. It inhibits neutrophil migration/function, targeting the papillary inflammatory process.

COURSE-SOURCE NOTES

Course wording and scope distinctions
  • EB overview: slides say “subepidermal”; BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: classic K5/K14 simplex cleaves within basal keratinocytes. Primary study.
  • Junctional prognosis: course says typical early death; BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT: severe generalized disease differs from intermediate disease, which may have normal lifespan. NIH source.
  • Size boundary: figures use ≤1 cm, extracted text/First Aid uses <1 cm. Learn the small-versus-large distinction.
  • Acquired EB is explicitly not discussed. The static spider-angioma video placeholder cannot supply a playable clip. Course drug lists are preserved without an invented dosing algorithm.
