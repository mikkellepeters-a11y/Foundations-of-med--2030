01Actin: the core rulesLO 1
Actin is the smallest of the three major cytoskeletal filament systems and is especially abundant at the cell cortex. Unlike microtubules, actin can form both long unbranched filaments and highly branched networks. The polymer is F-actin; its monomer is G-actin.

Polarity

F-actin has a fast-growing barbed (+) end and a slower pointed (-) end. Growth is favored at the plus end; disassembly is favored at the minus end.

Nucleotide

Actin subunits bind ATP, in contrast with tubulin's GTP. ATP-actin is assembly-ready; hydrolysis to ADP makes older filament regions less stable.

Do not mix these up
Microtubule: alpha/beta-tubulin + GTP. Microfilament: G-actin/F-actin + ATP.
Current-deck diagram: ATP-bound G-actin is incorporated into a polarized filament while formin remains associated with the growing barbed (+) end.
02Build the right actin architectureLO 1–2
Master discriminator
Formin = linear. Arp2/3 = branched. This distinction should be automatic.

Current-deck branched-actin figure: Arp2/3 nucleates daughter filaments from pre-existing actin to build a dendritic network that pushes the membrane forward.
Microvillus architecture: a stable bundle of parallel actin filaments increases apical surface area for absorption.
Microvilli vs filopodia: both contain bundled actin, but microvilli are relatively stable absorptive structures, whereas filopodia are dynamic exploratory protrusions used during migration. Filopodia cooperate with lamellipodia and focal adhesions to choose and execute a direction of movement.
Filopodia and lamellipodia are different actin architectures used by the same migrating cell: bundled protrusions for sensing, branched lamellipodia for membrane advance.
Actin-binding protein categories
Keep the functions straight: profilin prepares/delivers monomers; capping proteins stop end dynamics; severing proteins cut filaments; bundling/cross-linking proteins organize higher-order structures; myosins are ATP-powered actin motors.

Cytochalasin D in the current deck: blocking the plus end shifts the filament toward net disassembly.
Why this matters
Cell migration, phagocytosis, endocytosis, and cytokinesis all require regulated assembly and disassembly. A cytoskeleton that cannot remodel is just as functionally problematic as one that cannot assemble.
04Actin generates protrusion, engulfment and constrictionLO 3

Cell migration

Leading-edge actin polymerization forms lamellipodia/filopodia. Focal adhesions couple the actin network to the extracellular environment while rear adhesions must release.

Phagocytosis

Local actin assembly drives pseudopod extension around a target, followed by phagosome closure.

Cytokinesis

An actin-myosin contractile ring forms at the mid-zone. Myosin constricts the cortical actin belt like a purse string.

Muscle contraction

Myosin uses ATP to slide along actin thin filaments. The lecture treats this as the core actin-motor principle; detailed sarcomere mechanics come later.

Current-deck phagocytosis sequence: actin-driven probing and pseudopod extension culminate in phagosome closure.
Current-deck cytokinesis diagrams: actin + myosin form the contractile ring that physically separates daughter cells.
05Pathogens and disease exploit actin machineryLO 3
Listeria monocytogenes is the cleanest mechanism to know: the organism recruits host Arp2/3 machinery to build an actin comet tail for intracellular propulsion and cell-to-cell spread. The current lecture also lists Shigella, Toxoplasma, and vaccinia virus as cytoskeletal hijackers.
Clinical / disease linkActin connection
Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein (WASP)Works with Arp2/3 in branched-actin formation; the lecture emphasizes immune-cell motility and phagocytic capacity in this X-linked disorder.
Profilin mutationsSome autosomal-dominant variants are associated with ALS; the exact mechanism of motor-neuron injury is presented as unresolved.
Metastatic behaviorNormally non-motile cells can acquire actin-dependent motility during metastatic transformation.

06The plasma membrane needs a mechanical skeletonLO 4
Red blood cells (RBCs) repeatedly deform in small vessels, so a submembranous lattice provides resilience. In the lecture framework, spectrin tetramers + short actin filaments form the mesh, ankyrin helps support/anchor it, and the complex connects to the membrane through the Band 3 complex.
Current-deck RBC membrane-skeleton diagrams: spectrin and short actin build a flexible submembranous lattice linked to integral membrane proteins.

Muscle uses a different solution: dystrophin links intracellular actin to a transmembrane glycoprotein complex that connects to the basal lamina. Loss of this mechanical linkage makes repeated contraction injurious to muscle cells.
Current-deck dystrophin complex: dystrophin links the intracellular actin cytoskeleton to a membrane-spanning complex and the basal lamina.
Clinical anchor
Duchenne muscular dystrophy: the current slide specifically identifies mutation in dystrophin as the example of disease from this membrane-cytoskeleton linkage.
07Intermediate filaments: lecture contextCurrent deck
This material is present in the lecture but is not one of the four formal objectives. Keep it as a secondary layer after the actin and plasma-membrane framework is solid.

Core properties

Rope-like coiled-coil proteins, no polarity after assembly, and much more stable than actin/microtubules. Their major job is structural support under stress.

Tissue examples

Keratins in epithelia, vimentin in connective-tissue cells, neurofilaments in neurons, and lamins beneath the nuclear envelope.

Current-deck intermediate-filament diagrams: coiled-coil assembly produces a stable, nonpolar rope-like filament.
Disease exampleConnection

Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndromeLamin A/C mutation → abnormal nuclear-envelope support and accelerated-aging phenotype.

08Rapid recall mapAll LOs

Build

G-actin → F-actin. ATP-bound subunits. Barbed (+) grows faster; pointed (-) loses subunits.

Choose architecture

Formin = linear. Arp2/3 + WASP = branched. Bundling proteins make microvilli/filopodia.

Use dynamics

Phalloidin stabilizes F-actin. Cytochalasin D caps + end and drives net breakdown.

Move

Lamellipodia + filopodia support migration; actin drives phagocytic pseudopods; actin/myosin constrict cytokinesis.

Protect RBCs

Spectrin + short actin + ankyrin/Band 3 create deformable membrane support.

Protect muscle

Dystrophin connects actin to the membrane/ECM support system. Defect → muscular dystrophy.
