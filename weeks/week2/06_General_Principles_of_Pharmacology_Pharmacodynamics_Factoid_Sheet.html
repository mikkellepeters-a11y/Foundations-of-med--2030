Pharmacodynamics

Week 2 · Core 06 · Factoid Sheet · current lecture scope
Graph rule: horizontal shift = potency; maximal height = efficacy. Binding rule: low KD = high affinity.

Therapeutic agents

  • Broad classes: small molecules + biologics.
  • Biologics include mAbs, growth factors, hormones, allergens, blood factors.
  • Useful stems: -mab, -tinib, -tide, -imod.

Small-molecule targets

  • GPCRs and other receptors
  • Enzymes / kinases
  • Ligand- and voltage-gated ion channels
  • Nuclear receptors / transcription factors
  • Transport / structural proteins
  • RNA

Affinity and occupancy

KD = [drug] at 50% receptor occupancy
  • Low KD = high affinity.
  • High KD = low affinity.
  • KD describes binding; do not automatically call it efficacy.

Two-state receptor model

LigandR ⇌ R* behaviorEffect
Full agonistStrongly favors R*Maximal activation
Partial agonistFavors R*, but less stronglySubmaximal Emax
Competitive/neutral antagonistDoes not favor R or R*Blocks agonist; surmountable
Inverse agonistFavors inactive RReduces constitutive activity

Potency

  • Less drug needed = more potent.
  • More potent curve sits farther left.
  • Lecture example: fentanyl > morphine potency.

Efficacy

  • Maximal physiological effect.
  • Full agonist > partial agonist efficacy in same system.
  • Plateau height matters, not left/right location.

Competitive antagonism

  • Binds agonist/orthosteric site without activating receptor.
  • Shifts agonist curve to the right.
  • Enough agonist can overcome the block; maximal response can still be reached.

Allosterism, variability & safety

Orthosteric vs allosteric

  • Orthosteric site = natural ligand site.
  • Allosteric ligand binds elsewhere.
  • Allosteric sites are less conserved across receptor subtypes in the lecture.

PAM vs NAM

  • PAM = positive allosteric modulator.
  • NAM = negative allosteric modulator.
  • Allosteric does NOT automatically mean inhibitory.

Pharmacodynamic variability

SourceReasoning consequence
Desensitization / downregulationRepeated exposure can reduce receptor responsiveness.
Genetic polymorphismsVariants in drug targets/signaling pathways can alter response despite similar exposure.
Downstream constitutive signalingBlocking an upstream receptor may fail if a downstream node is already active.
Therapeutic Index = TD50 / ED50
  • TD50 = toxic dose in 50% of population.
  • ED50 = effective dose in 50% of population.
  • Larger TI = wider margin between desired and toxic effects.
  • Smaller TI = narrower margin; requires more careful dosing/monitoring.

Eight instant discriminators

  1. Low KD → high affinity.
  2. Left-shifted response curve → greater potency.
  3. Higher plateau → greater efficacy.
  4. Partial agonist → lower Emax than full agonist.
  5. Competitive antagonist → right shift with recoverable Emax.
  6. Inverse agonist → lowers constitutive/basal activity.
  7. Orthosteric = natural ligand site; allosteric = separate regulatory site.
  8. TI = TD50/ED50.
