Lyonization
  • One X randomly inactivated early in female development.
  • Inactive X = Barr body.
  • Female tissues become mosaics.
  • Skewing can create a manifesting carrier.
Consanguinity
  • Raises chance both partners carry the same rare recessive allele.
  • Siblings share ~1/2 genes; first cousins 1/8; first cousins once removed 1/16; second cousins 1/32.
Penetrance

Genotype -> phenotype present or absent. Incomplete penetrance can make an AD pedigree appear to skip a generation.

Disease risk = allele risk x penetrance
Variable expressivity vs pleiotropy

Expressivity: same genotype, different severity. Pleiotropy: one gene, multiple distinct traits.

Hardy-Weinberg

p + q = 1
p² + 2pq + q² = 1
AR shortcut
prevalence q² -> sqrt -> q -> p=1-q -> carriers 2pq
XLR shortcut

Male incidence = q because males are hemizygous. Affected females = q²; carrier females = 2pq.

Probability

AND events multiply. Mutually exclusive OR routes add.

Inheritance risk - last-pass sheet

High-yield carrier logic

Unaffected sibling of AR affected child

Parents must be Aa x Aa. Among unaffected outcomes AA, Aa, Aa -> 2/3 carrier risk.

AR affected child risk
P(parent 1 carrier) x P(parent 2 carrier) x 1/4
XLR affected child, carrier mother, father unaffected

Sex unspecified: 1/2 boy x 1/2 mutant X = 1/4. If the child is specified as a boy: 1/2.

Each pregnancy is independent

Previous children do not change the Mendelian probability for the next pregnancy unless they change what you know about genotype.

Source calculations worth knowing

CF carrier + population risk example

Unaffected sister of CF patient: 2/3 carrier risk. Partner q=1/50 -> 2pq=0.0392. Child risk = 2/3 x 0.0392 x 1/4 ≈ 1/153.

80% penetrant AD example

Aa x aa gives 1/2 inheritance x 0.8 penetrance = 0.4 = 40% disease risk.

X-linked consanguineous example

Trace the mutant X through obligate-carrier women and multiply the sequential 1/2 events; handout result = 1/8.
