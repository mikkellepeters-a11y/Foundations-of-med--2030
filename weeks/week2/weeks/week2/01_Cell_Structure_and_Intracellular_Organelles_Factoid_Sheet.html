Cell Structure & Intracellular Organelles - Factoid Sheet

Week 2 Cellular & Molecular Foundations • Core 01 • memorize-first companion
Core frame: know what each organelle does, how membrane asymmetry is maintained, how passive vs active transport differ, and what the Na+/K+-ATPase gradients power.

Cell + nucleus - memorize cold

  • Cytosol: ~pH 7.2; extracellular fluid ~pH 7.4.
  • Only nucleus + nucleolus are visible by light microscopy in this handout; most other organelles require EM.
  • Malignant cells: often increased nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio.
  • Nucleosome: DNA around 8 histones - 2 each H2A, H2B, H3, H4.
  • Euchromatin: less condensed, accessible, transcriptionally active.
  • Heterochromatin: condensed, inaccessible, transcriptionally silent.
  • Nucleolus: rRNA transcription, pre-rRNA processing, ribosomal-subunit assembly.

Chromatin direction

Histone acetylation + DNA demethylation favor euchromatin.

DNA methylation + histone deacetylation favor heterochromatin.

Cells with high protein-synthesis/proliferative activity may have more prominent nucleoli.

Mitochondrial inheritance

  • mtDNA: ~16.5 kb, 37 genes.
  • Encodes 13 proteins, 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs.
  • Maternal transmission of mtDNA disease.
  • Affected males do not transmit mtDNA disease.
  • Heteroplasmy explains variable severity among tissues/children.

Mature RBC exception

Circulating fully differentiated RBCs have proteasomes but no other organelles in this handout.

Clinical tie-ins shown: hereditary spherocytosis (spectrin/peripheral cytoskeleton), proteasome inhibition with bortezomib.

Membrane transport + Na+/K+-ATPase

Transport types

ProcessProtein?Energy?Direction / signature

Facilitated diffusionYesNoDown gradient; specific; protein-mediated; carrier transport can saturate and be competitively inhibited.
Primary activeYesDirect ATPCan move solute against gradient; pumps.
Secondary activeYesIon-gradient energyUses gradient made by primary transport to move another solute uphill.

Channel vs carrier

Channel: pore, very fast, down electrochemical gradient; may be voltage-, ligand-, or mechanically gated. CFTR is presented as an ATP-gated anion channel.

Carrier: binds solute + changes conformation; finite number -> saturation.

Secondary-active vocabulary

Symporter: transported particles move in the same direction.

Antiporter: transported particles move in opposite directions.

Na+-glucose cotransport: Na+ moves down its gradient while glucose is brought uphill.

Na+/K+-ATPase - memorize exactly

1 ATP -> 3 Na+ OUT + 2 K+ IN. The pump is electrogenic because more positive charge leaves than enters.
Gradient producedMeaning
Outside -> inside Na+ gradientNa+ strongly favors entry and can power secondary active transport.
Inside -> outside K+ gradientK+ is concentrated intracellularly.
Inside-negative membrane potentialElectrical gradient contributes to cellular excitability.

What the gradients are used for

  • Secondary active transport of nutrients/solutes.
  • Neuronal signaling and depolarization.
  • Skeletal + cardiac muscle function.
  • Enzyme regulation through control of cytosolic Ca2+.
  • Na+/Ca2+ exchanger helps keep intracellular Ca2+ low.
  • Hormone secretion, including insulin release from beta cells.

If you see X -> think Y

  • More prominent nucleoli -> high ribosome biogenesis / protein synthesis.
  • Maternal inheritance + variable severity -> mtDNA disease + heteroplasmy.
  • VLCFA problem -> peroxisome.
  • Polyubiquitin tag -> proteasome.
  • PS externalization -> apoptotic clearance / procoagulant platelet surface.
  • Transport saturates -> protein-mediated carrier process.
  • Direct ATP use -> primary active transport; gradient energy -> secondary active transport.
