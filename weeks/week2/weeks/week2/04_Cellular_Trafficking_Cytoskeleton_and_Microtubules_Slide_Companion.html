01Microtubules are polarized hollow tracksLO 1
The cytoskeleton has three major filament systems: microtubules, microfilaments (actin), and intermediate filaments. Microtubules contribute to cell shape, rigidity, long-distance cargo transport, and cell division. Their role as intracellular tracks is the central organizing idea for the lecture.
Current-deck structure diagram: alpha/beta-tubulin heterodimers stack head-to-tail into polarized protofilaments; usually 13 protofilaments form the hollow microtubule.
MTOC / centrosome diagram: gamma-tubulin ring complexes nucleate microtubules and help overcome the thermodynamic barrier to assembly.

Basic unit

One alpha/beta-tubulin heterodimer. Head-to-tail stacking creates polarity.

Ends

Plus end = terminal beta-tubulin. Minus end = terminal alpha-tubulin.

Nucleation

Microtubule-organizing centers (MTOCs) contain gamma-tubulin ring complexes that act as a nucleating template.

Centrosome

In many cells, MTOCs cluster near the nucleus in the centrosome. Specialized cells may also nucleate non-centrosomal microtubules.

Direction anchor
Polarity is not decorative. It is what lets motors know which way is “out” and which way is “back.”
02Dynamic instability: GTP cap, catastrophe, rescueLO 1, 4
Current-deck dynamic-instability diagram: a GTP-tubulin cap stabilizes a growing end; loss of the cap allows rapid depolymerization.
Growth: GTP-bound tubulin dimers add to the growing microtubule. As long as addition stays ahead of GTP hydrolysis, a stabilizing GTP cap remains at the distal end.
Catastrophe: when GTP hydrolysis catches the growing end, GDP-bound tubulin becomes exposed and the microtubule rapidly shrinks. Rescue is the switch back from shrinkage to growth.
Lecture-covered framework
Microtubules must remain dynamic for normal cell migration, growth-cone behavior, mitosis, and effective intracellular organization. Stability and instability are both regulated states, not “good” versus “bad.”
Context that helps the framework
Microtubule end-binding proteins, post-translational modifications of tubulin, and microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) can shift microtubules toward more stable or more dynamic states and can alter motor traffic. The lecture later connects this idea to Tau.
03Kinesin and dynein turn polarity into directed trafficLO 2, 4
Most kinesins move cargo toward the microtubule plus end; cytoplasmic dynein handles most minus-end traffic.
Kinesin’s two motor domains alternate ATP binding/hydrolysis. Conformational change in the linker region is coupled to forward displacement.

Do not mix the motor systems
Kinesin and dynein use microtubules. Myosins use actin. The lecture explicitly separates cytoplasmic dynein (cargo transport) from axonemal dynein (ciliary motion).
Neuron logic: axons are long and thin, so diffusion is too slow. Small defects in microtubule dynamics or motor regulation can therefore create a major intracellular “traffic jam.”
04Motile cilia versus the primary ciliumLO 3
Motile cilia: the characteristic 9+2 axoneme contains outer doublets, central microtubules, axonemal dynein, and linker proteins.
Primary/non-motile cilium: typically 9+0, lacking the central pair. It functions primarily as a sensory/signaling organelle.

Why dynein produces bending: if adjacent doublets were free, dynein would slide them apart. Linker proteins constrain the doublets, converting sliding force into bending and a coordinated power stroke.
05Ciliopathies: use structure to predict the phenotypeLO 3, 4
Primary ciliary dyskinesia: defective motile cilia impair mucus clearance and can impair sperm and reproductive-tract motility.

Primary ciliary dyskinesia

Defective motile cilia → mucus buildup, recurrent sinus/bronchial/ear infections, and fertility problems.

Kartagener syndrome

A PCD subtype emphasized as situs inversus + sinusitis + bronchiectasis.

Polycystic kidney disease

Many implicated proteins localize to the primary cilium. The lecture uses renal fluid-flow sensing and calcium signaling as the mechanistic frame.

Bardet-Biedl syndrome

A primary-cilium disorder associated in the lecture with kidney cysts/dysfunction, vision loss, polydactyly, weight gain, and developmental/signaling abnormalities.

Shortcut: respiratory clearing + fertility + laterality problem points you toward motile cilia. Kidney cysts + sensory/signaling defects point you toward the primary cilium.
06Neurologic disease: neurons expose small transport defectsLO 4
The lecture’s neuron analogy: long axons and polarized microtubules make transport defects especially consequential.
Tau is a microtubule-associated protein. In Alzheimer disease, hyperphosphorylated Tau loses microtubule affinity and forms intracellular neurofibrillary tangles.
Disease / defectMicrotubule connection emphasized in current lecture
LissencephalyDefective neuronal migration; some forms involve loss of one copy of Lis1, a dynein regulator. The mechanistic theme is failed coordination of the centrosome/dynein system with the migrating nucleus.
Perry syndromeAutosomal-dominant mutations in Dynactin 1 (Dctn1/p150Glued); tremor/parkinsonism, weight loss, depression; later onset.
Spinal muscular atrophy / ALS examplesThe lecture uses BicD and Kinesin examples to make the point that hyperactive motors can also cause disease. Regulation matters in both directions.
Alzheimer diseaseTau is a MAP that normally stabilizes microtubules. Hyperphosphorylated Tau detaches and aggregates into intracellular NFTs.
Lecture message
“Unregulated activity is bad.” A motor defect does not have to mean “too little movement.” Loss of normal autoinhibition or adaptor control can create pathologic hyperactivity.
07Mitosis and cancer therapy: target dynamics, not just the existence of microtubulesLO 1, 4
Current handout image: the microtubule network reorganizes into the mitotic spindle and later coordinates with actin during cytokinesis.
The mitotic spindle is an extremely dynamic microtubule structure. Every replicated chromosome must attach appropriately before the spindle-assembly checkpoint permits progression. Prolonged mitotic arrest can lead to apoptosis.
Agent / targetCurrent-lecture mechanismWhy it works
VinblastineAt chemotherapy-range low doses, alters microtubule dynamics; high concentrations can destabilize microtubules more broadly.Replicating cells are especially sensitive to disturbed spindle dynamics.
Paclitaxel (Taxol)Stabilizes microtubules, opposite the direction of vinblastine’s destabilizing effect.Over-stability is also incompatible with the dynamic mitotic spindle.
Eg5 inhibitionThe lecture introduces the mitosis-specific kinesin Eg5 and monastrol as a research/therapy concept.Targets a mitotic motor rather than every microtubule-dependent process.
Why peripheral neuropathy + myelosuppression? Neurons depend heavily on microtubule transport, while blood-cell production depends on ongoing cell division. The same biology that makes the drugs useful explains major toxicity patterns.

One-pass recall map

Build the track

Alpha/beta-tubulin → head-to-tail protofilament → ~13 protofilaments → polarized hollow microtubule.

Start the track

MTOC / gamma-tubulin ring complex nucleates microtubules; centrosome is a major MTOC location.

Keep it dynamic

GTP cap = growth stability. Cap loss = catastrophe. Return to growth = rescue.

Move cargo

Kinesin usually plus-end; cytoplasmic dynein minus-end; both consume ATP and require regulation.

Separate cilia

Motile 9+2 + axonemal dynein versus primary 9+0 sensory cilium.

Map diseases

PCD/Kartagener = motile cilia; PKD/BBS = primary cilia; Tau/Lis1/Dynactin/motors = neurologic transport disease.

Use dynamics clinically

Vinblastine disturbs dynamics; paclitaxel stabilizes. Either way, the mitotic spindle cannot function normally.

Remember the theme

Too little motor activity can be bad, but too much unregulated activity can also be bad.
