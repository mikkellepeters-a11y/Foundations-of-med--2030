General Principles of Pharmacology - Pharmacokinetics

Week 2 · Core 08 · Factoid Sheet · official-objective focused
Bioavailability (F)
  • Fraction of administered drug reaching systemic circulation.
  • Lecture uses oral AUC vs IV AUC to conceptualize F.
  • Oral dosing may be reduced by incomplete absorption + first-pass effect.
  • P-glycoprotein can limit gut absorption and CNS exposure.
Low F → larger oral dose/rate for the same systemic target.
Volume of distribution (Vd)
Vd = amount / plasma concentration
  • Apparent, not anatomic, volume.
  • High Vd → extensive tissue distribution / low plasma concentration.
  • Low Vd → vascular retention, large/protein-bound drugs.
  • Large Vd → larger loading dose.
Clearance (Cl)
  • Volume of plasma cleared of drug per unit time.
  • Total Cl = renal + hepatic + other clearance.
  • Renal insufficiency ↓ Cl for renally eliminated drugs.
  • High Cl → higher maintenance dosing rate.
Half-life
t1/2 = 0.693 × Vd / Cl
  • Vd ↑ → t1/2 ↑
  • Cl ↑ → t1/2 ↓
  • Approach to steady state is determined by half-life.

Metabolism
  • Phase I: CYP oxidation; products may remain active; CYP activity readily inhibited/induced.
  • Phase II: conjugation (glucuronidation, acetylation, etc.); metabolites usually inactive.

Dosing math

Loading dose
LD = Vd × target

Question asks how much drug is needed to fill the apparent distribution space.

Maintenance rate
Rate = Cl × target

Question asks how quickly cleared drug must be replaced.

Incomplete bioavailability
Oral LD = Vd × target / F
Oral rate = Cl × target / F

If F=0.5, double the calculated systemic requirement.

Steady state

Reached when elimination rate = dosing rate. The approach is controlled by half-life.

Pharmacokinetics - interactions & reasoning

Major interaction mechanisms
  1. CYP inhibition / induction
  2. P-gp inhibition / induction
  3. Displacement from plasma protein binding
Pharmacokinetic variability
  • CYP genetic variability → F / Cl
  • Renal/hepatic/cardiac insufficiency → Cl
  • Age / muscle mass → Vd
  • Edema → Vd
  • Bowel disease → F
CYP reasoning
Inhibitor → metabolism slows → substrate exposure tends to rise.
Inducer → metabolism accelerates → substrate exposure tends to fall.
P-gp reasoning
P-gp = efflux pump.

More efflux can reduce net gut/CNS exposure; inhibition can increase exposure to a P-gp substrate.

Eight last-pass lines

1. F = systemic fraction delivered.
2. Vd tells you how plasma concentration relates to total drug amount.
3. Cl tells you how efficiently drug is removed.
4. t1/2 rises with Vd and falls with Cl.
5. Loading dose follows Vd.
6. Maintenance rate follows Cl.
7. Divide oral dosing requirements by F when F<1.
8. CYP/P-gp genetics and interactions change exposure by changing metabolism or transport.
