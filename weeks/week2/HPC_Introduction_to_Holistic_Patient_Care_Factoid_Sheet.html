Introduction to Holistic Patient Care

Week 2 HPC · compact factoid sheet
Core definition: Holistic patient care addresses the patient's physical, mental/emotional, social/environmental, cultural, and spiritual needs while considering family background, health beliefs, identities, values, and unique needs.

Four domains

Physical / biological
Disease, symptoms, function, pain, medications.
Psychological / behavioral
Mood, coping, behavior, trauma, adherence, motivation.
Social / environmental
Housing, finances, transport, work, food, insurance, support.
Cultural / spiritual
Beliefs, identity, values, culture, language, spirituality.
HPC ≠ CAM. Complementary and alternative medicine describes nonstandard medical products/practices. HPC is the whole-person model of care.

What physicians must do

Know the whole person in context · ask nonjudgmentally about goals/preferences · communicate across culture/language/education/development · lead interdisciplinary care · know community resources · include patients/families in decisions even when they disagree with you.

Patient benefits

More informed treatment decisions
Care team that knows/listens/is accountable
Tools/services aligned with preferences and values
Possible improvement in mental/physical outcomes and recovery markers

Provider / system benefits

Improved patient/family satisfaction
Enhanced provider reputation
Better clinician/staff morale + productivity
Improved resource allocation
Reduced expenses
Improved financial margins across care continuum

Clinical application

Outpatient: trusted relationships, empathy, two-way communication, and referral to peer support, social work, financial counseling, behavioral health, transportation, daily-living help, or language/literacy resources when needed.

Hospital: respect patient-defined family, invite desired family participation in rounding/decisions, communicate updates, and support family needs in the care environment.
Exam trap: “holistic” does not simply mean “non-drug.” Pick the option that best integrates the patient's goals, function, context, shared decision-making, and interdisciplinary/community resources.
Fast recall: Whole person + context + communication + shared decisions + interdisciplinary/community resources.
